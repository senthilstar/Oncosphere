# What is immunotherapy?

Your immune system normally finds and removes abnormal cells. Some cancers hide from it by using "brakes" on immune cells called **checkpoints** (such as PD-1 and PD-L1). **Checkpoint inhibitor** drugs — for example pembrolizumab, nivolumab, atezolizumab and dostarlimab — release these brakes so immune cells can attack the cancer.

## Who may benefit?
It depends on the cancer type and sometimes on tests on the tumour, such as **PD-L1**, **MSI-H/dMMR** or **tumour mutational burden (TMB)**. Your oncologist decides whether it suits you.

## Side effects
Because the immune system becomes more active, it can sometimes inflame healthy organs — skin, gut, liver, lungs, thyroid and others. Tell your team quickly about **new diarrhoea, cough or breathlessness, rash, yellow eyes, severe tiredness or headache**, even weeks after a dose.

## Questions to ask your doctor
- Is immunotherapy an option for my cancer? Do I need a tumour test first?
- How often is it given, and for how long?
- Whom do I call if I get side effects?
