1. Epidemiology and molecular landscape (including India and Asia)

  • Adenocarcinoma is the most common NSCLC histology worldwide and is now the dominant subtype in Indian series, including a growing share in never-smokers, women and younger patients.
  • India. GLOBOCAN 2022 recorded about 81,700 new lung cancers and 75,000 deaths in India. Roughly 70% present at locally advanced or metastatic stage (PGIMER/DHR report, 2025).
  • EGFR in India. 26% of 780 adenocarcinomas carried an EGFR mutation at Tata Memorial (PLoS One 2013; 23.2% of all 907 NSCLC). Other Indian cohorts report 28–33%. Western rates are about 10–15%, and East Asian rates 40–50%.
  • ALK in India. 2.5% in 3,351 tested adenocarcinomas and 3% in a 500-patient multicentre series. Some reviews quote up to about 10%, depending on enrichment and assay.
  • ROS1. About 1–2% in the West; 2.8–4.1% reported in Indian series.
  • KRAS G12C is about 13–14% of NSCLC in the West and is less frequent in Asian/Indian never-smokers verify Indian rate.
  • A misdiagnosed tuberculosis phase (empirical ATT before a tissue diagnosis) is a recognised cause of diagnostic delay in India.
In a never-smoking Indian woman with adenocarcinoma, the pre-test probability of an actionable driver (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, HER2, RET, MET) exceeds 50–60%. Never start immunotherapy before driver results are back. IO followed by osimertinib raises the risk of serious pneumonitis.

Oligometastatic disease: definitions that matter

  • EORTC Lung Cancer Group consensus (Dingemans, JTO 2019). Up to 5 metastases in up to 3 organs, all treatable radically, with mediastinal nodes not counted as metastases. Staging requires FDG-PET-CT and brain MRI.
  • ESTRO/EORTC classification (Guckenberger, Lancet Oncol 2020). Separates de-novo (synchronous or metachronous) from repeat and induced oligometastatic disease (oligo-persistence, oligo-progression). This matters for trial eligibility and for the "local consolidative therapy" (LCT) decision.
  • The 9th edition splits M1c because survival worsens with each additional organ system involved. M1b and M1c1 prognosis overlaps in some datasets.

3. Diagnostic work-up and biomarker testing

1 · Suspect stage IVCECT chest/abdomen, then FDG-PET-CT. Contrast MRI brain for all stage IV (including asymptomatic patients, given the CNS-penetrant TKI options).
2 · Tissue first, plasma in parallelCore biopsy (≥2–3 cores) or EBUS-TBNA with a cell block. Send plasma ctDNA at the same time if tissue is scant or will be delayed.
3 · Broad NGSDNA NGS (EGFR, KRAS, BRAF, MET ex14, HER2, NTRK, RET, ROS1, ALK) plus RNA NGS for fusions and MET ex14 skipping.
4 · IHCPD-L1 TPS (22C3/SP263 interchangeable for TPS; SP142 is not). ALK D5F3 IHC is acceptable as a stand-alone test. Add c-MET IHC (Teliso-V, SAFFRON) and HER2 IHC (T-DXd tumour-agnostic) as needed.
5 · Hold IO until drivers are knownIf the patient is unstable, start chemotherapy alone, then add the targeted agent or IO once results are back.

Tissue vs liquid biopsy ("plasma-first")

  • Plasma NGS is highly specific. A positive result is actionable and can save 1–3 weeks. A negative result needs tissue NGS, because sensitivity is about 70–80% and lower for fusions, CNS-only or low-volume disease.
  • Concurrent tissue + plasma testing raises the yield of actionable alterations (ASCO 2026 endorses combining both).
  • Baseline EGFR ctDNA positivity (~80%) is prognostic. In FLAURA2 the OS benefit of adding chemotherapy held regardless of ctDNA clearance. PACE-LUNG (ESMO 2026) tested ctDNA-guided escalation and remains investigational.

Reflex-testing nuances

  • Sequential single-gene PCR (EGFR, then ALK IHC, then ROS1 FISH…) wastes tissue. ASCO says it "should not be routine" outside truly resource-limited settings.
  • Test all non-squamous NSCLC regardless of smoking status, and squamous disease in never/light smokers or small biopsies.
  • Use RNA NGS (or targeted RNA panels) when DNA NGS is negative in a never-smoker. Fusions (ALK, ROS1, RET, NTRK, NRG1) and MET ex14 skipping are under-called on DNA-only panels.
  • PD-L1 ≥50% plus never-smoker plus "driver-negative" on a small panel should prompt a re-test.
  • At progression: re-biopsy (tissue ± plasma) to look for MET amplification or overexpression, transformation to SCLC/squamous, C797S, ALK/ROS1 resistance mutations, and HER2/BRAF/RET fusions.
  • EGFR RT-PCR (or ddPCR) on tissue is widely available and cheap. Many Indian centres still run EGFR PCR, then ALK IHC (D5F3), then ROS1 IHC with FISH confirmation. If you must, run them in parallel on cut sections, not sequentially.
  • Cell blocks from pleural fluid are an under-used source of DNA/RNA. Ask the pathologist to prepare a cell block from every malignant effusion.
  • For a patient who cannot afford NGS, a pragmatic minimum is EGFR, ALK IHC, ROS1 IHC and PD-L1. Then add NGS (or plasma NGS) if all are negative in a never-smoker.
  • Pharma-sponsored testing programmes exist for several targets programme names and eligibility vary; verify locally.

PD-L1 assays

22C3 (pembrolizumab), SP263 (approved for several agents) and 28-8 are analytically close for TPS. SP142 under-calls tumour cells. Report TPS bands as <1%, 1–49% and ≥50%. PD-L1 is heterogeneous: a small biopsy can misclassify, so do not deny IO on a borderline small-sample result.

Imaging

PET-CT plus MRI brain at baseline. MRI brain every 2–3 months in the first year for driver-positive patients with baseline CNS disease interval varies by guideline. Response by RECIST 1.1 and RANO-BM.

4. Historical evolution: from "one size fits all" to 13+ actionable targets

The chemotherapy era

  • ECOG 1594 (Schiller, NEJM 2002; n=1,207): four platinum doublets gave equivalent results, with median OS ≈ 7.9 months. This created the "platinum plateau".
  • E4599 (Sandler, NEJM 2006; n=878): adding bevacizumab to carboplatin-paclitaxel gave OS 12.3 vs 10.3 mo (HR 0.79) and ORR 35% vs 15%. It was the first agent to break the 1-year barrier, and patients with squamous histology or haemoptysis were excluded.
  • JMDB (Scagliotti, JCO 2008; n=1,725): cisplatin-pemetrexed was non-inferior overall but superior in adenocarcinoma (OS 12.6 vs 10.9 mo). This established histology-directed therapy.
  • PARAMOUNT (Paz-Ares, Lancet Oncol 2012 / JCO 2013; n=539): continuation pemetrexed maintenance gave PFS HR 0.62 and OS 13.9 vs 11.0 mo (HR 0.78).
  • Second line: docetaxel (TAX 317) was the standard. REVEL (+ramucirumab; OS 10.5 vs 9.1 mo, HR 0.86) and LUME-Lung 1 (+nintedanib, adenocarcinoma only; OS 12.6 vs 10.3 mo, HR 0.83) added modest gains.

The targeted era

  • IPASS (Mok, NEJM 2009): gefitinib vs carboplatin-paclitaxel in selected East Asians. PFS HR 0.48 if EGFR-mutant but 2.85 if wild-type. This showed that the biomarker, not the clinical phenotype, selects patients.
  • EGFR TKI generations: 1st (gefitinib, erlotinib), 2nd (afatinib, dacomitinib), 3rd (osimertinib, lazertinib, aumolertinib, firmonertinib [furmonertinib]).
  • ALK: crizotinib (PROFILE 1014) → alectinib (ALEX) → lorlatinib (CROWN), with median PFS rising from ~11 months to >60 months.

The immunotherapy era

  • 2015: nivolumab beats docetaxel (CheckMate 057; OS 12.2 vs 9.4 mo).
  • 2016: KEYNOTE-024 makes pembrolizumab monotherapy first-line for PD-L1 ≥50%.
  • 2018: KEYNOTE-189 establishes chemo-IO for all PD-L1 levels.
  • 2020–2023: dual checkpoint inhibition (CheckMate 227/9LA, POSEIDON) and cemiplimab (EMPOWER-Lung 1/3).

The "post-PD-1" era (2024–2026)

  • PD-1×VEGF bispecifics: ivonescimab beats pembrolizumab on PFS (HARMONi-2) and on OS at interim (HR 0.73, China). The FDA decision on the EGFR post-TKI BLA is due on 14 Nov 2026.
  • ADCs: TROP2 (Dato-DXd, sac-TMT), HER2 (T-DXd), c-MET (Teliso-V), HER3 and EGFR×HER3 (iza-bren, ruzaltatug rezetecan).
  • Combination first-line for EGFR with an OS benefit (FLAURA2, MARIPOSA), so osimertinib monotherapy is no longer the only standard.
  • Next-generation KRAS (divarasib beats sotorasib/adagrasib head-to-head) and pan-RAS(ON) inhibition (daraxonrasib, in the NCCN emerging-biomarker table).

6.2 Uncommon EGFR mutations (G719X, L861Q, S768I, compound mutations)

  • Afatinib. In the pooled LUX-Lung 2/3/6 analysis (Yang, Lancet Oncol 2015), "major uncommon" mutations (G719X, L861Q, S768I) had an ORR of about 71% and median PFS of about 10.7 mo verify. FDA label expansion followed in 2018.
  • Osimertinib. KCSG-LU15-09 (Cho, JCO 2020; n=36): ORR 50%, median PFS 8.2 mo verify. It is preferred by many for CNS coverage.
  • Compound mutations (e.g., G719X + S768I) generally do as well as or better than single uncommon mutations. Exon 18 deletions and E709X respond to afatinib.
  • De-novo T790M calls for osimertinib. Exon 20 insertions are a separate entity (below). A769_D770insASV-type insertions are poorly sensitive to all classical TKIs, whereas A763_Y764insFQEA is sensitive to them.
  • NCCN lists afatinib, osimertinib, gefitinib, erlotinib and dacomitinib for S768I, L861Q and G719X, with afatinib or osimertinib preferred.

For first line, NCCN/ASCO list crizotinib, entrectinib, repotrectinib and taletrectinib. Prefer a CNS-active agent when brain metastases are present.

Larotrectinib (pooled lung cohort ORR ~73% verify; small n), entrectinib and repotrectinib (tumour-agnostic approval Jun 2024) are all approved. STK11 / KEAP1 / SMARCA4 nuances

  • STK11 loss gives a "cold" TME and is associated with poor outcomes on PD-(L)1 monotherapy. It is partly prognostic, not purely predictive.
  • KEAP1 (NRF2 activation) predicts poor outcomes across chemo and IO.
  • POSEIDON exploratory and CheckMate 227 subsets suggest that adding a CTLA-4 inhibitor helps STK11/KEAP1-mutant disease. Prospective trials (e.g., TRITON) are testing this trial results not checked.
  • SMARCA4-deficient tumours are aggressive. Chemo-IO is preferred.

Duration and de-escalation

  • Two years of IO is the trial-defined norm. DIAL (IFCT-2103, phase 2; ESMO 2026) found similar 18- and 24-month survival when IO was stopped at 6 months, with pemetrexed maintenance continued. The phase 3 is ongoing.
  • MOIO (ESMO 2026): 12-weekly IO dosing failed non-inferiority, with more deaths. Do not extend intervals beyond labelled schedules.
  • India: weight-based and vial-sharing pembrolizumab, and low-dose nivolumab, are used for affordability. Evidence for low-dose IO in lung cancer is limited compared with head and neck (Tata data) lung-specific data not checked.

17. Special clinical settings

Brain metastases

  • Driver-positive, asymptomatic, small: start a CNS-penetrant TKI (osimertinib, lorlatinib, alectinib, brigatinib, selpercatinib, repotrectinib/taletrectinib) and get an MRI at 6–8 weeks. Defer SRS. FLAURA2 and CROWN show high IC control.
  • Symptomatic, large (>3 cm), oedema or posterior fossa: SRS or surgery first, then systemic therapy.
  • Driver-negative: SRS for limited lesions (1–4, and increasingly up to 10–15 by total volume), then chemo-IO. Pembrolizumab has activity in untreated small brain mets (phase 2, ~30% IC ORR) verify.
  • Avoid WBRT where possible (QUARTZ showed no QoL/OS benefit vs supportive care in poor-prognosis patients). If WBRT is used, add hippocampal avoidance + memantine.

Leptomeningeal disease

  • EGFRm: osimertinib 160 mg (BLOOM; LM ORR ~62% by BICR) verify. Amivantamab + lazertinib also has CSF activity.
  • ALK: lorlatinib. Consider an intrathecal pemetrexed trial approach (Chinese phase 1/2 data) verify.
  • Focal RT for symptomatic bulky sites; proton craniospinal irradiation (phase 2, mixed histologies) where available.

Oligometastatic disease: consolidative local therapy

  • Gomez (Lancet Oncol 2016; JCO 2019; n=49): LCT after first-line systemic therapy gave PFS 14.2 vs 4.4 mo and OS 41.2 vs 17.0 mo.
  • SINDAS (JCO 2023 year verify; n=133; EGFRm, China): TKI + upfront RT gave PFS 20.2 vs 12.5 mo and OS 25.5 vs 17.4 mo (1st-generation TKI era).
  • SABR-COMET (JCO 2020; n=99, mixed primaries): 5-yr OS 42.3% vs 17.7%.
  • SARON (ESMO 2026; n=140; driver-negative): PFS 13.9 vs 8.9 mo (HR 0.64) and local failure 13% vs 40%, but OS 31.0 vs 28.0 mo (HR 0.99). This tempers enthusiasm in the IO era.
  • Practical: ≤3–5 lesions with controlled disease after 3–4 months of systemic therapy → discuss SABR/surgery at the tumour board. NRG-LU002 (maintenance ± LCT) did not show a clear benefit verify.

Elderly and PS 2

  • IFCT-0501 (Lancet 2011): monthly carboplatin + weekly paclitaxel vs monotherapy in patients aged 70–89 gave OS 10.3 vs 6.2 mo verify.
  • IPSOS (Lancet 2023; n=453): atezolizumab vs single-agent chemotherapy in platinum-ineligible patients (PS 2–3 or aged ≥70 with comorbidity) gave OS 10.3 vs 9.2 mo (HR 0.78), with 2-yr OS doubled verify.
  • Driver-positive PS 3–4 patients can still be offered a TKI. Dramatic "Lazarus" responses are common with EGFR, ALK and ROS1.
  • Use G8/CARG geriatric screens. Pemetrexed needs CrCl ≥45 mL/min plus folate/B12.

Bone metastases

  • Denosumab 120 mg SC q4w or zoledronic acid 4 mg q3–4w (q12w de-escalation acceptable). Denosumab was superior for time to first SRE in solid tumours (Henry 2011). A lung-subgroup OS signal was exploratory.
  • Calcium + vitamin D; dental review (osteonecrosis of the jaw); watch hypocalcaemia with denosumab.
  • 8 Gy single-fraction RT for pain; SBRT for oligo or spine lesions (SC.24); spinal cord compression → steroids + surgery/RT.

Malignant pleural effusion

  • Send the fluid as a cell block for NGS. Fluid can also be used for PD-L1, with caution.
  • Recurrent effusion: indwelling pleural catheter (IPC) or talc pleurodesis. IPC + talc (IPC-PLUS) improves pleurodesis rates. TKIs and bevacizumab-containing regimens often control effusions.

Transformation to SCLC and re-biopsy at progression

  • Occurs in ~3–14% of EGFRm tumours at TKI resistance (median ~18 months). Higher risk with TP53 + RB1 alterations at baseline. Suspect it with a rising NSE/ProGRP or rapid visceral or brain progression.
  • Treat with platinum-etoposide ± continued TKI. IO adds little. Tarlatamab (DLL3) is an option at relapse transformed-SCLC data limited.
  • Re-biopsy at every progression on targeted therapy when feasible. Plasma first, then tissue if negative or if transformation is suspected (plasma cannot diagnose histology).

Drug interactions (including TB co-treatment)

  • Most TKIs are CYP3A4 substrates. Watch rifampicin (TB co-treatment is common in India) and azoles. Rifampicin markedly lowers osimertinib, alectinib and lorlatinib levels; switch to rifabutin or increase the dose per label.
  • PPIs reduce gefitinib, erlotinib, dacomitinib and capmatinib absorption (time doses around them). Osimertinib and lorlatinib are not affected.

Points of disagreement and nuance

24. Clinical pearls and self-test questions

Ten pearls

  1. Never start IO in a never-smoker with adenocarcinoma before driver results are back.
  2. A negative plasma NGS result is not a negative test. Get tissue (ideally DNA + RNA).
  3. MRI brain is part of baseline staging for every stage IV adenocarcinoma.
  4. For EGFRm disease, two first-line regimens now beat osimertinib on OS. Match the choice to patient burden, CNS status and access.
  5. Gefitinib + pemetrexed-carboplatin (Tata Memorial) is the Indian evidence-based, low-cost intensification.
  6. Lorlatinib's 5-year PFS of 60% means ALK+ disease is increasingly a chronic illness. Manage the lipids and the mood.
  7. Re-biopsy at progression: MET (IHC/FISH), SCLC transformation and new fusions all change management.
  8. RET, ALK, ROS1 and HER2-mutant tumours respond poorly to IO. Give TKIs and ADCs first.
  9. KRAS G12C + STK11/KEAP1 co-mutations call for dual checkpoint inhibition or a trial.
  10. After chemo-IO, docetaxel ± ramucirumab is still the benchmark that most new agents fail to beat.

Self-test questions (with short answers)

What does M1c1 vs M1c2 mean in TNM 9?
M1c1 means multiple extrathoracic metastases in one organ system. M1c2 means more than one organ system. Both are stage IVB.
Name the OS-positive first-line EGFR trials vs osimertinib.
FLAURA2 (osi + chemo; HR 0.77; 47.5 vs 37.6 mo) and MARIPOSA (ami + laz; HR 0.75; 3-yr OS 60% vs 51%).
Which assay does not fit PD-L1 TPS interchangeability?
SP142. 22C3, 28-8 and SP263 are broadly comparable for TPS.
Mechanisms of osimertinib resistance?
MET amplification/overexpression, C797S, histologic transformation, HER2 amplification, fusions (ALK/RET/BRAF), BRAF V600E/KRAS/PIK3CA, and unknown mechanisms in about 40–50%.
Which first-line trial established cemiplimab in PD-L1 ≥50%?
EMPOWER-Lung 1 (OS HR 0.57 at primary analysis).
Why did sotorasib fail to improve OS in CodeBreaK 200?
Modest PFS gain (HR 0.66), crossover from docetaxel, and limited single-agent potency. Divarasib later improved OS head-to-head (Krascendo 1, HR 0.56).
What did SARON show?
Consolidation RT in driver-negative synchronous oligometastatic NSCLC improved PFS (HR 0.64) and local control but not OS (HR 0.99).
Which HER2 drugs are FDA-approved for first line (Nov 2026)?
Zongertinib (Feb 2026) and sevabertinib (Sept 2026), both accelerated. T-DXd 1L (DESTINY-Lung04) is reported but not yet in NCCN first line.

25. Caveats and limitations

  • Figures marked verify come from memory or secondary summaries (congress coverage, press releases, KOL summaries) and were not checked against the primary publication this session. Older landmark trials (FLAURA CNS ORR, ALEX/ALTA-1L intracranial ORR, VISION, ARROW, LIBRETTO-001 PFS, CheckMate 9LA PFS, COCOON percentages, IFCT-0501, IPSOS, BLOOM) fall in this group.

Several trial sample sizes (WU-KONG28, DESTINY-Lung04, PERSEE, OptiTROP-Lung06) were not available and are shown as "—".
  • NCCN v9.2026 content is summarised from secondary reports (OncoDaily, AJMC, ASCO Post) because the guideline needs a login. Category labels and "preferred" status for EGFR first-line regimens should be checked on nccn.org.
  • ASCO living guideline: the HER2 text reflects the Feb 2026 (v2026.3.0) summary. Later 2026.3.x versions may differ.
  • Indian drug prices are unofficial online-pharmacy listings (PharmEasy, PlatinumRx, MrMed) and a clinic blog (generic alectinib). They are not government tender or PMJAY rates.

  • Indian guideline documents (ICMR consensus, NCG) were not re-read this session. Statements about them are general and marked verify.
  • Driver-prevalence donut values are teaching approximations from published ranges. The Indian KRAS, BRAF, MET, RET and HER2 slices are especially uncertain.
  • Pending regulatory events (as of 11 Nov 2026): ivonescimab BLA, PDUFA 14 Nov 2026; neladalkib, PDUFA 27 Nov 2026; zipalertinib, PDUFA 27 Feb 2027. Re-check after these dates.
