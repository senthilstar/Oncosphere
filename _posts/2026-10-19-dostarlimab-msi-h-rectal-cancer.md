On **19 October 2026**, the US FDA granted **accelerated approval** to dostarlimab (Jemperli, GSK) for adults with **Stage II/III locally advanced rectal cancer that is MSI-H or dMMR**, as determined by an FDA-authorised test.

## Background
About 5–10% of rectal cancers are mismatch-repair deficient. The landmark single-centre study from Memorial Sloan Kettering (Cercek et al., NEJM 2022) reported that the first 12 patients treated with dostarlimab alone had a clinical complete response, avoiding chemoradiation and surgery at the time of reporting.

## For patients
If you have rectal cancer, ask whether your biopsy has been tested for **MMR (mismatch repair) or MSI**. The result can change which treatment is best.

<p class="note">Accelerated approval means continued approval may depend on confirmatory trials. This is a US decision.</p>

## How the treatment is given
In the Memorial Sloan Kettering study, dostarlimab 500 mg was given intravenously every 3 weeks for 6 months. Patients with a clinical complete response (by endoscopy, MRI and digital rectal examination) entered watch-and-wait follow-up instead of chemoradiation and surgery. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2201445">[NEJM 2022]</a> Check the FDA label for the approved dose and schedule.

## Why dMMR tumours respond
Mismatch-repair–deficient tumours accumulate thousands of mutations, producing neoantigens that the immune system can recognise once the PD-1 "brake" is released. The same biology underlies immunotherapy benefit in dMMR metastatic colorectal cancer (KEYNOTE-177). <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2017699">[NEJM 2020]</a> dMMR rectal tumours, in contrast, respond relatively poorly to standard chemotherapy.

## What accelerated approval does and does not mean
- It is based on a surrogate endpoint (here, clinical complete response) that is "reasonably likely" to predict clinical benefit.
- Durability beyond a few years, and the best surveillance schedule, are still being studied.
- Organ preservation requires intensive follow-up: regular MRI, endoscopy and CEA. Missing follow-up visits is risky.

## Questions to ask your doctor
1. Has my tumour been tested for MMR proteins (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) or MSI?
2. If dMMR, could I have Lynch syndrome, and should my family be tested?
3. Is immunotherapy available to me, and what follow-up would organ preservation need?
