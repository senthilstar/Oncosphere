On **19 October 2026**, the US FDA granted **accelerated approval** to single-agent dostarlimab for adults with **stage II/III locally advanced rectal cancer** that is MSI-H or mismatch-repair deficient (dMMR), determined by an FDA-authorised test for solid tumours. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-dostarlimab-locally-advanced-rectal-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a>

## Background

In the initial single-centre study, all 12 patients who had completed dostarlimab treatment had a clinical complete response (cCR), with no chemoradiation or surgery at the time of reporting. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2201445">[NEJM 2022]</a> A cCR means no tumour is detected on the specified clinical assessments; it does not prove cure.

## Updated evidence: expanded single-centre study

The phase 2 study enrolled 117 patients with stage I–III dMMR tumours eligible for curative surgery across rectal and non-rectal cohorts, with dostarlimab planned for 6 months. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661660/">[NEJM 2025]</a>

- **Rectal cohort:** all 49 patients who completed treatment achieved cCR and chose non-operative management; 37 had maintained cCR for at least 12 months after treatment. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661660/">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- **Durability:** in 50 rectal patients, estimated 2-year recurrence-free survival was 96% (95% CI 90–100), with median recurrence follow-up of 30.2 months. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661660/">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- **Non-rectal cohort:** 35 of 54 treatment completers achieved cCR; estimated 2-year recurrence-free survival was 85% (95% CI 70–100) among 67 patients. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661660/">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- **Safety:** among 124 patients receiving at least one dose, grade 3 or 4 events occurred, including encephalitis, diabetes and neutropenia; immune toxicity can be serious and may persist. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661660/">[NEJM 2025]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a>

## The pivotal trial behind the approval: AZUR-1

AZUR-1 enrolled **154** previously untreated patients with MSI-H/dMMR stage II/III locally advanced rectal cancer; 86% had stage III disease and 26% had T4 tumours. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a> At the **20 April 2026** interim cutoff, median follow-up was **26.3 months**; this was a single-arm study, so it cannot directly compare outcomes with standard treatment. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42927858/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>

cCR12 is cCR maintained for at least 12 months after treatment without additional cancer treatment; the label reports the following results. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a>

| Endpoint | Investigator | Independent central review |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluable for cCR12 | 144 | 116 |
| cCR12, n (%) and 95% CI | 98 (68%); 60–76 | 54 (47%); 37–56 |
| Objective response, n (%) and 95% CI | 126 (82%); 75–88 | 121 (79%); 71–85 |
| cCR at first or second post-treatment assessment | 102 (66%) | 43 (28%) |
| Near-complete response at those assessments | 9 (6%) | 44 (29%) |

The table uses the label's different denominators: evaluable patients for cCR12 and all 154 enrolled patients for response at the first or second assessment after treatment. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a> Central-review cCR12 was less mature, with cCR first identified later than by investigators; 38 patients with central-review cCR were still being followed for cCR12. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42927858/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a>

- **Composite response:** the published interim analysis reports investigator-assessed cCR12 or pathological complete response (pCR) after dostarlimab alone in **110/144 (76%)**; this includes **12/15** patients who underwent surgery after dostarlimab alone and had pCR. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42927858/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a> The composite includes surgery and should not be read as the organ-preservation rate.
- **Subsequent treatment:** of 154 patients, 110 (71%) had no further oncological treatment and had not progressed or relapsed; 26 (17%) underwent surgery, 13 (8%) received additional systemic treatment or radiation without surgery, one progressed without further treatment, and four withdrew or were lost to follow-up. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a>
- **Safety:** serious adverse reactions occurred in 20%, treatment discontinuation for adverse reactions in 3%, and immune-mediated reactions in 23% of 154 patients. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a> Fatigue occurred in 32%, rash in 22%, diarrhoea or colitis in 18%, pruritus in 17%, hypothyroidism in 13%, and rectal stenosis in 3.9%; a fatal sepsis event was associated with intestinal obstruction and postoperative complications. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a>

In the label's separate single-centre study, **cCR12 was 93% (95% CI 82–99)**; 50 patients were enrolled. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a> Of 27 followed for at least 3 years, one had a local recurrence treated with lymphadenectomy. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a>

**Confirmation required:** the FDA requires continued AZUR-1 follow-up for cCR12, cCR24 and cCR36, organ preservation and relapse or metastatic disease at 3 years, and survival at 5 years; trial completion is scheduled for October 2029 and final report submission for July 2030. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/761174Orig1s015ltr.pdf">[FDA letter 2026]</a>

## For patients

Ask whether your rectal cancer biopsy has been tested for mismatch repair proteins or MSI. The result can help determine whether immunotherapy is an option. A complete response still requires surveillance, and further treatment may be needed if cancer remains or returns.

<p class="note">Accelerated approval means continued approval may depend on confirmatory trials. This is a US decision.</p>

## How the treatment is given

The US label specifies **500 mg intravenously every 3 weeks for 9 cycles**, stopping earlier for disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a> AZUR-1 assessed response with endoscopy at week 6, multimodal assessment at week 12 and again after treatment; patients with cCR at either of the first two post-treatment assessments could enter non-operative management. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a> Surveillance in that trial used multimodal assessments every 4 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months thereafter. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a> Patients without cCR or with recurrence or progression were evaluated for standard treatment. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[US label 2026]</a>

## Why dMMR tumours respond

Defective mismatch repair can produce mutation-rich tumours with antigens that make them susceptible to immune recognition; PD-1 blockade can release an immune checkpoint. The same biomarker identifies metastatic colorectal cancers that can benefit from immunotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2017699">[NEJM 2020]</a>

## What accelerated approval does and does not mean

- The approval uses sustained cCR as an early measure; long-term clinical benefit and relapse risk need further follow-up. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/761174Orig1s015ltr.pdf">[FDA letter 2026]</a>
- A clinical response does not guarantee that surgery will never be needed. Decisions about residual or recurrent disease require a multidisciplinary team.
- Organ preservation requires reliable follow-up with clinical assessment, imaging and endoscopy. Discuss surveillance access before choosing this approach.

## Questions to ask your doctor

1. Has my tumour been tested for MMR proteins (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) or MSI?
2. If dMMR, could I have Lynch syndrome, and should my family be tested?
3. Is immunotherapy available to me, and what follow-up would organ preservation need?
