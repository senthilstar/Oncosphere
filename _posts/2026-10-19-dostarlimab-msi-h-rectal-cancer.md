On **19 October 2026**, the US FDA granted **accelerated approval** to dostarlimab (Jemperli, GSK) for adults with **Stage II/III locally advanced rectal cancer that is MSI-H or dMMR**, as determined by an FDA-authorised test.

## Background
About 5–10% of rectal cancers are mismatch-repair deficient. The landmark single-centre study from Memorial Sloan Kettering (Cercek et al., NEJM 2022) reported that the first 12 patients treated with dostarlimab alone had a clinical complete response, avoiding chemoradiation and surgery at the time of reporting.

## Updated evidence: the expanded MSK study (NEJM 2025)
The same phase 2 study was expanded to 117 patients with Stage I–III dMMR tumours, given 6 months of dostarlimab. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2404512">[NEJM 2025]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661660/">[PMC full text]</a>
- **Rectal cohort:** all **49 of 49** patients who completed treatment had a clinical complete response, and none needed surgery on the primary tumour. 37 had a sustained complete response at 12 months, meeting the primary endpoint.
- **Rectal cohort durability:** 2-year recurrence-free survival **96%** (95% CI 90–100), median follow-up for recurrence 30.2 months.
- **Other dMMR tumours (colon, gastric, urothelial and others):** 35 of 54 (65%) had a clinical complete response; 2-year recurrence-free survival 85%.
- **Safety:** 95% of patients had either no adverse events (35%) or reversible grade 1–2 events (60%). No patient lost the chance of curative surgery.

## The pivotal trial behind the approval: AZUR-1
The approval rests mainly on **AZUR-1** (NCT05723562), a multicentre, single-arm phase 2 trial of dostarlimab alone in **154** previously untreated MSI-H/dMMR Stage II/III rectal cancers (86% Stage III; 26% T4). Dostarlimab 500 mg IV every 3 weeks for **9 cycles**; response checked by endoscopy, rectal MRI and CT at week 6, week 12 and about 3 weeks after the last dose. Interim cut-off 20 Apr 2026, median follow-up 26 months. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-dostarlimab-locally-advanced-rectal-cancer">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[USPI 10/2026]</a>

| Endpoint (AZUR-1) | Investigator | Independent central review |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluable for 12-month sustained cCR (cCR12) | 144 | 116 |
| **cCR12 (primary endpoint is per central review)** | **68%** (95% CI 60–76) | **47%** (95% CI 37–56) |
| Objective response | 82% (75–88) | 79% (71–85) |
| Clinical complete response | 66% | 28% |
| Near-complete response | 6% | 29% |

- The gap between investigator and central-review cCR12 is largely immature follow-up: 38 patients in cCR by central review had less than 12 months of follow-up at cut-off, and 44 more were near-complete responders.
- **What happened to the 154:** 110 (71%) needed nothing beyond dostarlimab and had not progressed; 26 (17%) had surgery; 13 (8%) had extra systemic therapy or radiation; 1 progressed without further treatment; 4 withdrew.
- Of 15 who went to surgery after dostarlimab alone because imaging suggested residual tumour, **12 had a pathological complete response**.
- No progression among patients with cCR12, cCR or near-cCR at median follow-up of 26.3 months.
- **Safety (n=154):** serious adverse reactions 20%; discontinuation for toxicity 3%; immune-mediated reactions 23%. Commonest: fatigue 32%, rash 22%, diarrhoea/colitis 18%, pruritus 17%, hypothyroidism 13%. Rectal stenosis 3.9%; one death from sepsis linked to intestinal obstruction and post-surgical complications.

The FDA also reviewed the MSK study (19-288, n=50 in the label): cCR12 **93%** (95% CI 82–99), objective response 100%, no chemoradiation or surgery needed; among 27 patients followed at least 3 years, 1 local recurrence (treated with lymphadenectomy). <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[USPI 10/2026]</a>

**Confirmation required:** AZUR-1 must report cCR24 and cCR36, 3-year organ-preservation and relapse rates, and 5-year survival (trial completion 10/2029, final report 07/2030). <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/761174Orig1s015ltr.pdf">[FDA letter]</a> GSK says a combined complete-response rate of 76% (110/144, counting pCR at surgery) will be presented as an ESMO 2026 late-breaker. <a class="cite" href="https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/media/press-releases/jemperli-dostarlimab-data-show-76-of-patients-with-msi-hdmmr-locally-advanced-rectal-cancer-achieved-a-complete-response/">[GSK]</a>

## For patients
If you have rectal cancer, ask whether your biopsy has been tested for **MMR (mismatch repair) or MSI**. The result can change which treatment is best.

<p class="note">Accelerated approval means continued approval may depend on confirmatory trials. This is a US decision.</p>

## How the treatment is given
In the Memorial Sloan Kettering study, dostarlimab 500 mg was given intravenously every 3 weeks for 6 months. Patients with a clinical complete response (by endoscopy, MRI and digital rectal examination) entered watch-and-wait follow-up instead of chemoradiation and surgery. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2201445">[NEJM 2022]</a> The approved label dose is the same: **500 mg IV every 3 weeks for 9 cycles** (about 6 months). <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761174s015lbl.pdf">[USPI 10/2026]</a> Non-operative surveillance in AZUR-1 was every 4 months for 2 years, then every 6 months.

## Why dMMR tumours respond
Mismatch-repair–deficient tumours accumulate thousands of mutations, producing neoantigens that the immune system can recognise once the PD-1 "brake" is released. The same biology underlies immunotherapy benefit in dMMR metastatic colorectal cancer (KEYNOTE-177). <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2017699">[NEJM 2020]</a> dMMR rectal tumours, in contrast, respond relatively poorly to standard chemotherapy.

## What accelerated approval does and does not mean
- It is based on a surrogate endpoint (here, clinical complete response) that is "reasonably likely" to predict clinical benefit.
- Durability beyond a few years, and the best surveillance schedule, are still being studied.
- Organ preservation requires intensive follow-up: regular MRI, endoscopy and CEA. Missing follow-up visits is risky.

## Questions to ask your doctor
1. Has my tumour been tested for MMR proteins (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) or MSI?
2. If dMMR, could I have Lynch syndrome, and should my family be tested?
3. Is immunotherapy available to me, and what follow-up would organ preservation need?
