## Why this matters

A blood test that searches for signals from multiple cancers may reach diseases without established screening programmes. The relevant clinical question is whether investigation and treatment after a signal improve outcomes, not simply whether more cancers are found.

In NHS-Galleri, adding the test to usual care did **not reduce the combined incidence of stage III–IV cancer**, the primary endpoint. Stage IV results warrant follow-up, but a mortality benefit has not been demonstrated. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a>

## How the test and the trial worked

Galleri analyses methylation patterns in cell-free DNA and, when a cancer signal is detected, predicts its likely origin to guide investigation. A result is not a diagnosis; a negative result cannot exclude cancer. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a>

In England, **142,250** participants aged **50–77 years** were randomised **1:1** after a baseline blood draw: **71,122** to testing and **71,128** to stored, untested samples; there were up to **three annual visits**. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a> Participants and trial staff were initially unaware of allocation. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a>

Positive results prompted referral through NHS urgent suspected-cancer pathways. The primary endpoint was incidence of stage III or IV disease across **12 prespecified cancer types**; stage IV in those types was a key secondary endpoint. GRAIL funded the trial, registered as **NCT05611632**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a>

## Primary result: no fall in stage III–IV cancer

There were **706 versus 688** primary-endpoint events in the tested and control groups: incidence rate ratio (IRR) **1.03 (95% CI 0.92–1.14; P=0.63)**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a> The primary endpoint was not met; the confidence interval includes both a decrease and an increase. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a>

Across successive rounds, stage III–IV IRRs were **1.19 (95% CI 0.98–1.43)**, **0.95 (0.77–1.17)** and **0.88 (0.73–1.07)**. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a> Each interval includes **1**; the later estimates do not overturn the overall primary result. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a>

## Stage IV, earlier stages and route to diagnosis

There were **342 versus 397** stage IV events in the prespecified cancer types, with an overall IRR of **0.86 (95% CI 0.74–1.00)**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a> By round the estimates were **0.91 (0.71–1.18)**, **0.78 (0.57–1.06)** and **0.74 (0.57–0.95)**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a> This secondary result is encouraging but cannot establish fewer cancer deaths.

For the prespecified cancers, stage I–II diagnoses were **647 versus 559**, relative risk **1.16 (95% CI 1.03–1.30)**. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a> Across all cancers, screen-detected diagnoses were **1,173 versus 290**, clinically detected diagnoses **2,464 versus 3,110**, and emergency presentations **225 versus 286**. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a>

Across all cancer types, **3,637 versus 3,400** cancers were diagnosed over **three years** of screening. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.17_suppl.LBA100">[ASCO 2026]</a> More diagnoses and a change in route to diagnosis do not necessarily mean longer life; follow-up is needed to assess overdiagnosis and patient outcomes.

## How accurate was the test?

The performance analysis was descriptive and did not test hypotheses. Across the screening rounds, positive results occurred in **722/70,325 (1.03%)**, **518/64,498 (0.80%)** and **561/62,323 (0.90%)** participants; **937 participants** had Galleri-detected primary cancers in aggregate. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a> This test-specific count is different from the randomised comparison of all screen-detected cancers, which includes other screening routes.

Positive predictive value (PPV), the proportion of positive tests associated with a cancer diagnosis, was **58.0%, 50.4% and 45.8%** in the successive rounds. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a> A positive result therefore needs a diagnostic workup; a substantial proportion of positive results were not associated with cancer.

Specificity ranged from **99.50% to 99.60%**; episode sensitivity ranged from **26.7% to 37.2%** for all cancers and **47.6% to 63.4%** for the prespecified types. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a> Among true-positive results, the first or second predicted cancer origin was correct in **91.1%–93.6%** across rounds; first-prediction accuracy pooled across rounds was **87.0% (815/937)**. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a> These estimates describe the trial setting and do not mean that a negative test rules out cancer.

## The FDA advisory panel and where approval stands

GRAIL submitted its premarket approval application on **29 January 2026**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/194909/download">[FDA 2026]</a> On **23 September 2026**, the FDA panel reviewed a proposed use in adults aged **50 or older** and voted **10–0** for safety, **6–4** for effectiveness and **7–2**, with **one abstention**, for benefits outweighing risks. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/195032/download">[FDA 2026]</a>

The panel discussed variation in sensitivity by cancer type, limited numbers for some cancers, uncertainty about long-term outcomes and whether the proposed use should include the word “early”. It recommended clear labelling, continued established screening and further follow-up, including after approval. Screening for breast, colorectal, lung, cervical or prostate cancer should continue where recommended. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/195032/download">[FDA 2026]</a>

The vote was advice, not FDA authorisation. In its **5 November 2026** quarterly filing, GRAIL reported that Galleri had **not been FDA approved or cleared**, and that the company expected a decision by **early first quarter 2027**; that timing is a company expectation, not an FDA commitment. <a class="cite" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1699031/000162828026071041/gral-20260930.htm">[GRAIL SEC filing 2026]</a> The test is offered in the US as a laboratory-developed test. <a class="cite" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1699031/000162828026071041/gral-20260930.htm">[GRAIL SEC filing 2026]</a>

## What it changes in practice

Discuss the unmet primary endpoint and lack of demonstrated mortality benefit with patients considering testing. A positive signal calls for a structured workup directed by the predicted origin, potentially including imaging, endoscopy and biopsy; diagnostic confirmation remains necessary.

A negative result must not delay symptom assessment or replace recommended screening. Fewer than **1%** of participants experienced trial-related adverse events, and none were serious. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2505723">[NEJM 2026]</a> The burden and uncertainty of investigating a positive result also matter when deciding whether to test.

## India and resource-limited settings

CDSCO approval and routine availability in India could not be verified. US central-laboratory processing raises access and sample-logistics questions; performance in English adults cannot establish performance in Indian populations, and PPV varies with cancer prevalence.

The trial linked testing to NHS urgent diagnostic pathways. Implementing a similar approach requires capacity for timely imaging, endoscopy, biopsy and treatment, as well as evidence that the complete pathway benefits patients.

Priorities include tobacco control and appropriate established cervical, breast and oral cavity screening; India’s programme includes visual oral examination. <a class="cite" href="https://platform.who.int/docs/default-source/mca-documents/policy-documents/operational-guidance/ind-cc-37-07-operational-guidance-2016-eng-operational-framework-management-of-common-cancers.pdf">[National Health Mission 2016]</a> Direct-to-consumer marketing warrants caution while local performance, access and consequences of follow-up remain uncertain.

## Open questions

Longer follow-up is needed for mortality, overdiagnosis, psychological effects and resource use. Sensitivity varies by cancer type and should be explained when interpreting a result. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13668664/">[Nature Medicine 2026]</a> It must establish whether the stage IV pattern translates into better patient outcomes and how to interpret additional diagnoses.

Health-system decisions require both test performance and the consequences of resolving positive signals, including access to timely investigation.

## Bottom line

NHS-Galleri missed its primary stage III–IV endpoint. The stage IV findings and changes in diagnostic routes justify further follow-up; established screening and symptom assessment remain essential. Patients need to understand both the possible value of a positive signal and the limits of a negative result.
