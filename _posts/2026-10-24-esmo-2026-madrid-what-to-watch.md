The European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress 2026 is being held in **Madrid from 23 to 27 October 2026**.

## Key sessions
- **Presidential Symposium I** — Saturday 24 October, 16:30–18:15 CEST (20:00–21:45 IST), Alicante Auditorium, Hall 6. Late-breaking abstracts span melanoma, bladder cancer, small-cell lung cancer and high-risk prostate cancer.
- **Presidential Symposium II** — Sunday 25 October, 16:30–18:15 CET (21:00–22:45 IST), same venue, covering NSCLC, gastric and biliary-tract cancers.

The programme also includes sessions on artificial intelligence in oncology, toxicities of novel therapies and patient-advocacy sessions on access and equity — topics highly relevant to public-sector oncology in India.

I will post short summaries of the practice-changing results here after the data are presented. Abstracts are available through the official ESMO programme linked below.

## How to follow ESMO from India
- Presidential Symposia fall in the Indian evening (times above in IST). Recorded sessions are typically available to registered delegates on the ESMO platform.
- Abstracts and late-breaking abstracts are published in *Annals of Oncology*; simultaneous papers often appear in NEJM or The Lancet on the day of presentation.
- ESMO Daily Reporter and society social-media channels provide same-day summaries.

## How to judge a "practice-changing" headline
1. **Was the endpoint overall survival or a surrogate** (PFS, pCR, response)? Surrogates can mislead.
2. **Was the control arm the current standard** where you practise?
3. **Is the absolute benefit clinically meaningful** relative to toxicity and cost? ESMO's own Magnitude of Clinical Benefit Scale (ESMO-MCBS) helps grade this.
4. **Is the result published and peer reviewed**, or only presented?
5. **Is the drug accessible**, and is there a cheaper equivalent?
