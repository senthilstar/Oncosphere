On **13 November 2026** the FDA approved **durvalumab** (Imfinzi) with **enfortumab vedotin-ejfv** as neoadjuvant treatment, followed by single-agent durvalumab as adjuvant treatment after radical cystectomy, for adults with **muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) who are ineligible for or have refused cisplatin-based chemotherapy**. Priority review.

## The regimen
- **Neoadjuvant:** durvalumab 1500 mg + enfortumab vedotin 1.25 mg/kg, 3 cycles (q3w).
- **Radical cystectomy.**
- **Adjuvant:** durvalumab q4w, 9 cycles.

## VOLGA in brief
Randomised, open-label, phase 3 ([NCT04960709](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04960709)); **695** patients randomised 1:1:1 — durvalumab + EV (n = 233), the same plus tremelimumab (n = 233, not approved), or upfront cystectomy ± locally approved adjuvant therapy (n = 229). Stratified by PD-L1, clinical stage and cisplatin status (ineligible vs refusal). Primary endpoint: EFS by blinded independent central review.

| Durvalumab + EV vs cystectomy alone | Result |
|---|---|
| EFS HR | **0.51** (95% CI 0.38–0.67); p < 0.0001 |
| Median EFS | Not reached (33.2–NR) vs **12.1 mo** (7.6–27.0) |
| 24-month EFS | ~64% vs ~45% |
| OS HR | **0.58** (95% CI 0.40–0.83); p = 0.0027 |
| 24-month OS | ~78% vs ~69% |
| Grade 3–4 AEs (any cause) | 64% vs 44% |

- Median follow-up 23 months (data cut-off 2 March 2026).
- EFS benefit held for **PD-L1-high** (HR 0.58) and **PD-L1-low/negative** (HR 0.46), and for **cisplatin-ineligible** (HR 0.53) and **cisplatin-refusing** (HR 0.49) patients.
- Full trial detail, including the triplet arm, is in our [ESMO 2026 Presidential Symposium I note]({{ '/esmo-2026-presidential-symposium-1/' | relative_url }}).

## Practice points
- Fills the gap where cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy was not an option and the standard was cystectomy alone (± adjuvant nivolumab).
- Toxicity is front-loaded before surgery: watch EV skin reactions, neuropathy, hyperglycaemia and immune-related events, and keep the cystectomy date protected.
- The comparator was surgery, not another active perioperative regimen; cisplatin-eligible patients remain outside this label.
