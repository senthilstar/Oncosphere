## Why this matters

A rising molecular signal can now prompt a treatment decision before a scan shows progression. In SERENA-6, patients receiving an aromatase inhibitor (AI) and a CDK4/6 inhibitor underwent serial blood testing for an acquired ESR1 mutation. On detection, changing the AI to oral camizestrant while retaining the CDK4/6 inhibitor delayed progression compared with continuing the original regimen. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a>

This is a change in the timing of treatment, not merely a new drug choice. The FDA granted accelerated approval on **4 Sep 2026**, after an advisory committee had voted against the evidence being clinically meaningful. The EU had authorised the treatment on **20 Jul 2026**. A longer progression-free interval is demonstrated; whether an early switch improves survival or the whole treatment strategy remains unresolved. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/fda-odac-vote-on-camizestrant-breast-cancer.html">[AstraZeneca]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/etcamah">[EMA]</a>

## The biology in brief

Oestrogen receptor signalling drives many HR-positive breast cancers. AIs reduce oestrogen availability, but tumours can acquire **ESR1 mutations** that permit receptor signalling despite oestrogen deprivation. A mutation detected in circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) is a molecular sign of resistance, even while imaging still suggests disease control.

Camizestrant is an oral selective oestrogen receptor degrader (SERD) and complete ER antagonist, active against wild-type and mutant ESR1. The rationale is to change the endocrine partner as resistance emerges, while maintaining CDK4/6 inhibition. ctDNA surveillance makes that timing possible; a negative blood result, however, must be interpreted in the context of assay performance and tumour DNA shedding. <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/etcamah">[EMA]</a>

## SERENA-6 design

SERENA-6 was randomised, double-blind and placebo-controlled. Its **315** participants had received initial endocrine-based treatment with an AI plus a CDK4/6 inhibitor for at least **6 months**, without progression, and had an ESR1 mutation detected during serial ctDNA testing. They were randomised **1:1** to replace the AI with camizestrant or continue the AI, with the same CDK4/6 inhibitor continued. The control AIs were anastrozole or letrozole. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a>

Investigator-assessed progression-free survival (PFS), measured from mutation detection, was the primary endpoint. Blinded independent central review (BICR) assessed PFS too. Later endpoints included PFS on subsequent treatment (PFS2), overall survival (OS) and patient-reported quality of life (QoL). Pre/perimenopausal women and men received an LHRH agonist in the trial; the EU indication specifies an LHRH agonist or antagonist for these groups. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2502929">[NEJM]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/etcamah">[EMA]</a>

## Efficacy: a clear PFS signal, an incomplete survival answer

Investigator-assessed median PFS was **16 versus 9.2 months**, hazard ratio (HR) **0.44 (95% CI 0.31–0.60)**. BICR supported the finding: **19.3 versus 11.5 months**, HR **0.43 (0.29–0.63)**. These are comparisons after molecular detection, not from the start of first-line treatment. They show that the switch delayed the trial-defined progression event; they do not by themselves establish that patients live longer. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2502929">[NEJM]</a>

| Key measure | Camizestrant switch versus continued AI |
|---|---|
| Investigator PFS | 16 versus 9.2 months; HR 0.44 (95% CI 0.31–0.60) |
| BICR PFS | 19.3 versus 11.5 months; HR 0.43 (0.29–0.63) |
| Later PFS2 | 25.7 versus 19.1 months; HR 0.63 (0.46–0.86) |
| Interim OS | HR 0.91 (0.48–1.73) |
| Later OS | HR 0.87 (0.57–1.30); immature |
| Time to QoL deterioration | 21.0 versus 6.4 months; HR 0.54 |

PFS2 captures progression on the next line or death and therefore reaches beyond the initial treatment period. At the interim analysis it had **85 events** and did not reach its prespecified significance threshold. A later prespecified analysis reported a favourable PFS2 estimate. This is encouraging, but the FDA reviewers did not regard PFS2 as adequate evidence of clinical benefit for the timing strategy. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2502929">[NEJM]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/fda-odac-vote-on-camizestrant-breast-cancer.html">[AstraZeneca]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/192245/download">[FDA reviewers]</a>

OS remained immature: the interim estimate was based on **39 deaths**. Both OS intervals are wide and include no difference. They cannot establish a survival advantage or exclude an important difference in either direction. The published QoL analysis favoured the switch for time to deterioration in global health status, but FDA reviewers did not accept the patient-reported results as evidence of clinical benefit for this decision. Discuss the finding alongside that limitation. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2502929">[NEJM]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/fda-odac-vote-on-camizestrant-breast-cancer.html">[AstraZeneca]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/192245/download">[FDA reviewers]</a>

## Toxicity and practical safety

In the safety population, any-grade neutropenia occurred in **54.8% versus 44.5%**, and grade **≥3** neutropenia in **45.2% versus 34.2%**. Grade **≥3** adverse events overall were **60.0% versus 45.8%**, mainly haematologic. Continue blood-count surveillance and assess fever or other symptoms promptly; the continued CDK4/6 inhibitor remains relevant to marrow toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2502929">[NEJM]</a>

Photopsia occurred in **20.0% versus 7.7%**, mostly grade **1**, with no discontinuations and no structural eye effects reported. Explain visual symptoms before treatment and evaluate a change in vision rather than assuming all symptoms are harmless. Bradycardia and sinus bradycardia were reported in **5.2%** and **2.6%**, respectively, in the camizestrant arm; none was grade **≥3** or led to discontinuation. Ask about dizziness, syncope and a slow pulse. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2502929">[NEJM]</a>

The FDA label carries a **boxed warning for arrhythmia risk from QTc prolongation when used with QTc-prolonging drugs**; bradycardia and embryo-fetal toxicity are also warnings. Review the complete medication list, including supportive medicines. Take particular care with ribociclib and other QT-prolonging drugs. Baseline and follow-up ECG and heart-rate assessment are reasonable clinical practice, with monitoring tailored to risk and applicable product information. Review reproductive precautions. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a>

## The regulatory split

The FDA asked its oncology advisory committee whether switching at mutation detection, before radiographic progression, had demonstrated clinically meaningful benefit. On **30 Apr 2026**, the vote was **3 yes, 6 no**. Reviewers questioned the meaning of PFS measured from molecular detection, whether switching early improves long-term outcomes, and whether PFS2, patient reports or time without chemotherapy or antibody-drug conjugates could settle that question. They also weighed cardiac toxicity against uncertain benefit. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/192244/download">[ODAC question]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/192245/download">[FDA reviewers]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/fda-odac-vote-on-camizestrant-breast-cancer.html">[AstraZeneca]</a>

The CHMP gave a positive opinion on **21 May 2026** and EU authorisation followed on **20 Jul 2026**. The FDA nevertheless granted accelerated approval on **4 Sep 2026**, based on PFS from mutation detection, with continued approval contingent on confirmatory evidence. Its postmarketing requirements include final OS from SERENA-6 and a new trial reporting time to strategy failure and OS. The letter specifies final reports in **12/2028** and **09/2032**, respectively. These different decisions reflect the weight placed on the available endpoint and residual uncertainty; they do not make the missing survival answer disappear. <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/smop-initial/chmp-summary-positive-opinion-etcamah_en.pdf">[CHMP]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/etcamah">[EMA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/220359Orig1s000ltr.pdf">[FDA letter]</a>

## Who fits the label

The FDA indication covers adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, with an ESR1 mutation detected by an FDA-authorized test during AI plus CDK4/6 inhibitor therapy. The authorised partners are abemaciclib, palbociclib and ribociclib. Guardant360 CDx is the FDA-approved blood-based companion diagnostic. Camizestrant is **75 mg orally once daily**, with or without food; continue the CDK4/6 inhibitor at the dose used when the mutation was detected. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-camizestrant-cdk46-inhibitor-esr1-mutated-hr-positive-her2-negative">[FDA]</a>

The EU wording specifies detection of ESR1 mutation **without disease progression during first-line endocrine therapy plus CDK4/6 inhibition**. It requires LHRH agonist or antagonist treatment for pre/perimenopausal women and men. Check the relevant local product information and assay requirements: trial eligibility and regional label wording are related but not interchangeable. <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/etcamah">[EMA]</a>

## What it changes in practice

For a patient fitting the studied setting, serial ctDNA surveillance can identify an opportunity to change endocrine therapy before scans progress. The case for adopting it is the consistent PFS result, a biologically directed intervention and the possibility of preserving disease control while continuing the established CDK4/6 partner. A testing pathway needs reliable sample collection, result review, an action plan and a conversation before testing about what a positive result would mean.

The counter-argument is substantial: continuing effective treatment until radiological progression, then switching to an oral SERD, might yield a similar long-term outcome while deferring drug exposure and testing costs. SERENA-6 does not establish that these strategies have the same outcome, nor that the early switch is superior for survival. PFS measured from mutation detection cannot resolve that timing question alone. Explain the uncertainty and incorporate the patient's priorities, access and cardiac risk. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/192245/download">[FDA reviewers]</a>

## India and resource-limited settings

Serial ctDNA testing adds repeated costs and depends on access to an appropriate assay, timely reporting and clinical follow-through. Drug access, ECG facilities and the ability to assess bradycardia and drug interactions also influence feasibility. A positive result is useful only if a safe and affordable treatment pathway follows it.

Continuing AI plus CDK4/6 inhibition until clinical or radiological progression remains a sound standard when the molecular-switch pathway is unavailable or after an informed decision against it. The evidence does not require a patient to undertake testing they cannot access or afford. **We did not verify CDSCO approval or an Indian price** for camizestrant. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/192245/download">[FDA reviewers]</a>

## Open questions

- Does an early molecular switch improve mature OS or time to strategy failure compared with a later switch?
- Which patients benefit most, and how should a negative or uncertain ctDNA result inform repeat testing?
- How should cardiac monitoring and drug interactions be managed in routine practice, especially with ribociclib?
- Can the testing and treatment pathway be delivered equitably where resources are constrained?

## Bottom line

SERENA-6 makes molecular progression an actionable treatment point for a defined population and shows a substantial PFS improvement. The FDA and EU authorisations provide routes to use it, but **OS is immature and the long-term advantage of switching before radiological progression is unproven**. Offer a balanced discussion of the potential delay in progression, toxicity, testing burden and the option to continue current treatment.
