## Background
- Desmoid tumours (aggressive fibromatosis): locally aggressive, non-metastasising; driven by Wnt/β-catenin (CTNNB1 or APC/FAP).
- Global consensus: **active surveillance first**; treat only progressive or symptomatic disease. Surgery is no longer default.
- Pre-DeFi systemic options: sorafenib (phase 3, Gounder NEJM 2018), low-dose methotrexate–vinblastine, NSAIDs/tamoxifen (weak evidence), doxorubicin-based chemotherapy.

## Trial design
Phase 3, double-blind; n=142 with progressing desmoids (≥20% growth within 12 months). Nirogacestat 150 mg twice daily vs placebo. Primary endpoint: PFS.

## Results
- PFS: HR **0.29** (95% CI 0.15–0.55; p<0.001). <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210140">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- 2-year event-free probability: **76% vs 44%**.
- ORR **41% vs 8%**; complete response 7% vs 0%.
- Improvements in pain, desmoid-specific symptoms and quality of life.

## Safety
Diarrhoea (most common), nausea, fatigue, hypophosphataemia, maculopapular rash. **Ovarian dysfunction in ~75% of women of reproductive potential** — mostly reversible, but counsel on fertility and contraception. Mostly grade 1–2.

## Place in therapy
NCCN lists nirogacestat as a preferred systemic option for progressive/symptomatic desmoids. Longer follow-up and the optimal duration of therapy remain open questions.

## How nirogacestat works
γ-Secretase cleaves the Notch receptor; blocking it reduces Notch signalling, which interacts with the Wnt/β-catenin pathway that drives desmoid growth. The same mechanism explains several class effects (diarrhoea, skin changes, ovarian effects).

## Endpoints and who was enrolled
- Eligible patients had **radiographic progression of ≥20% within 12 months** before entry — the trial did not test treatment of stable, asymptomatic tumours.
- Many had already received surgery, radiotherapy or systemic therapy; both treatment-naïve and pre-treated patients were included.
- Progression was assessed by blinded central review (RECIST 1.1), with clinical progression also counted as an event.
- Patient-reported outcomes (pain, desmoid symptoms, physical functioning) favoured nirogacestat. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2210140">[NEJM 2023]</a>

## Practical prescribing
- Dose: 150 mg orally twice daily, continuous; dose reduction to 100 mg twice daily is used for toxicity (FDA label). <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-nirogacestat-desmoid-tumors">[FDA]</a>
- Check phosphate and potassium; replace as needed.
- Counsel women about menstrual changes and fertility; ovarian dysfunction resolved in most women in the trial after stopping or on continued therapy, but long-term effects are unknown.
- Avoid strong CYP3A inhibitors/inducers (and grapefruit) — check drug interactions.
- Responses are slow: tumour shrinkage often takes months, and MRI changes (T2 signal reduction) can precede size change.

## How other options compare
- **Sorafenib** (phase 3 vs placebo): improved PFS in progressive or symptomatic desmoids; cheaper and available as a generic in India. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1805052">[NEJM 2018]</a>
- **Methotrexate + vinblastine/vinorelbine:** low-dose, well tolerated, long experience — useful in children and in pregnancy-planning patients.
- **Surgery:** reserved for selected cases; recurrence is common and morbidity can exceed that of the tumour.
- **Pregnancy-associated desmoids** often stabilise or regress after delivery — another reason to observe first.
