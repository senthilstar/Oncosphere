## Why this matters

PSMAddition is the first phase 3 radioligand trial in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer. It moves Pluvicto from its established castration-resistant setting into an earlier metastatic treatment decision, alongside androgen deprivation and an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ARPI). The question is whether delivering targeted radiation before resistance develops can delay progression enough to justify added treatment burden. <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)04871-9/fulltext">[ESMO]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/fda-approves-pluvicto-psma-metastatic-hormone-sensitive-prostate-cancer-mhspc-advancing-potential-new-standard-care-across-metastatic-disease">[Novartis]</a>

The FDA uses **metastatic androgen pathway modulation-naive or -sensitive (mAPMN/S)** for the approved population, previously called metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC). The terminology describes disease still untreated by, or sensitive to, androgen pathway modulation; it should not be confused with castration-resistant disease. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-lutetium-lu-177-vipivotide-tetraxetan-androgen-receptor-pathway-inhibitor-therapy">[FDA]</a>

## How Lu-PSMA-617 works

Lu-PSMA-617 carries a beta-emitting radioactive payload to cells expressing prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA). The ligand binds the target and brings radiation close to tumour deposits. Normal tissues can also take up or be exposed to radiation, so targeting does not eliminate toxicity.

PSMA PET helps identify patients whose metastases express the target. A positive scan supports a biological rationale for treatment, but uptake alone cannot promise a response or guarantee that every site of disease is adequately targeted. Imaging must be interpreted in the context of the full disease picture.

## PSMAddition design

The randomised, open-label phase 3 superiority trial enrolled patients at **169 sites in 20 countries**. Participants had metastatic disease on conventional imaging and were treatment-naive or minimally treated, with at most **45 days** of prior treatment. Central review of PSMA-11 PET required at least one PSMA-positive metastasis. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42561994/">[Lancet]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)04871-9/fulltext">[ESMO]</a>

Assignment was **1:1** to Lu-PSMA-617 plus androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) and investigator-choice ARPI, or ADT plus ARPI alone. Lu-PSMA-617 was given intravenously at **7.4 GBq every 6 weeks for up to 6 cycles**. Randomisation accounted for disease volume, age, and previous or planned primary-tumour treatment. Control patients could cross over after centrally confirmed radiographic progression. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42561994/">[Lancet]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)04871-9/fulltext">[ESMO]</a>

The primary endpoint was radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS), assessed centrally using the trial's prostate cancer criteria, or death. Overall survival (OS), response, safety and quality of life were secondary outcomes. Central imaging review helps limit assessment bias, but open-label treatment can still affect symptom reporting and subsequent care. The trial compares treatment strategies: it does not isolate an ARPI choice or establish that ADT can be omitted. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-lutetium-lu-177-vipivotide-tetraxetan-androgen-receptor-pathway-inhibitor-therapy">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)04871-9/fulltext">[ESMO]</a>

## Who was enrolled

Of **1144** randomised patients, **572** entered each group. **50%** had de novo metastatic disease and **68%** had high-volume disease. Median age was **68.0 years**. These characteristics matter when applying the result to someone with recurrent or lower-volume disease: the overall trial result is informative, but a subgroup estimate cannot settle the benefit for every presentation. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42561994/">[Lancet]</a>

## Efficacy: rPFS and immature OS

At the primary rPFS analysis, events occurred in **139/572 (24.3%) versus 172/572 (30.1%)**. Median rPFS was not reached in either group. The HR was **0.72 (95% CI 0.58–0.90; p=0.002)** at an information fraction of **74.4%**. This is evidence of delayed radiographic progression or death, not a direct estimate of an individual patient's added time without progression. <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)04871-9/fulltext">[ESMO]</a>

Interim OS had **85 versus 99 deaths**, HR **0.84 (95% CI 0.63–1.13)**, with **47.3%** information. The interval crosses the no-difference line and OS remains immature. Among patients with measurable disease, objective response was **85.3% versus 80.8%**. Response describes tumour shrinkage in that subset; it is not interchangeable with survival. <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)04871-9/fulltext">[ESMO]</a>

A later **sponsor-reported, not yet peer-reviewed** update gave rPFS HR **0.67 (95% CI 0.55–0.82)** and OS HR **0.80 (95% CI 0.63–1.01)**. The latter interval still crosses the no-difference line. Await the final OS analysis and a full peer-reviewed account before treating the update as definitive survival evidence. <a class="cite" href="https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/fda-approves-pluvicto-psma-metastatic-hormone-sensitive-prostate-cancer-mhspc-advancing-potential-new-standard-care-across-metastatic-disease">[Novartis]</a>

Crossover after progression may complicate the OS comparison: subsequent radioligand treatment can reduce the contrast between initial strategies. It does not prove that early treatment improves survival, nor can its effect be quantified from the information presented here. Subsequent therapies and follow-up will also matter.

## Toxicity and practical safety

Grade **≥3 adverse events were 50.7% versus 43.0%**, and serious adverse events **31.9% versus 28.7%**. Dry mouth was common: grade **1** occurred in **41.0% versus 3.4%**, and grade **2** in **4.8% versus 0.4%**. The primary report described dry mouth as low grade and not serious, but persistent symptoms can still affect eating and daily life. Grade **≥3 cytopenias were 14.4% versus 5.0%**. Time to quality-of-life worsening did not differ meaningfully on the reported measures. <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(25)04871-9/fulltext">[ESMO]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42561994/">[Lancet]</a>

The label warns about radiation exposure, myelosuppression, renal toxicity, embryo-fetal toxicity and infertility. Check blood counts and renal function, support hydration and salivary care, and counsel patients and household contacts on the nuclear medicine team's radiation-safety instructions. Investigate symptoms and laboratory changes promptly; coordinate interruptions and supportive care with the treating team and applicable label. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-lutetium-lu-177-vipivotide-tetraxetan-androgen-receptor-pathway-inhibitor-therapy">[FDA]</a>

## The label

On **31 Jul 2026**, the FDA approved lutetium Lu 177 vipivotide tetraxetan plus ARPI for adults with PSMA-positive mAPMN/S prostate cancer. Selection uses Locametz or another approved PSMA PET product. The labelled schedule is **7.4 GBq every 6 weeks for 6 doses**, stopping earlier for progression or unacceptable toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-lutetium-lu-177-vipivotide-tetraxetan-androgen-receptor-pathway-inhibitor-therapy">[FDA]</a>

The investigator's ARPI choices included abiraterone, apalutamide, enzalutamide, darolutamide or another ARPI. Patients also received a GnRH agonist or antagonist or had undergone bilateral orchiectomy. The FDA review involved Project Orbis with the UK MHRA; the FDA decision itself does not establish approval elsewhere. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-lutetium-lu-177-vipivotide-tetraxetan-androgen-receptor-pathway-inhibitor-therapy">[FDA]</a>

## What it changes in practice

Consider the strategy for a patient with PSMA-positive metastatic disease who is fit for treatment and has adequate marrow and renal reserve. Discuss the scan, nuclear medicine visits, symptom monitoring and radiation precautions alongside the rPFS benefit. A shared decision should reflect the patient's priorities and the uncertainty around OS.

High- versus low-volume and de novo versus recurrent disease remain important questions; avoid assuming that an individual subgroup has the same precision as the overall result. Docetaxel triplets and PARP inhibitor combinations provide other intensification routes for selected patients. The [TALAPRO-3 deep dive](/talazoparib-enzalutamide-hrr-mhspc-talapro-3/) discusses an HRR-selected PARP strategy. These approaches were not directly compared in PSMAddition, so biology, fitness, toxicity, access and patient preference guide the choice.

A further sequencing question is whether early Lu-PSMA use changes the value or feasibility of treatment at castration resistance. The trial does not establish the best sequence or whether retreatment will work. Do not present early treatment as a proven OS gain.

## India and resource-limited settings

PSMA PET and Lu-177 therapy are available at many Indian nuclear medicine centres, but access is uneven. Travel, scheduling and the cost of six cycles plus PET can determine whether the strategy is feasible. Discuss the entire pathway, including monitoring and supportive care, before starting.

ADT plus an ARPI, including generic abiraterone, or docetaxel remain solid standards where appropriate. The choice should remain clinically sound and affordable for the individual. We did not verify CDSCO approval of Pluvicto for this indication or an Indian price.

## Open questions

- Will mature OS establish a survival benefit, and how will crossover and subsequent treatment affect interpretation?
- Which patients with lower-volume or recurrent disease gain most, and can imaging refine selection?
- How should radioligand treatment be sequenced with docetaxel and biomarker-directed combinations?
- Can retreatment in castration-resistant disease help after earlier exposure, and how can access improve?

## Bottom line

PSMAddition supports adding Lu-PSMA-617 to ADT and an ARPI for selected PSMA-positive metastatic hormone-sensitive disease. Radiographic progression was delayed; **OS is not yet proven**. Balance that benefit against marrow, renal and salivary toxicity, logistics and access.
