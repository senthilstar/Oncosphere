## Why this matters

Adjuvant treatment follows surgery with curative intent, when the cancer visible to the surgeon has been removed but microscopic disease may remain. ATOMIC brings immunotherapy into this setting for **stage III mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) colon cancer**. The FDA approval makes atezolizumab the first adjuvant immunotherapy in curative colon cancer, in combination with a fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin. The decision matters because treatment is offered to people who may already be cured by surgery, so both avoided recurrence and treatment burden deserve attention. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA]</a>

The trial showed a substantial improvement in disease-free survival (DFS) when atezolizumab was added to mFOLFOX6. Overall survival (OS) is still immature. A lower risk of recurrence or death at this analysis is meaningful, but it does not yet establish a survival advantage. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2507874">[NEJM]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://ascopost.com/news/march-2026/adjuvant-atezolizumab-and-mfolfox6-in-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer/">[ASCO Post]</a>

## The biology in brief

Mismatch repair normally corrects errors introduced when DNA is copied. A deficient repair system allows errors to accumulate, producing a high mutational load and tumour antigens that can be recognised by the immune system. Tumours may nevertheless suppress an immune response through the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway. Blocking that pathway can help release an existing antitumour response; atezolizumab targets PD-L1.

This biology also makes dMMR disease a distinct adjuvant problem. Its response to chemotherapy cannot simply be assumed to match that of mismatch repair-proficient (pMMR) disease. Repair deficiency and the resulting tumour biology may reduce the benefit of chemotherapy alone, while immune recognition offers another therapeutic route. That rationale supports the trial; it does not establish which individual patient needs each component or can safely omit chemotherapy.

## ATOMIC design

Alliance A021502 (ATOMIC) was a multicentre, randomised, open-label, active-controlled study after complete resection of stage III dMMR colon cancer, with no residual nodal or metastatic disease. It enrolled **711 adults and one paediatric patient**. The **712** participants were assigned **1:1**: **355** to atezolizumab plus mFOLFOX6 and **357** to mFOLFOX6 alone. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2507874">[NEJM]</a>

Both groups received mFOLFOX6 for **12 cycles**. The experimental group received atezolizumab **840 mg every 2 weeks** with chemotherapy, followed by atezolizumab alone for **6 months**. Randomisation was stratified by T stage, N stage and tumour location. The population included patients across risk groups; **53.9%** had T4, N2 or both. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopost.com/news/march-2026/adjuvant-atezolizumab-and-mfolfox6-in-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer/">[ASCO Post]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.allianceforclinicaltrialsinoncology.org/news/2026/landmark-alliance-atomic-trial-establishes-new-standard-of-care-for-patients-with-stage-iii-dmmr-colon-cancer/">[Alliance]</a>

Investigator-assessed DFS was the major efficacy outcome; OS and the adverse-event profile were secondary endpoints. Open-label treatment is relevant when interpreting investigator-assessed outcomes, although the DFS difference is substantial. The comparison tests the addition of atezolizumab to chemotherapy followed by continued atezolizumab; it cannot isolate the contribution of each treatment phase. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2507874">[NEJM]</a>

## Efficacy: DFS and an immature OS result

At a median DFS follow-up of **40.9 months**, the estimated **3-year DFS was 86.3% (95% CI 81.8–89.8) versus 76.2% (70.9–80.6)**, favouring the atezolizumab arm. The DFS hazard ratio (HR) was **0.50 (95% CI 0.35–0.73)**. Median DFS was not reached in either group. Recurrence or death occurred in **46 versus 81** patients, respectively. These estimates describe the randomised groups, not an individual patient's chance of cure. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2507874">[NEJM]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://news.gastro.org/issues/2026/march/atezolizumab-boosts-colon-cancer-disease-free-survival-study-finds/">[AGA]</a>

An exploratory analysis by chemotherapy received reported a DFS HR of **0.41 (0.27–0.64)** among patients receiving **more than 6 cycles**, and **0.97 (0.44–2.11)** among those receiving **6 cycles or fewer**. These groups were defined by treatment received, not a randomised chemotherapy-duration assignment. Patients may have stopped early for reasons related to prognosis or toxicity. The estimates therefore do not establish a minimum effective duration or prove that the drug has no benefit with shorter chemotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopost.com/news/march-2026/adjuvant-atezolizumab-and-mfolfox6-in-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer/">[ASCO Post]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://news.gastro.org/issues/2026/march/atezolizumab-boosts-colon-cancer-disease-free-survival-study-finds/">[AGA]</a>

OS had a median follow-up of **45.8 months** and **31 versus 33 deaths**. Estimated **5-year OS was 89.7% (85.2–92.9) versus 87.9% (83.1–91.4)**, HR **0.90 (0.55–1.47)**. The interval includes a range of possible effects. Many patients received immunotherapy later at recurrence, which complicates interpretation of the adjuvant strategy's effect on OS. Longer follow-up is needed; later treatment is not the same as randomised crossover. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopost.com/news/march-2026/adjuvant-atezolizumab-and-mfolfox6-in-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer/">[ASCO Post]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://news.gastro.org/issues/2026/march/atezolizumab-boosts-colon-cancer-disease-free-survival-study-finds/">[AGA]</a>

## Toxicity and practical safety

Grade **3/4 adverse events occurred in 84.1% versus 71.9%**. The regimen adds immune-related risks to the established burdens of fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin treatment. Discuss this before starting: the aim is cure, and a patient may be exposed to toxicity without being able to know whether they personally needed the additional treatment. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2507874">[NEJM]</a>

Monitor blood counts and chemotherapy-related symptoms, and maintain vigilance for immune effects during and after treatment. Check thyroid function and investigate fatigue or other symptoms that could reflect endocrine dysfunction. Diarrhoea during chemotherapy has several possible causes; persistent or severe symptoms need assessment for immune-mediated colitis as well as infection and chemotherapy toxicity. Liver test abnormalities can signal hepatitis, while cough or breathlessness can signal pneumonitis. Prompt assessment, appropriate treatment interruption and management according to the relevant product information are essential; do not assume a symptom has only one cause.

## The label

The FDA approved atezolizumab with a **fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin** as adjuvant treatment for adults and paediatric patients aged **2 years or older** with stage III dMMR colon cancer. For adults, intravenous options are **840 mg every 2 weeks, 1200 mg every 3 weeks or 1680 mg every 4 weeks**, with chemotherapy for **6 months**, then alone for **6 months** or until recurrence or unacceptable toxicity. Paediatric intravenous dosing is **10 mg/kg (maximum 840 mg) every 2 weeks** or **15 mg/kg (maximum 1200 mg) every 3 weeks**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA]</a>

A subcutaneous atezolizumab and hyaluronidase formulation is an option for adults and paediatric patients aged **12 years or older** weighing at least **40 kg**: **15 mL every 3 weeks**, containing **1875 mg atezolizumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase**. Its use is not established below those age or weight thresholds. The trial's chemotherapy backbone was mFOLFOX6; the FDA wording is broader. Check the applicable label and local availability when selecting a formulation and regimen. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA]</a>

Determine dMMR by immunohistochemistry (IHC); the VENTANA MMR RxDx Panel is an example of a test cited with the approval. Interpret the pathology result with the clinical picture and resolve an equivocal or discordant result before using it to direct treatment. <a class="cite" href="https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2026-10-09">[Roche]</a>

## What it changes in practice

Test MMR on **every resected colon cancer** and make the result visible at the adjuvant discussion. For a patient with completely resected stage III dMMR colon cancer who is fit for the regimen, discuss atezolizumab with mFOLFOX6 followed by atezolizumab alone, for **12 months** total. Explain the DFS benefit, the immature OS result, the additional visits and monitoring, and the possibility of immune toxicity. Fit, recovery from surgery and patient preferences matter.

ATOMIC does not establish this approach for pMMR tumours, stage II colon cancer or rectal cancer. Avoid carrying the result across those boundaries without evidence. The trial also does not answer whether immunotherapy alone, a shorter adjuvant approach, or neoadjuvant immunotherapy could provide comparable outcomes. These are open research questions rather than established substitutes for the studied strategy. <a class="cite" href="https://news.gastro.org/issues/2026/march/atezolizumab-boosts-colon-cancer-disease-free-survival-study-finds/">[AGA]</a>

## India and resource-limited settings

MMR IHC is relatively inexpensive and widely available, making a tissue-based testing pathway feasible in many centres. A result should lead to an action plan: appropriate adjuvant discussion for dMMR disease and assessment for possible Lynch syndrome, including germline referral and counselling where indicated. The tumour result alone does not establish an inherited diagnosis.

Access to a year of PD-L1 therapy can be a major financial and logistical obstacle. Discuss treatment costs and travel, monitoring and the patient's priorities before committing to a course. If atezolizumab is unaffordable, **FOLFOX or CAPOX remains standard** adjuvant chemotherapy; support the patient in receiving a feasible treatment plan. We did not verify CDSCO approval or an Indian price for this indication.

## Open questions

- Will mature OS show a benefit, given effective immunotherapy may also be used after recurrence?
- Can chemotherapy be shortened or omitted, and can the duration of adjuvant immunotherapy be reduced without compromising outcomes?
- Which patients benefit most, and can biomarkers refine treatment beyond dMMR status?
- How should neoadjuvant immunotherapy fit into treatment, and how can testing and treatment become more accessible?

## Bottom line

ATOMIC supports adding atezolizumab to adjuvant chemotherapy in resected stage III dMMR colon cancer, with a substantial DFS improvement. **OS remains immature**, toxicity is considerable, and the chemotherapy-duration subgroup findings are exploratory. Test the tumour, explain the trade-offs and tailor the decision to the patient and local access.
