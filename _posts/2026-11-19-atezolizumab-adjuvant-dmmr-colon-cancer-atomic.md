## Why this matters

After curative surgery, adjuvant treatment aims to eliminate disease that is not clinically visible. ATOMIC tested whether adding PD-L1 blockade to chemotherapy reduces recurrence in resected **stage III mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) colon cancer**. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a>

Disease-free survival (DFS) improved with the atezolizumab strategy; overall survival (OS) remains immature. On **8 October 2026**, the FDA approved adjuvant atezolizumab with a fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin for this population, with age and formulation restrictions described below. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a>

## The biology in brief

Mismatch repair corrects DNA copying errors. Deficiency allows mutations to accumulate and can generate tumour antigens recognised by immune cells. Atezolizumab blocks PD-L1, a pathway that can suppress the immune response. This provides a rationale for immunotherapy, but does not identify which individual can safely omit chemotherapy.

## ATOMIC design

This open-label, multicentre randomised trial enrolled **712 patients: 711 adults and one paediatric patient**, after complete resection of stage III dMMR colon cancer without residual nodal or metastatic disease. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a> Allocation was **1:1**, with **355** assigned atezolizumab plus modified FOLFOX6 (mFOLFOX6: fluorouracil, leucovorin and oxaliplatin) and **357** assigned mFOLFOX6 alone. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a>

Both arms were assigned **12 cycles** of mFOLFOX6; the experimental arm also received atezolizumab, at **840 mg every 2 weeks** for adults, continuing alone for **6 months** after chemotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a> Randomisation was stratified by T stage, N stage and tumour location; **53.9%** had T4, N2 or both. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a>

Investigator-assessed DFS was the primary endpoint; OS and safety were secondary endpoints. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> The comparison evaluates chemotherapy plus concurrent and continued atezolizumab as a package; it cannot isolate the contribution of either phase. Open-label treatment also matters when interpreting investigator-assessed outcomes.

## Efficacy: DFS and an immature OS result

At median DFS follow-up of **40.9 months**, estimated **3-year DFS was 86.3% (95% CI 81.8–89.8) versus 76.2% (95% CI 70.9–80.6)**, an absolute difference of **10.1 percentage points**. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> The DFS hazard ratio (HR) was **0.50 (95% CI 0.35–0.73)**, with **46 versus 81** recurrence or death events; median DFS was not reached in either arm. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a> These are group estimates, not an individual prediction of cure.

An analysis by chemotherapy received estimated DFS HRs of **0.41 (95% CI 0.27–0.64)** after **more than 6 cycles** and **0.97 (95% CI 0.44–2.11)** after **6 cycles or fewer**. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> Treatment duration was not randomised, and reasons for stopping may themselves relate to prognosis or toxicity. A later exploratory congress analysis used a **6-month landmark** and examined chemotherapy and atezolizumab duration; its findings cannot establish that shortening either treatment preserves benefit. <a class="cite" href="https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534%2826%2900189-4/abstract">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>

At median OS follow-up of **45.8 months**, there had been **31 versus 33 deaths**; estimated **5-year OS was 89.7% (95% CI 85.2–92.9) versus 87.9% (95% CI 83.1–91.4)**, HR **0.90 (95% CI 0.55–1.47)**. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> The interval remains wide and does not establish an OS advantage. Immunotherapy available after recurrence may complicate interpretation, but is not equivalent to randomised crossover.

## Toxicity and practical safety

Grade **3/4 adverse events occurred in 84.1% versus 71.9%** of treated patients. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> Chemotherapy toxicity and potentially persistent immune-mediated toxicity both matter when treatment is offered after apparently complete resection.

Monitor blood counts, liver tests and thyroid function, and investigate symptoms during and after treatment. Diarrhoea may reflect chemotherapy, infection or immune-mediated colitis; cough or breathlessness may indicate pneumonitis, and fatigue may signal endocrine dysfunction. Assess promptly and follow product information for interruption and management. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a>

## The label

The FDA indication covers intravenous atezolizumab with a **fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin** after surgery for stage III dMMR colon cancer in adults and children aged **2 years or older**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a> Adult intravenous doses are **840 mg every 2 weeks, 1200 mg every 3 weeks, or 1680 mg every 4 weeks**, with chemotherapy for **6 months**, then alone for **6 months** or until recurrence or unacceptable toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a> Paediatric intravenous doses are **10 mg/kg (maximum 840 mg) every 2 weeks** or **15 mg/kg (maximum 1200 mg) every 3 weeks**, on the same treatment schedule. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a>

The FDA also approved subcutaneous atezolizumab and hyaluronidase for adults and children aged **12 years or older** weighing at least **40 kg**, at **15 mL every 3 weeks** (atezolizumab **1875 mg** and hyaluronidase **30,000 units**) on the same treatment schedule. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a> ATOMIC used mFOLFOX6; the FDA chemotherapy wording is broader. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-atezolizumab-combination-chemotherapy-stage-iii-mismatch-repair-deficient-colon-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a> Check the applicable local label and formulation availability.

Use tumour mismatch repair testing to identify dMMR, and resolve equivocal or discordant results before treatment selection. Interpret immunohistochemistry alongside the clinical and pathology findings.

## What it changes in practice

Test MMR on every resected colon cancer and bring the result to the adjuvant discussion. For a fit patient with completely resected stage III dMMR colon cancer, discuss the studied mFOLFOX6 plus atezolizumab approach, followed by atezolizumab alone, for a planned **12 months** in total. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> Explain the DFS gain, uncertainty about OS, monitoring, visits and immune toxicity alongside the patient's recovery and preferences.

ATOMIC does not establish this regimen for pMMR tumours, stage II colon cancer or rectal cancer. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> Immunotherapy alone, shorter adjuvant therapy and neoadjuvant immunotherapy remain separate research questions.

## India and resource-limited settings

MMR immunohistochemistry offers a tissue-based testing pathway where available. A dMMR result should prompt both an adjuvant treatment discussion and assessment for possible Lynch syndrome, including genetic counselling and germline referral when indicated; tumour testing alone does not establish an inherited diagnosis.

A year of PD-L1 therapy can create financial and travel burdens. Discuss costs, monitoring and patient priorities before treatment. If atezolizumab is inaccessible, FOLFOX or CAPOX remains an adjuvant chemotherapy option; tailor duration and regimen to risk, fitness and local practice. CDSCO approval for this indication and an Indian price have not been verified.

An age-based exploratory ATOMIC analysis was also presented at the 2026 ESMO congress; the available trial-group summary does not provide results sufficient to change treatment selection. <a class="cite" href="https://www.allianceforclinicaltrialsinoncology.org/news/2026/alliance-research-at-esmo-2026/">[Alliance 2026]</a>

## Open questions

- Will mature OS show a benefit when effective immunotherapy can also be used after recurrence?
- Can chemotherapy or immunotherapy be shortened or omitted without compromising outcomes?
- Can biomarkers refine selection beyond dMMR status?
- How should neoadjuvant immunotherapy fit into treatment, and how can access improve?

## Bottom line

ATOMIC supports adding atezolizumab to adjuvant mFOLFOX6 for resected stage III dMMR colon cancer. DFS improved, while OS remains immature and toxicity is substantial. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13020640/">[NEJM 2026]</a> Discuss the evidence and its limits in the context of local access.
