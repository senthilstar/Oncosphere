## Why this matters

For decades, patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) unable to receive cisplatin generally proceeded directly to cystectomy without a proven perioperative systemic therapy. KEYNOTE-905 is the first phase 3 trial to show an overall survival (OS) benefit in this setting. KEYNOTE-B15 subsequently showed an advantage over neoadjuvant gemcitabine–cisplatin in cisplatin-eligible patients. These are distinct comparisons, both built around planned surgery. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2511674">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2601486">[NEJM 2026]</a>

The FDA approved the combination for cisplatin-ineligible patients on **21 November 2025**, then broadened the indication on **10 July 2026**. European approval followed in **October 2026**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-or-pembrolizumab-and-berahyaluronidase-alfa-pmph-each-enfortumab-vedotin">[FDA 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.merck.com/news/european-commission-approves-keytruda-pembrolizumab-plus-padcev-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-as-perioperative-treatment-for-adults-with-resectable-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer-mibc/">[Sponsor-reported 2026]</a>

## How the combination works

Enfortumab vedotin (EV) is an antibody–drug conjugate targeting nectin-4 and carrying the microtubule-disrupting payload MMAE. Pembrolizumab blocks PD-1, releasing an immune checkpoint. The combination brings targeted cytotoxic delivery and immune activation together around surgery; activity does not eliminate the need to assess toxicity or resectability. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a>

## KEYNOTE-905/EV-303 design

This open-label, randomised phase 3 trial enrolled candidates for radical cystectomy and pelvic lymph-node dissection who were cisplatin-ineligible or declined cisplatin. Eligible clinical stages were **cT2–T4aN0M0 or T1–T4aN1M0**. The ineligible proportion was **84% versus 80%** in the EV plus pembrolizumab and surgery-alone groups; the remainder declined cisplatin. Renal function was among the criteria used to determine ineligibility. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.16_suppl.4613">[JCO 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://ascopost.com/articles/2026/03-march/perioperative-enfortumab-vedotin-plus-pembrolizumab-in-cisplatin-ineligible-bladder-cancer/">[ASCO Post 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03924895">[ClinicalTrials.gov]</a>

The reported comparison assigned **170** patients to neoadjuvant EV plus pembrolizumab, cystectomy, and adjuvant EV plus pembrolizumab followed by pembrolizumab alone, versus **174** to immediate surgery. Surgery followed **3 cycles** of combination treatment; the protocol specified **9 EV cycles** and **17 pembrolizumab cycles** in total. A separate perioperative pembrolizumab-alone arm was included; its results are not covered here. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2511674">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03924895">[ClinicalTrials.gov]</a>

Event-free survival (EFS), assessed by blinded independent central review (BICR), was the primary endpoint; OS was an additional endpoint. Central review helps assess events consistently, but the open-label design could influence treatment decisions and symptom reporting. This comparison evaluates the complete perioperative strategy against surgery alone. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a>

## KEYNOTE-905 results

At median follow-up of **25.6 months**, estimated **2-year EFS was 74.7% versus 39.4%**, HR **0.40 (95% CI 0.28–0.57)**. Median EFS was not reached versus **15.7 months**. Estimated **2-year OS was 79.7% versus 63.1%**, HR **0.50 (95% CI 0.33–0.74)**; median OS was not reached versus **41.7 months**. The hazard ratios describe relative event rates over follow-up, not the outcome of an individual patient. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2511674">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a>

The sponsor reported EFS events in **48/170 versus 95/174** and deaths in **38/170 versus 68/174**. Pathological complete response (pCR) was **57.1% versus 8.6%**; cystectomy was performed in **87.6% versus 89.7%**. pCR is a surgical pathology measure, while EFS and OS capture later outcomes as well. <a class="cite" href="https://www.merck.com/news/fda-approves-keytruda-pembrolizumab-and-keytruda-qlex-pembrolizumab-and-berahyaluronidase-alfa-pmph-each-with-padcev-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-as-perioperative-treatment-for/">[Sponsor-reported 2025]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2511674">[NEJM 2026]</a>

**Exploratory stage analyses** suggested EFS benefit in cT2N0, HR **0.26 (0.08–0.80)**, and cT3–T4aN0, HR **0.43 (0.29–0.63)**. The N1 group contained only **7 versus 10** patients; its HR **0.35 (0.09–1.40)** is imprecise. These subgroup estimates should not be used to rank benefit by stage. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.16_suppl.4613">[JCO 2026]</a>

## KEYNOTE-905 toxicity

Grade **≥3 adverse events of any cause** occurred in **71.3% versus 45.9%**. Events led to discontinuation of any study drug in **48.5%** of the combination group. There were **2 treatment-related deaths**, from myasthenia gravis and toxic epidermal necrolysis, both during neoadjuvant treatment. These serious outcomes matter when counselling a patient who is otherwise preparing for curative surgery. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2511674">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://ascopost.com/articles/2026/03-march/perioperative-enfortumab-vedotin-plus-pembrolizumab-in-cisplatin-ineligible-bladder-cancer/">[ASCO Post 2026]</a>

EV warnings include severe skin reactions, peripheral neuropathy, hyperglycaemia, pneumonitis and ocular disorders. Pembrolizumab can cause immune-mediated injury in multiple organs. Assess skin, neurological symptoms, glucose and respiratory symptoms before and during treatment; ask about vision and eye discomfort. Investigate new symptoms promptly, follow the relevant label for holds or discontinuation, and coordinate with surgery so toxicity does not derail the planned operation. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a>

## KEYNOTE-B15/EV-304: cisplatin-eligible patients

KEYNOTE-B15 randomised **405 versus 403** cisplatin-eligible cystectomy candidates to perioperative EV plus pembrolizumab or neoadjuvant gemcitabine–cisplatin followed by surgery. The experimental arm used **4 neoadjuvant cycles**, then **5 adjuvant EV cycles** and **13 adjuvant pembrolizumab cycles**. The control received **4 cycles** of gemcitabine–cisplatin before surgery. Cystectomy rates were **86.7% versus 89.6%**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2601486">[NEJM 2026]</a>

At median **33.6 months** from randomisation to cutoff, **2-year EFS was 79.4% versus 66.2%**, HR **0.53 (95% CI 0.41–0.70)**; **2-year OS was 86.9% versus 81.3%**, HR **0.65 (95% CI 0.48–0.89)**. Median EFS was not reached versus **48.5 months**; median OS was not reached in either group. pCR was **55.8% versus 32.5%**; downstaging to less than pT2N0 was **63.7% versus 45.2%**. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2601486">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-or-pembrolizumab-and-berahyaluronidase-alfa-pmph-each-enfortumab-vedotin">[FDA 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.urologytimes.com/view/keynote-b15-data-on-enfortumab-vedotin-plus-pembrolizumab-in-mibc-published-in-nejm">[Urology Times 2026]</a>

Grade **≥3 adverse events** occurred in **75.7% versus 67.2%**; discontinuation for treatment-related adverse events was **35.2% versus 11.1%**. Treatment duration was **10.2 versus 3.5 months**. In the experimental arm **65%** started adjuvant therapy and **51%** completed it. Only **7%** of the control group received adjuvant immunotherapy; the trial therefore does not establish how the regimen compares with gemcitabine–cisplatin followed by routine risk-adapted adjuvant immunotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2601486">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.springermedicine.com/bladder-cancer/cancer-therapy/keynote-b15-ev-304-perioperative-cisplatin-eligible-mibc/53047770">[Springer Medicine 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13533555/">[The Oncologist 2026]</a>

## Regulatory status

The initial FDA indication covered adults with MIBC ineligible for cisplatin; the later approval expanded use to adults with MIBC undergoing cystectomy. Pembrolizumab or the pembrolizumab–berahyaluronidase formulation **Keytruda Qlex** may be used with EV under the applicable US label. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-or-pembrolizumab-and-berahyaluronidase-alfa-pmph-each-enfortumab-vedotin">[FDA 2026]</a>

For the initial FDA schedule, intravenous pembrolizumab **200 mg every 3 weeks** accompanies EV **1.25 mg/kg** on days **1 and 8** of each **21-day cycle** for **3 cycles** before surgery. After surgery, EV is given for **6 cycles** with pembrolizumab, followed by pembrolizumab alone; adjuvant pembrolizumab may be **200 mg every 3 weeks for 14 cycles** or **400 mg every 6 weeks for 7 cycles**. Check the current label and formulation-specific instructions for the appropriate regimen and dose modifications. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a>

The EMA CHMP issued a positive opinion on **17 September 2026** extending the Padcev indication to resectable MIBC regardless of cisplatin eligibility. The sponsor reported European Commission approval in **October 2026**. Local labels govern use. <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/smop/chmp-post-authorisation-summary-positive-opinion-padcev-vr0000336191_en.pdf">[EMA 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.merck.com/news/european-commission-approves-keytruda-pembrolizumab-plus-padcev-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-as-perioperative-treatment-for-adults-with-resectable-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer-mibc/">[Sponsor-reported 2026]</a>

## How it compares with VOLGA

[VOLGA]({{ '/durvalumab-enfortumab-vedotin-mibc-fda-approval-volga/' | relative_url }}) evaluated neoadjuvant durvalumab plus EV followed by cystectomy and adjuvant durvalumab, against surgery alone, in patients ineligible for or declining cisplatin. The FDA approved that regimen on **13 November 2026**. Its adjuvant treatment differs from the EV plus pembrolizumab strategy. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-durvalumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer">[FDA 2026]</a>

The figure places trial estimates together for orientation. Different populations, control arms, treatment schedules and follow-up make cross-trial ranking unreliable. Neither trial directly compared these regimens; choice requires consideration of eligibility, toxicity, local approval and access. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2608323">[NEJM 2026]</a>

## What it changes in practice

Start with resectability and fitness for cystectomy, not drug selection alone. Review renal function and cisplatin eligibility, baseline neuropathy, diabetes control and skin history. Explain the possibility of severe toxicity, interruption and the need for close coordination with the surgical team. Discuss the absolute trial estimates as group outcomes and acknowledge uncertainty for an individual.

Cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy remains an evidence-based choice for suitable patients, especially where the newer regimen is inaccessible or its toxicity and treatment burden are unacceptable. The trials do not isolate the contribution of neoadjuvant versus adjuvant treatment. They do not answer whether the combination improves outcomes with bladder preservation or trimodality therapy, or whether it can replace cystectomy.

## India and resource-limited settings

Drug cost and the approximately **1 year** treatment burden can make EV plus pembrolizumab difficult to deliver; travel, monitoring, supportive care and access through tertiary centres also matter. Discuss the entire course and surgery before starting. For eligible patients who cannot afford EV plus pembrolizumab, cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy remains a valid, far cheaper evidence-based standard. Cisplatin-ineligible patients without access may proceed to surgery alone or enter a trial when available. We did not verify CDSCO approval for this perioperative indication or any Indian price.

## Open questions

- Which patients need both the preoperative and postoperative components, and can treatment be safely shortened?
- How should the regimen be compared with cisplatin-based chemotherapy followed by risk-adapted adjuvant immunotherapy?
- Can treatment be integrated with bladder-preserving trimodality therapy, and which patients benefit most?
- How will longer follow-up, toxicity management and equitable access affect decisions?

## Bottom line

Perioperative EV plus pembrolizumab improved EFS and OS against surgery alone in cisplatin-ineligible or declining patients and against gemcitabine–cisplatin in cisplatin-eligible patients. Patient selection, treatment toxicity, timely cystectomy and access remain central to applying these results. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2511674">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2601486">[NEJM 2026]</a>
