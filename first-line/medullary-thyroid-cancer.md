## Before cycle 1 — decide whether treatment is needed

- Verify medullary histology with appropriate pathology and biochemical correlation. This is a C-cell malignancy; the differentiated-thyroid treatment pathway does not apply. Radioiodine has no therapeutic role.
- Map structural disease, including neck burden and distant sites. Record pain, compressive symptoms, diarrhoea, weight loss and any impending organ compromise.
- Review serial calcitonin and CEA alongside imaging. Marker doubling times inform tempo, but a rising marker alone does not establish a need to expose an otherwise well patient to chronic drug toxicity.
- Offer germline RET testing and genetic counselling to every patient. A negative inherited result does not rule out a somatic tumour alteration; arrange tumour testing when advanced disease needs a systemic decision.
- If a plasma assay is negative, consider whether low tumour shedding limits interpretation. Pursue tissue assessment where feasible rather than declaring the cancer definitively RET-negative.
- Before surgery, evaluate hereditary endocrine risks, including possible pheochromocytoma when hereditary status is unresolved. Coordinate endocrine assessment before an operative plan is finalised.
- Obtain blood pressure, ECG, electrolytes, liver and renal tests, thyroid function and nutritional assessment. Check swallowing, prior neck radiation, bleeding and wound-healing issues before choosing a multikinase drug.

## Choosing first line — indication before prescription

| Situation | Preferred approach | Alternatives / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Low-volume, asymptomatic, structurally indolent disease | Surveillance with agreed triggers | Treat a threatening focal lesion if necessary |
| Progressive or symptomatic RET-mutant disease | Selpercatinib | Confirm access, interactions and the applicable indication |
| RET-negative disease requiring systemic control | Cabozantinib or vandetanib | Choose by cardiac, gastrointestinal and local invasion risks |
| Selective RET therapy unavailable or unsuitable | Multikinase treatment | Do not infer equal tolerability from oral administration |
| Painful or threatening focal disease | Surgery, radiation or selected ablation | May accompany or postpone systemic treatment |
| Targeted choices exhausted | Trial assessment; selective salvage | Chemotherapy is a later consideration, not the default for a negative RET result |

As of November 2026, verify local approval and supply for the exact agent. A RET mutation in medullary cancer and a RET fusion in another thyroid histology are distinct treatment contexts; match the test and indication precisely.

## The evidence — selective inhibition changes the comparison

- **LIBRETTO-531:** Selpercatinib versus cabozantinib/vandetanib in RET-mutant, kinase-inhibitor-naive disease: PFS HR 0.28; PFS at 12 months 86.8% versus 65.7%. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37870969/">[N Engl J Med 2023]</a> Treatment-failure-free survival HR 0.25. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37870969/">[N Engl J Med 2023]</a>
- **EXAM survival analysis:** Cabozantinib versus placebo: OS 26.6 versus 21.1 months; HR 0.85, without statistical significance. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1093/annonc/mdx479">[Ann Oncol 2017]</a> The RET M918T subgroup finding was exploratory. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1093/annonc/mdx479">[Ann Oncol 2017]</a>
- **ZETA:** Vandetanib demonstrated a PFS benefit versus placebo. Use this as evidence for an active multikinase option, not a direct comparison with selective RET treatment. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2011.35.5040">[J Clin Oncol 2012]</a>

The randomised selective-RET study addresses patients who actually require systemic treatment. It does not support treating every molecularly positive patient immediately. The older multikinase studies also enrolled different clinical populations; avoid using their separate progression medians to choose a winner.

## Pitfalls / pearls

- **QT risk:** review the medication list, ECG and electrolyte abnormalities before vandetanib or another QT-relevant drug. Correct reversible contributors and arrange repeat monitoring. Toxicity may persist after a long-acting agent is stopped.
- **Invasive neck disease:** bleeding, fistula and wound-healing risks can change the suitability of cabozantinib. Coordinate systemic treatment with any planned procedure or local radiation.
- **Selective does not mean harmless:** monitor hypertension, liver injury, oedema, diarrhoea and respiratory symptoms during selective RET inhibition. Review drug interactions before adding antibiotics or supportive medicines.
- **Diarrhoea has competing causes:** tumour secretion, medication toxicity and infection require different responses. Assess dehydration, weight and nutritional consequences before simply escalating antidiarrhoeal treatment.
- **Local therapy has a purpose:** focal treatment may relieve bone pain or protect a threatened structure while the rest of the disease remains controlled. Do not confuse a local-control decision with proof of a survival advantage.
- **Thyroid replacement:** maintain appropriate thyroid function. Routine TSH suppression is not the systemic anticancer strategy in medullary disease.

<p class="opinion">Opinion: define the reason to start treatment in the clinical record—symptoms, measured structural growth or a threatened organ. This makes later decisions about interruption, switching and acceptable toxicity clearer.</p>

## After first line — distinguish resistance from intolerance

At progression, review imaging and symptoms together. An isolated resistant site may merit local treatment while systemic control elsewhere persists. Widespread progression on selective RET inhibition warrants repeat tissue or plasma profiling where feasible, especially when a resistance-directed trial is accessible.

If stopping for intolerance, consider whether dose adaptation or a different appropriate targeted drug can preserve benefit. Multikinase treatment remains relevant after selective therapy when safe and available. Later salvage chemotherapy has limited evidence and must be weighed against functional reserve, symptoms and patient priorities. Continue endocrine follow-up, nutrition and active symptom management regardless of the drug sequence.
