**Advanced or recurrent endometrial carcinoma — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Separate Curative Local Options from Systemic Disease

- Confirm histology and stage; review endometrioid grade, serous, clear-cell, undifferentiated, dedifferentiated, mixed carcinoma and carcinosarcoma. Uterine sarcoma requires a separate pathway.
- Distinguish unresectable advanced disease, postoperative residual disease and recurrence. Completely resected disease requires an adjuvant assessment; metastatic-trial results should not automatically determine adjuvant treatment.
- **Record:**
  - ECOG status, frailty, weight, neuropathy, prior pelvic radiation and surgical morbidity.
  - Prior platinum/checkpoint therapy, dates, best response and reason for stopping.
  - Bleeding, pelvic pain, obstruction, bowel/bladder involvement and organ-threatening metastases.
- FIGO 2023: IVA → bladder or bowel mucosal invasion; IVB → abdominal peritoneal spread beyond pelvis; IVC → distant metastases, including extra-abdominal nodes or nodes above renal vessels. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.14923">[Int J Gynaecol Obstet 2023]</a>
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; pelvic MRI when local salvage is plausible. Isolated vaginal/pelvic relapse → assess curative surgery or radiation before defaulting to indefinite systemic therapy.
- CBC, renal/liver function, electrolytes, glucose and thyroid function; address bleeding, infection, malnutrition and thromboembolism.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Separate local salvage from systemic disease.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Assess frailty and plan affordable staging.

## 2️⃣ Molecular Classification — Obtain Results That Change Treatment

- 🧬 MMR IHC: MLH1, PMS2, MSH2 and MSH6; use validated MSI testing when appropriate. MLH1/PMS2 loss → MLH1 promoter methylation testing and hereditary-risk assessment as indicated.
- **Classify using an integrated algorithm:** pathogenic POLE exonuclease-domain mutation; dMMR; p53-abnormal; or no specific molecular profile (NSMP). Resolve multiple classifiers through expert pathology review.
- POLE-mutated prognosis is especially relevant in earlier-stage disease; it does not justify withholding needed metastatic treatment. p53-abnormal biology signals aggressive disease but does not replace MMR testing.
- ER/PR → identify endocrine candidates; HER2 IHC with confirmatory ISH for equivocal results → serous-directed options. Consider testing carcinosarcoma where a matched treatment is contemplated, while recognising its distinct evidence base.
- PD-L1 is not required for routine chemo-immunotherapy selection. Autoimmune disease, transplant and previous severe immune toxicity may change checkpoint fitness.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → MMR directs checkpoint choices; HER2 and ER/PR refine them.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Prioritise dependable MMR testing when resources are limited.

## 3️⃣ Preferred First Line — Add Checkpoint Therapy to Carboplatin–Paclitaxel

**Regimen 💉**

- Carboplatin AUC 5 + paclitaxel 175 mg/m² IV day 1 every 21 days, usually six cycles in the frontline immune-combination trials. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2302312">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks during chemotherapy → 400 mg every 6 weeks for up to 14 maintenance cycles in NRG-GY018; approximately two years total. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761467s008lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Dostarlimab 500 mg IV every 3 weeks for six combination cycles → 1,000 mg every 6 weeks, up to three years total in RUBY. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/761174s009lbl.pdf">[FDA 2024]</a>
- dMMR disease → particularly large benefit supports prioritising chemo-immunotherapy when chemotherapy is indicated. pMMR → discuss a checkpoint-containing regimen with explicit benefit, toxicity and affordability counselling.

**📊 NRG-GY018 / KEYNOTE-868: Pembrolizumab + Chemotherapy**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=816 in the original report; advanced/recurrent carcinoma; separate dMMR and pMMR cohorts; comparator placebo + the same chemotherapy. Carcinosarcoma was excluded. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2302312">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- **Initial PFS analysis — dMMR:** 12-month PFS 74% vs 38%; HR 0.30 (95% CI 0.19–0.48). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2302312">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- **Initial PFS analysis — pMMR:** median PFS 13.1 vs 8.7 months; HR 0.54 (95% CI 0.41–0.71). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2302312">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs: dMMR 63.3% vs 47.2%; pMMR 55.1% vs 45.3%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2302312">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- 🫀 Updated conference OS analysis → dMMR 48-month OS 80% vs 60%, HR 0.53 (95% CI 0.32–0.89); pMMR median OS 46.9 vs 35.1 months, HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.66–1.06), not statistically significant. Subsequent checkpoint treatment complicates interpretation. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.16_suppl.5502">[J Clin Oncol 2026 abstract]</a>
- Exploratory pMMR ORR 72.3% vs 59.0% in the later analysis; subsequent checkpoint therapy affects OS interpretation. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03566-1">[Nat Med 2025]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → dMMR derives substantial benefit from checkpoint addition.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Confirm induction and maintenance access before starting.

## 4️⃣ Preferred Therapy Unsuitable — Choose an Alternative

- 💊 If checkpoint therapy is contraindicated or inaccessible → carboplatin–paclitaxel; low-volume indolent receptor-positive disease → consider endocrine therapy.
- Dostarlimab–chemotherapy is an alternative preferred checkpoint regimen when suitable and available.

**📊 RUBY: Dostarlimab + Chemotherapy**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=494; primary advanced or first recurrent endometrial cancer, including eligible carcinosarcoma; comparator placebo–carboplatin–paclitaxel → placebo continuation. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2216334">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- **Initial dMMR/MSI-H analysis:** 24-month PFS 61.4% vs 15.7%; HR 0.28 (95% CI 0.16–0.50). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2216334">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- **Second interim OS analysis:** overall median OS 44.6 vs 28.2 months; HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.54–0.89). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.05.546">[Ann Oncol 2024]</a>
- **Molecular subgroups:** dMMR/MSI-H OS HR 0.32 (95% CI 0.17–0.63); pMMR/MSS OS HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.60–1.04). The pMMR subgroup interval includes no effect; avoid asserting definitive subgroup OS superiority. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.05.546">[Ann Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Initial grade ≥3 AEs 70.5% vs 59.8%; include marrow suppression, neuropathy and immune injury in counselling. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2216334">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- Updated dMMR follow-up → median PFS and OS not reached with dostarlimab; long-term estimates remain exploratory. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42215378/">[Gynecol Oncol 2026]</a>
- Plan maintenance access before induction; no progression and acceptable tolerance → continue the selected antibody. Progression → reassess the next line rather than calling it maintenance.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Select the alternative according to contraindications and disease tempo.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Generic chemotherapy or endocrine therapy can improve affordability.

## 5️⃣ Maintenance — Match the Regimen to MMR and Fitness

**📊 AtTEnd: Atezolizumab + Chemotherapy**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=551 randomized; advanced/recurrent carcinoma or carcinosarcoma; atezolizumab 1,200 mg IV every 3 weeks + carboplatin AUC 5–6/paclitaxel 175 mg/m² for 6–8 cycles → antibody continuation; comparator placebo pathway. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(24)00334-6">[Lancet Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⏱️ **Initial analysis — dMMR:** PFS not estimable vs 6.9 months; HR 0.36 (95% CI 0.23–0.57). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(24)00334-6">[Lancet Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⏱️ **Overall:** PFS 10.1 vs 8.9 months; HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.61–0.91). Interim OS 38.7 vs 30.2 months; HR 0.82 (95% CI 0.63–1.07) did not cross the prespecified significance boundary. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(24)00334-6">[Lancet Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 neutropenia 27% vs 28%; treatment-related serious AEs 13% vs 3%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(24)00334-6">[Lancet Oncol 2024]</a>

**📊 DUO-E: Durvalumab ± Olaparib Maintenance**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=718; control chemotherapy → placebo; chemotherapy–durvalumab → durvalumab; or chemotherapy–durvalumab → durvalumab–olaparib. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02132">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- **Regimen:** carboplatin–paclitaxel for six cycles; durvalumab 1,120 mg IV every 3 weeks during chemotherapy → 1,500 mg every 4 weeks; olaparib 300 mg orally twice daily starts in maintenance in the relevant arm. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02132">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- **Primary overall PFS:** durvalumab 10.2 vs 9.6 months; HR 0.71 (95% CI 0.57–0.89); durvalumab–olaparib 15.1 vs 9.6 months; HR 0.55 (95% CI 0.43–0.69). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02132">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs 56.4% control, 54.9% durvalumab, 67.2% durvalumab–olaparib. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02132">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- 🧬 dMMR → durvalumab PFS HR 0.42 (95% CI 0.22–0.80); pMMR → durvalumab–olaparib PFS 15.0 vs 9.7 months, HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.44–0.73). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02132">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- US label → durvalumab plus chemotherapy then durvalumab for dMMR; EU label → durvalumab–olaparib maintenance for pMMR after induction without progression. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-durvalumab-chemotherapy-mismatch-repair-deficient-primary-advanced-or-recurrent">[FDA 2024]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/imfinzi-epar-product-information_en.pdf">[EMA 2026]</a>
- These comparisons were against control; they do not establish a universal incremental benefit from adding olaparib to any checkpoint regimen. Verify the exact MMR-specific local indication; do not exchange maintenance components between trials.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Continue only the maintenance components studied in the chosen regimen.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Account for monitoring and maintenance costs.

## 6️⃣ Progression — Sequence by Prior Checkpoint Exposure

- Reassess histology, MMR, ER/PR, HER2, tempo and whether focal salvage is possible. A long treatment-free interval differs from progression on active platinum/checkpoint therapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.22.02152">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>
- dMMR and checkpoint-naive after platinum → pembrolizumab or dostarlimab monotherapy under the applicable indication; avoid automatically adding lenvatinib when checkpoint monotherapy is suitable.
- pMMR and checkpoint-naive after platinum → pembrolizumab–lenvatinib when fit and accessible.
- Progression during frontline checkpoint therapy → trial preferred where feasible; endocrine therapy, HER2-directed treatment, chemotherapy or local therapy according to biology. KEYNOTE-775 did not establish pembrolizumab–lenvatinib efficacy after frontline checkpoint resistance. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.22.02152">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>

**📊 KEYNOTE-775 / Study 309: Pembrolizumab + Lenvatinib After Platinum**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=827; previously treated advanced carcinoma; no prior PD-1/VEGF-directed therapy; comparator doxorubicin or weekly paclitaxel. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.22.02152">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>
- **Regimen:** pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks, up to 35 cycles, + lenvatinib 20 mg orally daily. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.22.02152">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>
- 📈 **Final prespecified analysis — pMMR:** OS 18.0 vs 12.2 months; HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.58–0.83); PFS 6.7 vs 3.8 months; HR 0.60 (95% CI 0.50–0.72); ORR 32.4% vs 15.1%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.22.02152">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Overall grade ≥3 treatment-emergent AEs 90.1% vs 73.7%; substantial dose adaptation is usual. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.22.02152">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Prior checkpoint exposure changes the evidence for subsequent therapy.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Discuss treatment burden and access at progression.

## 7️⃣ HER2-positive Serous Carcinoma — Consider Trastuzumab with the Doublet

**📊 NCT01367002: Carboplatin–Paclitaxel ± Trastuzumab**

- **Study design:** randomized phase 2; n=61 enrolled, 58 evaluable; stage III–IV or recurrent HER2-positive uterine serous carcinoma; HER2 IHC 3+ or IHC 2+/FISH-positive. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-20-0953">[Clin Cancer Res 2020]</a>
- **Regimen:** carboplatin AUC 5 + paclitaxel 175 mg/m² IV every 3 weeks for six cycles; trastuzumab 8 mg/kg IV loading → 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks, continued until progression/toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-20-0953">[Clin Cancer Res 2020]</a>
- ⏱️ **Updated analysis:** PFS 12.9 vs 8.0 months; HR 0.46 (90% CI 0.28–0.76); OS 29.6 vs 24.4 months; HR 0.58 (90% CI 0.34–0.99). These are reported 90%, not 95%, intervals. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-20-0953">[Clin Cancer Res 2020]</a>
- Primary advanced stage III–IV subgroup → PFS 17.7 vs 9.3 months; subgroup estimate is exploratory. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-20-0953">[Clin Cancer Res 2020]</a>
- Baseline and serial LVEF; review cardiac history. Do not automatically add trastuzumab on top of chemo-immunotherapy without supporting evidence for that combination.
- Later-line HER2-directed ADC treatment may be relevant under a tumour-agnostic/local indication or trial; reassess expression, pulmonary risk and access.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → HER2-positive serous disease warrants a trastuzumab discussion.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Consider quality-assured biosimilars where available.

## 8️⃣ ER/PR-positive Indolent Disease — Use Endocrine Therapy When Time Permits

- Low-grade, receptor-positive, low-volume endometrioid carcinoma without visceral crisis → endocrine therapy can defer cytotoxic treatment.
- Letrozole 2.5 mg orally daily; megestrol acetate or medroxyprogesterone acetate are alternatives, with thrombosis, weight gain and metabolic risks considered. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ygyno.2024.12.003">[Gynecol Oncol 2025]</a>
- Letrozole + everolimus 10 mg orally daily has phase 2 evidence; monitor stomatitis, glucose/lipids, cytopenias and pneumonitis. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2014.58.3401">[J Clin Oncol 2015]</a>
- Letrozole + palbociclib 125 mg orally daily on days 1–21 every 28 days is a selected combination; monitor CBC and reduce for cytopenias. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ygyno.2024.12.003">[Gynecol Oncol 2025]</a>

**📊 GOG-119: Tamoxifen + Intermittent Medroxyprogesterone**

- Phase 2; 58 eligible patients with advanced or recurrent carcinoma → continuous tamoxifen 40 mg/day plus medroxyprogesterone 200 mg/day on alternating weeks; ORR 33% (95% CI 21–46%), PFS 3 months, OS 13 months. Older population was not uniformly receptor-selected. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14751130/">[Gynecol Oncol 2004]</a>

**📊 PALEO / ENGOT-EN3: Letrozole ± Palbociclib**

- **Study design:** randomized, double-blind phase 2; n=77 randomized, 73 treated; ER-positive advanced/recurrent endometrioid carcinoma; letrozole 2.5 mg orally daily with palbociclib or placebo. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ygyno.2024.12.003">[Gynecol Oncol 2025]</a>
- ⏱️ **Published analysis:** PFS 8.3 vs 3.1 months; the reported 12-month landmark PFS HR was 0.57 (95% CI 0.32–0.99). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ygyno.2024.12.003">[Gynecol Oncol 2025]</a>
- 🎯 ORR 9% vs 16%; OS HR 1.15 (95% CI 0.58–2.26). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ygyno.2024.12.003">[Gynecol Oncol 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs 67% vs 30%; neutropenia 44% vs 0%. Monitor CBC; phase 2 evidence does not establish equivalence to frontline chemo-immunotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ygyno.2024.12.003">[Gynecol Oncol 2025]</a>
- Review endocrine combinations as selected/off-label options according to jurisdiction. Rapid progression or threatened organ function → choose a treatment with a more suitable response profile.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Indolent receptor-positive disease can justify endocrine treatment first.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Oral endocrine options may reduce treatment burden and cost.

## 9️⃣ Historical and Niche Regimens — Interpret Their Comparators

**📊 GOG-209: Carboplatin–Paclitaxel versus TAP**

- **Study design:** phase 3 noninferiority; n=1,381; advanced/recurrent carcinoma; carboplatin AUC 6 + paclitaxel 175 mg/m² IV every 3 weeks for up to seven cycles versus paclitaxel–doxorubicin–cisplatin with growth-factor support. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.20.01076">[J Clin Oncol 2020]</a>
- 📈 **Final analysis:** OS 37 vs 41 months; HR 1.002 (95% CI 0.88–1.15); PFS 13 vs 14 months; HR 1.032 (90% CI 0.93–1.15). ORR 52% in both arms among patients with measurable disease; grade ≥3 neutropenia 80% vs 52%. Noninferiority and practical tolerability established the doublet backbone. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.20.01076">[J Clin Oncol 2020]</a>
- Its schedule and population differ from modern six-cycle immune-combination protocols; historical OS medians cannot rank the regimens.
- Neuropathy/frailty → assess function, consider dose modification or a less intensive regimen. Generic chemotherapy remains useful when the checkpoint course cannot be funded.

- GOG-86P → chemotherapy–bevacizumab had no significant PFS improvement versus a historical chemotherapy control; an OS signal is hypothesis-generating. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29804638/">[Gynecol Oncol 2018]</a>

**📊 RUBY part 2: Dostarlimab–Chemotherapy → Dostarlimab–Niraparib**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=291; dostarlimab–chemotherapy → dostarlimab–niraparib vs chemotherapy → placebo. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42391799/">[Int J Gynecol Cancer 2026]</a>
- PFS overall 14.5 vs 8.3 months, HR 0.60 (95% CI 0.43–0.82); pMMR 14.3 vs 8.3 months, HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.44–0.91). <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42391799/">[Int J Gynecol Cancer 2026]</a>
- OS HR 1.20 (95% CI 0.81–1.78) at later follow-up. The control did not isolate the contribution of niraparib. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42391799/">[Int J Gynecol Cancer 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events 70.7% vs 37.5%. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42391799/">[Int J Gynecol Cancer 2026]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Historical controls and combination designs limit causal interpretation.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Use a sustainable chemotherapy plan when newer therapy is inaccessible.

## 🔟 Special Situations — Assess Surgery and Local Salvage

- Assess cytoreduction → aim for no visible residual disease when achievable with acceptable morbidity. Initially unresectable disease → reassess surgery or local treatment after systemic response.
- Selected oligometastatic disease → multidisciplinary review for resection or ablative radiotherapy.
- Long platinum-free interval and previous meaningful response → selected platinum rechallenge; there is no universally validated endometrial equivalent of an ovarian platinum-sensitivity rule.
- Doxorubicin, weekly paclitaxel or another appropriate single agent → modest salvage options, selected by prior exposure, neuropathy and cardiac/marrow reserve.
- Isolated vaginal recurrence without previous radiation → definitive radiation/brachytherapy evaluation. Previously irradiated central relapse → specialist surgical or reirradiation review.
- Pelvic bleeding → haemostatic radiation and transfusion/iron support when indicated; obstruction → decompression, stoma or surgery according to goals and feasibility.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Aim for complete visible cytoreduction only when feasible and safe.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Coordinate local treatment with travel and cost considerations.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Special Populations — Adapt for Histology, Frailty and Immune Risk

- Carcinosarcoma → carboplatin–paclitaxel remains an important backbone; RUBY and AtTEnd included eligible patients, whereas NRG-GY018 did not. Avoid treating subgroup inclusion as a separate powered comparison.
- Clear-cell/serous/p53-abnormal disease → avoid relying on endocrine therapy solely because a receptor stain is positive when clinical behaviour is aggressive.
- Transplant or severe active autoimmune illness → multidisciplinary checkpoint risk assessment; cytotoxic, endocrine or targeted alternatives may be preferable.
- Hereditary MMR abnormality → genetic counselling and family testing as indicated. A tumour treatment result and a germline diagnosis are different findings.
- Poor performance status → manage reversible anaemia, pain, infection and obstruction; assess whether anticancer therapy can realistically improve function.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ → Histology and immune risk modify systemic treatment choices.
- 2️⃣ 💰 → Tailor intensity to functional reserve and resources.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Follow-up and India Practice — Preserve Function and Affordable Continuation

- Review CBC/renal/liver tests before chemotherapy; monitor neuropathy, falls and daily function. Restage according to clinical tempo; NRG-GY018 assessed imaging every 9 weeks for the first 9 months, then every 12 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/761467s008lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Checkpoint therapy → thyroid/metabolic surveillance and prompt assessment of diarrhoea, breathlessness, jaundice or severe fatigue. Hold/discontinue according to immune-toxicity guidance; do not dose-reduce the antibody.
- Lenvatinib → monitor BP after 1 week, every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then at least monthly; monitor proteinuria. For toxicity, interrupt and reduce from 20 → 14 → 10 → 8 mg daily as indicated. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/206947s031lbl.pdf">[FDA 2024]</a>
- Olaparib → CBC, renal/interactions review and persistent-cytopenia evaluation.
- Integrate palliative care, pain control, nutrition and psychosocial support throughout treatment.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ MMR testing changes the first-line discussion; integrated POLE/p53/NSMP classification adds context.
- 2️⃣ Keep induction, maintenance cap and progression sequence tied to the chosen regimen.
- 3️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Prioritise dependable MMR IHC, then focused ER/PR and HER2 testing. Generic carboplatin–paclitaxel and selected endocrine therapy may be sustainable; quality-assured trastuzumab biosimilars can reduce HER2-pathway costs.
- 4️⃣ Verify current CDSCO labels, supply and PM-JAY/state-scheme coverage for the entire course, including maintenance, laboratory tests and travel. Overseas approval does not establish Indian access.
