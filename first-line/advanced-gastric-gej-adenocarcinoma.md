**Advanced gastric / gastro-oesophageal junction (GEJ) adenocarcinoma — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Decisions That Change Treatment

- **Confirm:** adenocarcinoma on adequate tissue; site (gastric vs GEJ vs distal oesophageal adenocarcinoma); unresectable locally advanced vs metastatic.
- **Stage:** contrast CT chest–abdomen–pelvis; endoscopic ultrasound/laparoscopy only when the result could make the disease curable → occult peritoneal disease is common and changes intent.
- **Treat first what kills first:**
  - 🩸 Bleeding → endoscopic haemostasis, transfusion, short-course radiotherapy.
  - ⚠️ Gastric outlet or GEJ obstruction → stent, venting or feeding route before chemotherapy.
  - 🍽️ Weight loss, low albumin → dietitian input from day 1; nutrition predicts tolerance.
- Limited metastatic disease in a fit patient → multidisciplinary review; any local therapy belongs in an expert or trial setting.
- → Goal: palliative systemic therapy that prolongs life and preserves swallowing, eating and function.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🎯 Secure tissue, intent and a feeding route before cycle 1.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Avoid repeat endoscopies: request all four biomarkers on the first diagnostic sample.

## 2️⃣ Fitness + Biomarkers — Order Four Tests Before Cycle 1

- 🧬 **HER2:** IHC with gastric scoring; IHC 2+ → ISH. Positive = IHC 3+ or IHC 2+/ISH+. Heterogeneity is frequent → test several biopsy fragments or the resection specimen.
- 🧬 **PD-L1 CPS:** use a validated assay; CPS cut-offs (≥1, ≥5, ≥10) and the TAP score are not interchangeable between antibodies → record assay and score.
- 🧬 **MMR IHC / MSI:** dMMR/MSI-H changes the weight given to immunotherapy; consider germline evaluation.
- 🧬 **CLDN18.2:** positive = ≥75% of tumour cells with moderate–strong membranous CLDN18 staining (VENTANA 43-14A assay). <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-zolbetuximab-clzb-chemotherapy-gastric-or-gastroesophageal-junction-adenocarcinoma">[FDA 2024]</a>
- **Fitness:** ECOG, age and frailty, renal function (cisplatin), neuropathy (oxaliplatin), autoimmune disease and steroid use (PD-1), cardiac function/LVEF (HER2 antibodies), DPYD status where testing exists.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 HER2 + PD-L1 CPS + MMR + CLDN18.2 → one request, one block.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ PD-L1 CPS and CLDN18.2 often overlap → decide using the full profile, not the first result to return.
- 3️⃣ 🇮🇳 If budget limits testing → HER2 and MMR first, then PD-L1 CPS, then CLDN18.2.

## 3️⃣ Preferred First Line, HER2-Negative — Add a PD-1 Inhibitor When PD-L1 Is Positive

**Backbone:** CAPOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² day 1 + capecitabine 1,000 mg/m² twice daily days 1–14, every 3 weeks) or mFOLFOX6; FP (cisplatin–fluorouracil) as an alternative. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00515-6">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>

| Profile | Preferred | Alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HER2-negative, CPS ≥1 (stronger at CPS ≥5–10) | Fluoropyrimidine–platinum + nivolumab, pembrolizumab or tislelizumab | Zolbetuximab + chemo if CLDN18.2-positive |
| HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive, CPS <1 | Fluoropyrimidine–oxaliplatin + zolbetuximab | Chemotherapy alone |
| HER2-negative, CPS <1, CLDN18.2-negative | Fluoropyrimidine–platinum doublet | Reduced-dose doublet if frail |
| HER2-positive | Trastuzumab + chemo + pembrolizumab (CPS ≥1), or zanidatamab ± tislelizumab + chemo | Trastuzumab + chemo |
| dMMR/MSI-H | PD-1 inhibitor + chemotherapy | Refer for trial/expert input |

**📊 CheckMate 649: Nivolumab + Chemotherapy vs Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, open-label; 1,581 randomised to nivolumab (360 mg q3w or 240 mg q2w) + CAPOX/FOLFOX vs chemotherapy; non-HER2-positive gastric, GEJ or oesophageal adenocarcinoma; primary population CPS ≥5. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00797-2">[Lancet 2021]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS, CPS ≥5: HR 0.71 (98.4% CI 0.59–0.86) at first analysis. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00797-2">[Lancet 2021]</a>
  - 🫀 OS at 3 years, CPS ≥5: HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.61–0.81); 36-month OS 21% vs 10%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01601">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
  - 🫀 OS, all randomised: HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.71–0.88) at 24-month minimum follow-up. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04508-4">[Nature 2022]</a>
  - ⏱️ PFS, CPS ≥5: HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.60–0.81); 36-month PFS 13% vs 8%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01601">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR, CPS ≥5: 60% vs 45%; median duration of response 9.6 vs 7.0 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01601">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs 59% vs 44%; treatment-related deaths 2% vs 1%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00797-2">[Lancet 2021]</a>

**📊 KEYNOTE-859: Pembrolizumab + Chemotherapy vs Placebo + Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=1,579; HER2-negative gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma, ECOG 0–1; pembrolizumab 200 mg q3w up to 35 cycles + FP or CAPOX. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00515-6">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS, ITT: 12.9 vs 11.5 months (HR 0.78; 95% CI 0.70–0.87). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00515-6">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
  - 🫀 OS, CPS ≥1: 13.0 vs 11.4 months (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.65–0.84). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00515-6">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
  - 🫀 OS, CPS ≥10: 15.7 vs 11.8 months (HR 0.65; 95% CI 0.53–0.79). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00515-6">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Serious treatment-related AEs 23% vs 19%; grade 3–5 anaemia 12% vs 10%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00515-6">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>

**📊 RATIONALE-305: Tislelizumab + Chemotherapy vs Placebo + Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=997; tislelizumab 200 mg q3w + CAPOX or FP; PD-L1 by TAP score. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078876">[BMJ 2024]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS, TAP ≥5%: 17.2 vs 12.6 months (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.59–0.94). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078876">[BMJ 2024]</a>
  - 🫀 OS, all randomised: 15.0 vs 12.9 months (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.70–0.92). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078876">[BMJ 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs 54% vs 50%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078876">[BMJ 2024]</a>
- 📋 US indication (26 Dec 2024): HER2-negative disease with PD-L1 ≥1. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2025/761417Orig1s000Approv.pdf">[FDA 2024]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 📊 Three PD-1 trials agree: survival gain is real but grows with PD-L1 expression.
- 2️⃣ 🎯 CPS ≥5–10 → clear case for chemo-immunotherapy; CPS 1–4 → smaller, still labelled gain.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Where only one PD-1 agent is affordable, any of the three on a CAPOX backbone is evidence-based.

## 4️⃣ Alternatives — When PD-L1 Is Negative or Immunotherapy Is Unsuitable

- 📋 2025 US label changes: pembrolizumab + chemotherapy for HER2-negative disease now limited to CPS ≥1; nivolumab + chemotherapy limited to PD-L1 ≥1. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2025/125514Orig1s182ltr.pdf">[FDA 2025]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2025/125554Orig1s133ltr.pdf">[FDA 2025]</a>
- → CPS <1 and CLDN18.2-negative → chemotherapy doublet alone is standard.
- ⚠️ Autoimmune disease needing immunosuppression, organ transplant, poor PS → chemotherapy alone.

**📊 REAL-2: Capecitabine and Oxaliplatin as Substitutes ✨**

- **Study design:** 2×2 design; ECF vs ECX vs EOF vs EOX; non-inferiority of capecitabine vs fluorouracil and oxaliplatin vs cisplatin. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa073149">[NEJM 2008]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 Capecitabine vs fluorouracil: HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.80–0.99); oxaliplatin vs cisplatin: HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.80–1.10) → both non-inferior. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa073149">[NEJM 2008]</a>
  - 🫀 Median OS 9.9 (ECF), 9.9 (ECX), 9.3 (EOF), 11.2 months (EOX). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa073149">[NEJM 2008]</a>
- ⚠️ Oxaliplatin → less grade 3–4 neutropenia, renal toxicity and thromboembolism; more diarrhoea and neuropathy than cisplatin. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa073149">[NEJM 2008]</a>
- → Modern practice: anthracycline-free doublet; oxaliplatin preferred for renal or thrombotic risk.

**📊 KEYNOTE-062: Pembrolizumab Alone or With Chemotherapy (CPS ≥1) ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=763; pembrolizumab vs pembrolizumab + cisplatin–fluoropyrimidine vs chemotherapy; HER2-negative, CPS ≥1. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2020.3370">[JAMA Oncol 2020]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 Pembrolizumab alone non-inferior for OS at CPS ≥1: 10.6 vs 11.1 months (HR 0.91; 99.2% CI 0.69–1.18). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2020.3370">[JAMA Oncol 2020]</a>
  - 🫀 Pembrolizumab + chemo not superior at CPS ≥1: 12.5 vs 11.1 months (HR 0.85; 95% CI 0.70–1.03). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2020.3370">[JAMA Oncol 2020]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–5 treatment-related AEs 17% (pembrolizumab alone) vs 73% vs 69%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2020.3370">[JAMA Oncol 2020]</a>
- → Single-agent PD-1 first line is not a standard for unselected disease; reserve for dMMR/MSI-H or trials.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🔁 CPS <1 → no reliable immunotherapy gain; do not add cost and toxicity.
- 2️⃣ 💊 CAPOX = outpatient, port-free, oral fluoropyrimidine → practical default.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Generic capecitabine and oxaliplatin keep the backbone affordable.

## 5️⃣ Unfit, Older or Maintenance — Reduce Intensity Without Losing Control

**📊 GO2: Reduced-Intensity CAPOX in Older or Frail Patients ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=514 in the dose-intensity randomisation; median age 76; 58% severely frail; Level A (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² + capecitabine 625 mg/m² twice daily days 1–21, q21d) vs Level B (0.8×) vs Level C (0.6×). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.0848">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS non-inferior: B vs A HR 1.09 (95% CI 0.89–1.32); C vs A HR 1.10 (95% CI 0.90–1.33). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.0848">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>
  - ✨ Level C → less toxicity and better overall treatment utility, even in younger or less frail patients. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.0848">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>
  - 🫀 Chemotherapy vs best supportive care (n=45): OS 6.1 vs 3.0 months (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.32–1.48), not significant. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.0848">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>
- → Frail or elderly → start at 60% doses and escalate only if well tolerated.
- 🔁 **Maintenance:** after 4–6 months of response, stop oxaliplatin when neuropathy builds; continue fluoropyrimidine with the biologic (PD-1, trastuzumab/zanidatamab, zolbetuximab) until progression or toxicity; pembrolizumab in trials ran to a maximum of 35 cycles. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00515-6">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- ECOG ≥3 or rapidly failing nutrition → best supportive care is a legitimate first-line choice.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ ⏱️ Dose reduction from cycle 1 in frail patients preserves benefit and quality of life.
- 2️⃣ 🔁 Drop the neurotoxic drug, keep the targeted partner.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Lower doses also lower drug cost and day-care visits.

## 6️⃣ Progression — Sequence by Prior Exposure

**📊 RAINBOW: Ramucirumab + Paclitaxel (Second Line) ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=665; progression after fluoropyrimidine–platinum; weekly paclitaxel ± ramucirumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(14)70420-6">[Lancet Oncol 2014]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS 9.6 vs 7.4 months (HR 0.807; 95% CI 0.678–0.962). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(14)70420-6">[Lancet Oncol 2014]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 neutropenia 41% vs 19%; hypertension 14% vs 2%; febrile neutropenia 3% vs 2%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(14)70420-6">[Lancet Oncol 2014]</a>

**📊 REGARD: Ramucirumab Alone (Second Line, Unfit for Chemotherapy) ✨**

- **Key outcomes:** n=355; OS 5.2 vs 3.8 months (HR 0.776; 95% CI 0.603–0.998); hypertension 16% vs 8%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)61719-5">[Lancet 2014]</a>

**📊 DESTINY-Gastric04: Trastuzumab Deruxtecan vs Ramucirumab–Paclitaxel (HER2-Positive, Second Line) ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=494; HER2-positive disease progressing after trastuzumab-based first line. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2503119">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS 14.7 vs 11.4 months (HR 0.70; 95% CI 0.55–0.90). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2503119">[NEJM 2025]</a>
  - ⏱️ PFS HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.59–0.92); 🎯 confirmed ORR 44.3% vs 29.1%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2503119">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 drug-related AEs 50.0% vs 54.1%; adjudicated ILD/pneumonitis 13.9% (mostly grade 1–2) vs 1.3%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2503119">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- 🧬 Re-biopsy before second-line HER2 therapy where feasible → HER2 can be lost after trastuzumab.
- Supporting Asian data (DESTINY-Gastric01, third line or later): ORR 51% vs 14%; OS 12.5 vs 8.4 months (HR 0.59; 95% CI 0.39–0.88). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2004413">[NEJM 2020]</a>

**📊 Third Line and Beyond ✨**

- 💊 Trifluridine–tipiracil (TAGS): n=507; OS 5.7 vs 3.6 months (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.56–0.85); grade ≥3 AEs 80% vs 58%, mainly neutropenia 34%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(18)30739-3">[Lancet Oncol 2018]</a>
- 💊 Nivolumab in PD-1-naive Asian patients (ATTRACTION-2): n=493; OS 5.26 vs 4.14 months (HR 0.63; 95% CI 0.51–0.78); 12-month OS 26.2% vs 10.9%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)31827-5">[Lancet 2017]</a>
- → Irinotecan- or taxane-based chemotherapy remains a practical option depending on prior exposure.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🔁 HER2-positive → trastuzumab deruxtecan second line; HER2-negative → ramucirumab–paclitaxel.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ Screen lungs at every cycle with trastuzumab deruxtecan.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Paclitaxel alone or irinotecan when ramucirumab or ADCs are unaffordable.

## 7️⃣ HER2-Positive — Choose the HER2 Platform and Add PD-1 by CPS

**📊 ToGA: Trastuzumab + Chemotherapy vs Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=594; HER2 IHC overexpression or FISH amplification; cisplatin + capecitabine or fluorouracil for 6 cycles ± trastuzumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61121-X">[Lancet 2010]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS 13.8 vs 11.1 months (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.60–0.91). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61121-X">[Lancet 2010]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 AEs 68% vs 68%; cardiac AEs 6% vs 6%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61121-X">[Lancet 2010]</a>

**📊 KEYNOTE-811: Pembrolizumab + Trastuzumab + Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=698; HER2-positive; pembrolizumab 200 mg or placebo + trastuzumab + fluoropyrimidine–platinum q3w. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)02033-0">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🎯 ORR (first 264 patients) 74.4% vs 51.9%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-3726">[Clin Cancer Res 2026]</a>
  - ⏱️ PFS 10.0 vs 8.1 months (HR 0.73; 95% CI 0.61–0.87), third interim. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)02033-0">[Lancet 2023]</a>
  - 🫀 Final OS 20.0 vs 16.8 months (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.67–0.94). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-3726">[Clin Cancer Res 2026]</a>
  - 🧬 CPS ≥1 (85%): OS HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.66–0.95); CPS <1 (15%): HR 1.10 (95% CI 0.72–1.68) → no benefit. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-3726">[Clin Cancer Res 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs 58% vs 51%; diarrhoea any grade 47% vs 42%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)02033-0">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- 📋 US label: CPS ≥1 only. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2025/125514Orig1s182ltr.pdf">[FDA 2025]</a>

**📊 HERIZON-GEA-01: Zanidatamab ± Tislelizumab + Chemotherapy vs Trastuzumab + Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, open-label, 3 arms (1:1:1); centrally confirmed HER2-positive; median follow-up 25.9 months; zanidatamab 1,800 mg (<70 kg) or 2,400 mg (≥70 kg) q3w; tislelizumab 200 mg q3w; CAPOX or FP. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729">[NEJM 2026]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-zanidatamab-hrii-and-tislelizumab-jsgr-her2-positive-gastric-gastroesophageal-junction">[FDA 2026]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 12.4 (zanidatamab–tislelizumab–chemo, n=302) and 12.4 (zanidatamab–chemo, n=304) vs 8.1 months (n=308); HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.51–0.78) and 0.65 (95% CI 0.52–0.81). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729">[NEJM 2026]</a>
  - 🫀 OS, triplet vs trastuzumab–chemo: 26.4 vs 19.2 months (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.57–0.90). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729">[NEJM 2026]</a>
  - 🫀 OS, zanidatamab–chemo: 24.4 months (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.64–1.01) → not significant at interim. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729">[NEJM 2026]</a>
  - 🧬 IHC 3+ subgroup, zanidatamab–chemo: PFS 14.2 vs 7.6 months (HR 0.55; 95% CI 0.43–0.69), exploratory. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-zanidatamab-hrii-and-tislelizumab-jsgr-her2-positive-gastric-gastroesophageal-junction">[FDA 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs 83.3% vs 73.8% vs 74.5%; grade ≥3 diarrhoea 24.8% vs 20.0% vs 12.9%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729">[NEJM 2026]</a>
- 📋 US approval 25 Aug 2026: triplet for IHC 3+ or IHC 2+/ISH+; zanidatamab–chemo for IHC 3+ only; boxed warning for diarrhoea and embryo-fetal toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-zanidatamab-hrii-and-tislelizumab-jsgr-her2-positive-gastric-gastroesophageal-junction">[FDA 2026]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🎯 HER2-positive + CPS ≥1 → trastuzumab + pembrolizumab + chemo, or zanidatamab + tislelizumab + chemo where available.
- 2️⃣ 🧬 CPS <1 → trastuzumab + chemo; zanidatamab + chemo is an option for IHC 3+.
- 3️⃣ ⚠️ Zanidatamab regimens → proactive diarrhoea plan and LVEF monitoring.
- 4️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Trastuzumab biosimilars make ToGA-based therapy the most widely deliverable HER2 regimen.

## 8️⃣ CLDN18.2-Positive, HER2-Negative — Decide When to Add Zolbetuximab

**📊 SPOTLIGHT: Zolbetuximab + mFOLFOX6 vs Placebo + mFOLFOX6 ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=565; CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative; excluded complete or partial gastric outlet obstruction. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00620-7">[Lancet 2023]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-zolbetuximab-clzb-chemotherapy-gastric-or-gastroesophageal-junction-adenocarcinoma">[FDA 2024]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 10.61 vs 8.67 months (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.60–0.94). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00620-7">[Lancet 2023]</a>
  - 🫀 OS 18.2 vs 15.5 months (HR 0.750; 95% CI 0.601–0.936). <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-zolbetuximab-clzb-chemotherapy-gastric-or-gastroesophageal-junction-adenocarcinoma">[FDA 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent AEs 87% vs 78%; nausea, vomiting and reduced appetite most common. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00620-7">[Lancet 2023]</a>

**📊 GLOW: Zolbetuximab + CAPOX vs Placebo + CAPOX ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=507; CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02465-7">[Nat Med 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 8.21 vs 6.80 months (HR 0.687; 95% CI 0.544–0.866). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02465-7">[Nat Med 2023]</a>
  - 🫀 OS 14.39 vs 12.16 months (HR 0.771; 95% CI 0.615–0.965). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02465-7">[Nat Med 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent AEs 72.8% vs 69.9%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02465-7">[Nat Med 2023]</a>

**Practical use**

- 💊 Dose: 800 mg/m² first dose, then 600 mg/m² q3w or 400 mg/m² q2w, with fluoropyrimidine–platinum. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-zolbetuximab-clzb-chemotherapy-gastric-or-gastroesophageal-junction-adenocarcinoma">[FDA 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Nausea/vomiting peaks in early cycles → NK1 + 5-HT3 + dexamethasone, slow initial infusion, pause and restart rather than stop.
- 🔁 CLDN18.2-positive and CPS ≥5 → no head-to-head data; PD-1 trials show stronger OS signal at higher CPS, zolbetuximab is the main targeted option when CPS is low. Combination of both is investigational.
- ⚠️ Pre-existing outlet obstruction → relieve first; these patients were excluded from SPOTLIGHT.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 CLDN18.2 ≥75% + HER2-negative + CPS <1 → zolbetuximab + chemo.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ Emesis control decides whether patients stay on treatment.
- 3️⃣ 🇮🇳 Check CDSCO status and the CLDN18 assay before promising zolbetuximab.

## 9️⃣ MSI-H, Dual Checkpoint and FGFR2b — Use Smaller Data Sets Carefully

**📊 MSI-H Subgroups of KEYNOTE-059, -061 and -062 ✨**

- MSI-H frequency: 4.0% (KN-059), 5.3% (KN-061), 7.3% (KN-062). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.0275">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>
- KN-062 MSI-H: ORR 57.1% (pembrolizumab), 64.7% (pembrolizumab + chemo) vs 36.8% (chemo); median PFS 11.2 months, not reached, and 6.6 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.0275">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>
- → dMMR/MSI-H → PD-1 inhibitor + chemotherapy first line; PD-1 alone is an option for those unfit for chemotherapy.

**📊 CheckMate 649: Nivolumab + Ipilimumab vs Chemotherapy ✨**

- OS in CPS ≥5 did not meet the significance boundary → dual checkpoint blockade is not a standard first line. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04508-4">[Nature 2022]</a>

**FGFR2b (bemarituzumab)**

- In FIGHT and FORTITUDE-101, OS hazard ratios moved towards no effect at later data cut-offs; a nivolumab-backbone phase 3 stopped for inadequate efficacy (sponsor report); not peer-reviewed in full. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s11912-026-01845-9">[Curr Oncol Rep 2026]</a>
- → FGFR2b testing is not needed for routine first-line choice; trials only.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 MSI-H is uncommon but most responsive → never skip MMR testing.
- 2️⃣ 🔁 Nivolumab + ipilimumab and FGFR2b antibodies → outside routine first-line use.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Targeted spending: test MMR (inexpensive IHC) for everyone.

## 🔟 Special Situations — Obstruction, Bleeding, Ascites and Limited Metastases

- ⚠️ **Outlet obstruction:** duodenal stent or gastrojejunostomy before chemotherapy; plan zolbetuximab only after symptoms are controlled.
- 🩸 **Bleeding:** endoscopic therapy, palliative radiotherapy; hold anti-angiogenics (ramucirumab) during active bleeding.
- 💧 **Malignant ascites/peritoneal disease:** drain for symptoms; poor PS and oral intake → reduced-dose doublet; intraperitoneal approaches are investigational.
- 🫀 **HER2 therapy and the heart:** baseline LVEF; LVEF ≥50% was required in HERIZON-GEA-01. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729">[NEJM 2026]</a>
- ⏱️ **Limited metastatic disease responding to systemic therapy:** multidisciplinary discussion; surgery after conversion is not standard outside trials.
- 🧪 **Liver dysfunction/jaundice:** check biliary obstruction; fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin doses per hepatic function.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ ⚠️ Fix obstruction, bleeding and nutrition first → chemotherapy tolerance follows.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Endoscopic stents and short-course radiotherapy are low-cost, high-value palliation.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Site, Histology and Special Populations — Apply the Data Correctly

- **GEJ and oesophageal adenocarcinoma:** CheckMate 649 and HERIZON-GEA-01 included oesophageal adenocarcinoma; KEYNOTE-859, KEYNOTE-811, SPOTLIGHT and GLOW were gastric/GEJ trials. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00797-2">[Lancet 2021]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2517729">[NEJM 2026]</a>
- **Diffuse / signet-ring histology:** HER2 positivity less common; CLDN18.2 testing worthwhile; peritoneal spread more frequent → watch for obstruction and ascites.
- **Young patients, diffuse histology or family history:** consider hereditary gastric cancer syndromes (CDH1) and Lynch syndrome (dMMR) → genetic counselling.
- **Older adults:** geriatric screen; reduced-dose CAPOX from cycle 1 (GO2). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.0848">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>
- **Squamous histology at the GEJ:** treat as oesophageal SCC, a different pathway.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 Check that the trial population matches the tumour site before applying its result.
- 2️⃣ 🇮🇳 Genetic counselling capacity is limited → prioritise young or familial cases.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Palliation, Follow-up + India Practice — Plan a Sustainable Course

- Early palliative care, nutrition and pain control alongside systemic therapy.
- Response imaging every 2–3 months or with new symptoms; monitor neuropathy, hand–foot syndrome, thyroid/hepatic/adrenal immune toxicity, LVEF on HER2 therapy, ILD on trastuzumab deruxtecan.
- 🔁 Pre-plan second line at first-line start (HER2 status known → DESTINY-Gastric04 route; HER2-negative → ramucirumab–paclitaxel route).
- 🇮🇳 Check current CDSCO indications and real-world supply for zolbetuximab, zanidatamab, tislelizumab and trastuzumab deruxtecan; an FDA approval does not establish Indian availability.
