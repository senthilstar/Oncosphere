**Advanced gallbladder and biliary cancer — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Establish Origin and Treatment Intent

- Confirm adenocarcinoma and origin: gallbladder, intrahepatic or extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma; distinguish ampullary carcinoma and metastasis from another primary.
- Expert multidisciplinary review → resectable, potentially convertible, unresectable local or metastatic disease. Complete resection remains the curative pathway when feasible.
- **Record:**
  - ECOG, frailty, sarcopenia, weight loss, pain, jaundice, pruritus, nutrition and family history/germline clues.
  - Sites and burden, disease tempo, oligometastases, vascular involvement, previous surgery and adjuvant therapy.
  - Stent type/date, drainage territory, prior cholangitis and resistant organisms.
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; liver MRI/MRCP for anatomical clarification. Obtain adequate tissue while preserving material for profiling.
- CBC, bilirubin/LFT, albumin, INR, creatinine/CrCl, electrolytes and magnesium; CEA and CA19-9 after drainage where possible. Obstruction can elevate CA19-9; Lewis-antigen-negative patients may not produce it. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Map disease extent and tempo → assess intent with the MDT.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 2️⃣ Biliary Drainage and Fitness — Correct Reversible Barriers

- Fever + obstruction → cultures, antibiotics and urgent endoscopic/interventional drainage assessment before cytotoxic treatment. A stent in place does not prove effective drainage.
- ⚠️ Fever with rising bilirubin during treatment → investigate stent blockage, cholangitis, abscess or sepsis before assuming progression.
- Drain clinically important obstruction; reassess bilirubin and functional recovery. Plan reintervention access before treatment starts.
- Cisplatin selection → renal reserve, hearing, neuropathy and hydration tolerance; checkpoint selection → autoimmune disease, transplant, immunosuppression and infection review.
- 🧬 Send HER2 IHC with ISH as appropriate, MMR/MSI and tumour profiling early. FGFR2 fusions/IDH1 mutations are enriched in intrahepatic disease; HER2 is particularly relevant to gallbladder/extrahepatic cancers. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- Inadequate tissue or resistance → ctDNA; consider repeat biopsy for evolving or discordant HER2 status. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- Broad profiling can identify BRAF V600E, NTRK/RET fusions, TMB-high, KRAS G12C and other rare actionable findings; require a validated alteration and treatment-specific eligibility. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Drain infection and send biomarkers early.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 3️⃣ Preferred First Line — Add Checkpoint Blockade to GemCis

**📊 TOPAZ-1: Durvalumab + Gemcitabine–Cisplatin**

- **Study design:** double-blind phase 3; n=685; untreated advanced biliary cancer; durvalumab versus placebo with identical GemCis. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>
- **Regimen:** durvalumab 1,500 mg IV day 1; gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² + cisplatin 25 mg/m² IV days 1,8, every 21 days for up to 8 cycles → durvalumab 1,500 mg every 4 weeks until progression/unacceptable toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>
- ⏱️ **Primary analysis:** PFS 7.2 vs 5.7 months (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.63–0.89); ORR 26.7% vs 18.7%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 AEs 75.7% vs 77.8%; monitor cytopenias, infection and immune injury. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>


- 🫀 **Updated OS (2024):** 12.9 vs 11.3 months (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.64–0.91); 24-month OS 23.6% vs 11.5%. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38823398/">[Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2024]</a>
- 📈 **Three-year exploratory update (2025):** OS 12.9 vs 11.3 months (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.63–0.87); 36-month OS 14.6% vs 6.9% (updated: J Hepatol 2025 supersedes earlier OS HR 0.76). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2025.05.003">[J Hepatol 2025]</a>
- PD-L1 selection is not required for this trial-defined approach; gallbladder subgroup estimates do not establish a separate preferred checkpoint agent. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Control obstruction and infection before starting.
- 2️⃣ Chemotherapy stops at its protocol cap; antibody continuation is a separate phase.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Generic GemCis is a defensible access pathway if checkpoint treatment cannot be sustained.

## 4️⃣ Choose an Alternative — Match Pembrolizumab, Chemotherapy or Fitness to Circumstances

**📊 KEYNOTE-966: Pembrolizumab + GemCis**

- **Study design:** double-blind phase 3; n=1,069; previously untreated unresectable/metastatic biliary cancer; pembrolizumab versus placebo. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00727-4">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- **Regimen:** pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks, up to 35 cycles; gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² and cisplatin 25 mg/m² IV days 1,8 every 21 days; cisplatin capped at 8 cycles, gemcitabine could continue. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00727-4">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- 🫀 Prespecified OS analysis: 12.7 vs 10.9 months (HR 0.83; 95% CI 0.72–0.95). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00727-4">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- 🫀 Longer follow-up → OS 12.7 vs 10.9 months (HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.75–0.98; updated: 0.86 supersedes earlier 0.83). <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42898854/">[EClinicalMedicine 2026]</a>
- ⏱️ PFS 6.5 vs 5.6 months (HR 0.85; 95% CI 0.75–0.97); ORR 29% vs 29% → exploratory follow-up; prespecified PFS significance threshold was not met. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42898854/">[EClinicalMedicine 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Maximum grade 3–4 AEs 79% vs 75%; grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs 70% vs 69%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00727-4">[Lancet 2023]</a>


**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Durvalumab-GemCis and pembrolizumab-GemCis are alternatives → no randomized comparison ranks one above the other; choose by schedule, cost and availability.
- 2️⃣ Do not copy the TOPAZ chemotherapy cap into this protocol without an explicit adaptation.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Select by local indication, infusion burden and total continuation cost.

## 5️⃣ Choose Maintenance or Reduced Treatment — Preserve Feasible Care

**📊 ABC-02: Establish the Chemotherapy Backbone**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=410; advanced biliary cancer; GemCis versus gemcitabine alone. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0908721">[N Engl J Med 2010]</a>
- GemCis days 1,8 every 21 days for 8 cycles versus gemcitabine days 1,8,15 every 28 days for 6 cycles. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0908721">[N Engl J Med 2010]</a>
- 📈 OS 11.7 vs 8.1 months (HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.52–0.80); PFS 8.0 vs 5.0 months; tumour control 81.4% vs 71.8%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0908721">[N Engl J Med 2010]</a>
- More neutropenia with the doublet; infection, renal injury and obstruction need active surveillance.


- After TOPAZ-1 chemotherapy → continue durvalumab alone if benefit and tolerance persist; after KEYNOTE-966 cisplatin → pembrolizumab with or without continuing gemcitabine as clinically appropriate. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00727-4">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- Cisplatin unsuitable → consider split-dose cisplatin when appropriate, or selected GEMOX, gemcitabine–carboplatin or fluoropyrimidine regimen; evidence differs from GemCis–checkpoint trials.
- Frailty → attenuated gemcitabine or symptom-directed care; reassess after drainage/nutrition. Avoid checkpoint monotherapy as an unselected substitute.
- Persistent renal injury, hearing loss or cumulative neuropathy → hold/reduce/stop the responsible drug; do not preserve dose intensity at the cost of organ function.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Adapt treatment to organ reserve and fitness.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 6️⃣ Progression — Choose FOLFOX or a Matched Target Before Decline

**📊 ABC-06: FOLFOX + Active Symptom Control**

- **Study design:** open-label phase 3; n=162; ECOG 0–1 after GemCis progression; FOLFOX plus symptom care versus symptom care alone. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00027-9">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>
- Every 14 days, up to 12 cycles: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m², L-folinic acid 175 mg or folinic acid 350 mg, 5-FU 400 mg/m² bolus → 2,400 mg/m² over 46 hours, IV. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00027-9">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>
- 📈 OS 6.2 vs 5.3 months (adjusted HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.50–0.97); 12-month OS 25.9% vs 11.4%; ORR 5% with FOLFOX. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00027-9">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–5 AEs 69% vs 52%; three chemotherapy-related deaths. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00027-9">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>


- Retrieve profiling, check stent function and reassess ECOG/neuropathy before another line. Evidence predates routine first-line checkpoint therapy.
- 🎯 Matched targeted therapy when eligible and accessible → otherwise FOLFOX or selected nal-IRI + 5-FU/leucovorin; evidence predates routine first-line checkpoint therapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00027-9">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>

**📊 NIFTY: Liposomal Irinotecan + 5-FU/Leucovorin vs 5-FU/Leucovorin ✨**

- **Study design:** randomised phase 2b; 178 assigned, 174 treated and analysed after GemCis; nal-IRI + fluorouracil/leucovorin versus fluorouracil/leucovorin. <a class="cite" href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaoncology/fullarticle/2802825">[JAMA Oncol 2023]</a>
- **Regimen:** nal-IRI 70 mg/m², leucovorin 400 mg/m² and fluorouracil 2,400 mg/m² IV over 46 hours, every 2 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34656226/">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 neutropenia 24% vs 1%; fatigue/asthenia 13% vs 3%. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34656226/">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>
- ⏱️ Updated central review: PFS 4.2 vs 1.7 months (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.44–0.86); OS 8.6 vs 5.3 months (HR 0.68; 95% CI 0.48–0.95). <a class="cite" href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaoncology/fullarticle/2802825">[JAMA Oncol 2023]</a>
- NALIRICC did not reproduce PFS or OS benefit → individualise; monitor neutropenia, diarrhoea, hydration and hepatic function. Consider UGT1A1 assessment in selected patients. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38870977/">[Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2024]</a>
- 🔁 Other selected salvage → FOLFIRI, CAPOX, GEMOX, capecitabine, irinotecan, modified FOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine/platinum rechallenge; weigh prior response, treatment-free interval, neuropathy, organ function and fitness.
- ⚠️ Consider DPD deficiency before fluoropyrimidines; monitor mucositis, diarrhoea and cytopenias.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Match subsequent treatment to biology and prior exposure.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 7️⃣ HER2-Amplified Disease — Select Antibody Therapy by Assay

**📊 HERIZON-BTC-01: Zanidatamab**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2b; 87 HER2-amplified previously treated patients; primary IHC 2+/3+ cohort n=80. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00242-5">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- Zanidatamab 20 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks until progression/toxicity. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00242-5">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- 🎯 Final IHC 2+/3+ cohort → ORR 41.3% (95% CI 30.4–52.8), PFS 5.5 months, OS 15.5 months; IHC 3+ subset → ORR 51.6% (95% CI 38.6–64.5), PFS 7.2 months, OS 18.1 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2025.4736">[JAMA Oncol 2025]</a>
- 🇺🇸 FDA accelerated approval → previously treated unresectable/metastatic IHC 3+ biliary cancer using an approved test; IHC 2+/amplified trial results do not establish US eligibility. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-zanidatamab-hrii-previously-treated-unresectable-or-metastatic-her2">[FDA 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3 treatment-related AEs 18%; diarrhoea, infusion reactions and cardiac monitoring matter. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00242-5">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- The response denominator includes IHC 2+ disease; regulatory eligibility can be narrower, including IHC 3+ requirements. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00242-5">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>


**📊 SGNTUC-019: Tucatinib + Trastuzumab ✨**

- Previously treated HER2-positive metastatic biliary cancer; n=30 → ORR 46.7% (90% CI 30.8–63.0), disease control 76.7%, median response duration 6.0 months, PFS 5.5 months, OS 15.5 months; 12-month OS 53.6%. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10730072/">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>
- Tucatinib 300 mg orally twice daily + trastuzumab 8 mg/kg IV loading, then 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10730072/">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events 60%; monitor fever, diarrhoea and liver tests. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10730072/">[J Clin Oncol 2023]</a>

**📊 MyPathway: Pertuzumab + Trastuzumab ✨**

- Previously treated HER2-amplified/overexpressing metastatic biliary cancer; n=39 → ORR 23% (95% CI 11–39). <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34339623/">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>
- 🧬 HER2 mutation is distinct from amplification or IHC 3+ → do not automatically apply amplification/overexpression evidence to isolated mutations. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>

**📊 SUMMIT: Neratinib for HER2 Mutation ✨**

- Previously treated HER2-mutant biliary cohort: 25 patients, including 10 gallbladder cancers → ORR 16% (95% CI 4.5–36.1); diarrhoea warrants prevention and monitoring. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-36399-y">[Nat Commun 2023]</a>
- Biosimilar trastuzumab-based combinations may provide an access option after specialist review; they are not interchangeable with zanidatamab evidence. Reassess HER2 if prior tissue is scant or discordant. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36328033/">[Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2023]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Separate HER2 mutation from amplification and expression.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 8️⃣ HER2-Expressing Disease — Weigh ADC Response Against Lung Risk

**📊 HERB: Trastuzumab Deruxtecan**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; 32 treated; primary HER2-positive biliary cohort n=22 after gemcitabine-based therapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02010">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks; ORR 36.4% (90% CI 19.6–56.1). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02010">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- Median PFS 5.1 months and OS 7.1 months in the HER2-positive cohort. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02010">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related anaemia 53.1%, neutropenia 31.3%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02010">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ ILD in 8/32 treated patients (25%), including two fatal events; this safety denominator includes HER2-low patients. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02010">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>


**📊 DESTINY-PanTumor02: Confirm the Tumour-Specific Denominator**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; 267 pretreated HER2 IHC 2+/3+ solid tumours; biliary cohort n=41. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02005">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks; biliary-cohort ORR 22.0%; centrally confirmed IHC 3+ biliary subset ORR 56.3% (n=16). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02005">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⏱️ Centrally confirmed IHC 3+ biliary subset → median PFS 7.4 months (95% CI 2.8–12.5). <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13452304/">[ESMO Open 2026]</a>
- Do not apply the all-tumour response rate to biliary cancer.


- New cough/dyspnoea → interrupt immediately, assess CT/infection and start appropriate pneumonitis management; symptomatic ILD → permanent discontinuation and corticosteroids.
- Prior HER2 therapy, assay, ILD risk and local indication determine eligibility. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02005">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Balance HER2-directed benefit against lung toxicity.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 9️⃣ Choose FGFR2 Therapy — Restrict Evidence to Eligible Cholangiocarcinoma

**📊 FIGHT-202: Pemigatinib**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; final FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement cohort n=108; previously treated cholangiocarcinoma. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103488">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- Pemigatinib 13.5 mg orally daily, days 1–14 of each 21-day cycle. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103488">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- Final ORR 37.0% (95% CI 27.9–46.9); PFS 7.0 months; OS 17.5 months. Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events 37.0% in the fusion/rearrangement cohort. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103488">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>


**📊 FOENIX-CCA2: Futibatinib**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; n=103; pretreated FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement-positive intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206834">[N Engl J Med 2023]</a>
- Futibatinib 20 mg orally daily continuously; ORR 42% (95% CI 32–52); PFS 9.0 months; OS 21.7 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206834">[N Engl J Med 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3 treatment-related hyperphosphataemia 30%; monitor phosphate, nails/skin and retinal symptoms. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206834">[N Engl J Med 2023]</a>


- A generic FGFR mutation does not reproduce FGFR2 fusion eligibility. Baseline/serial ophthalmic assessment, phosphate management and drug-interaction review are essential. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206834">[N Engl J Med 2023]</a>
- These later-line results do not establish a first-line gallbladder strategy.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Confirm the FGFR2 fusion and anatomical eligibility.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 🔟 IDH1 and Rare Targets — Treat the Demonstrated Alteration

**📊 ClarIDHy: Ivosidenib**

- **Study design:** double-blind phase 3; n=185 in primary PFS analysis; previously treated IDH1-mutant cholangiocarcinoma; ivosidenib versus placebo, crossover allowed. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(20)30157-1">[Lancet Oncol 2020]</a>
- Ivosidenib 500 mg orally daily; PFS 2.7 vs 1.4 months (HR 0.37; 95% CI 0.25–0.54). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(20)30157-1">[Lancet Oncol 2020]</a>
- Stable disease 51% vs 28%; ORR 2% vs 0%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(20)30157-1">[Lancet Oncol 2020]</a>
- 🫀 Final OS 10.3 vs 7.5 months (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.56–1.12); crossover complicates interpretation → monitor ECG/QTc, electrolytes and interactions. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34554208/">[JAMA Oncol 2021]</a>


- MSI-H/dMMR or TMB-high → consider checkpoint treatment within indication; benefit after progression on a prior checkpoint agent is uncertain.
- 🧬 BRAF V600E → dabrafenib + trametinib; NTRK fusion → larotrectinib or entrectinib; RET fusion → selpercatinib (tumour-agnostic indications, where eligible). KRAS G12C → emerging targeted strategies or trial. BRCA1/2, PALB2, ATM/HRD, ALK/ROS1 fusions and MET amplification → molecular tumour board; trial preferred. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- 🔁 At progression or when access is limited → clinical trial enrolment is a valid option at every line.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Treat the demonstrated alteration within its indication.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Choose by Histology and Extent — Individualise Unusual Disease

- NuTide:121 stopped for OS futility; NUC-1031 + cisplatin had shorter OS despite a higher response rate → do not substitute on mechanistic grounds. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39978598/">[J Hepatol 2025]</a>
- Capecitabine, 5-FU, GEMOX and regional regimens can be pragmatic alternatives; state the evidence and goal rather than implying equivalence to contemporary chemoimmunotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- Oligoprogression → MDT consideration of SBRT, ablation or other local treatment while continuing otherwise effective systemic therapy.
- Sustained major response with resectable residual oligometastatic disease → MDT conversion assessment; survival benefit from surgery is unproven. Selected liver-directed approaches include radiation, ablation and arterial therapy.
- Neuroendocrine/small-cell or mixed histology → expert pathology review and histology-led systemic therapy; pure squamous disease requires individualisation.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Use local intervention selectively after MDT review.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Consider testing and treatment access in the care plan.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Palliation and India Practice — Fund Drainage and Follow-up

- ⚠️ Checkpoint toxicity → assess pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, nephritis and myocarditis promptly.
- Pain control, nutrition, pruritus management and early supportive care alongside treatment; radiotherapy for focal painful/bleeding lesions when appropriate.
- Reassess symptoms/laboratories each cycle; imaging generally every 8–12 weeks, sooner for deterioration. A rising CA19-9 alone does not mandate a drug change. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- 🇮🇳 Gallbladder cancer contributes substantially to biliary cancer care in India, particularly northern/eastern regions → prioritise reliable histology, drainage and HER2 testing. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- 💰 When broad NGS is unaffordable → prioritise HER2 IHC, then MMR/MSI, then focused actionable testing; broaden when feasible. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11410730/">[ESMO Open 2024]</a>
- Generic GemCis/FOLFOX: lower cost tier; checkpoint agents, zanidatamab, T-DXd and FGFR inhibitors: substantially higher cost tier. Include travel, stent exchanges, antibiotics and monitoring.
- Verify current CDSCO indication, actual supply and assistance/PM-JAY or state coverage; overseas approval does not establish Indian approval.

- 1️⃣ Drainage failures can mimic drug failure.
- 2️⃣ Plan second-line testing while patients remain fit.
- 3️⃣ 💰 A complete affordable chemotherapy/support plan is preferable to an interrupted high-cost course.
