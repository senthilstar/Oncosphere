## Before cycle 1 — establish origin and drainage

- Confirm the primary site and histology. Gallbladder adenocarcinoma can share a systemic backbone with other biliary cancers while needing a different molecular emphasis. Neuroendocrine or unusual mixed morphology requires another pathology-led discussion.
- Define unresectable, recurrent or metastatic disease through multidisciplinary review. Record liver involvement, peritoneal disease, distant nodes and the anatomical reason surgery is not currently appropriate.
- Use staging imaging and dedicated liver/biliary imaging as needed. Map obstruction and stents; laboratory evidence alone cannot establish whether the drainage system is functioning.
- Treat cholangitis, relieve clinically important obstruction and reassess bilirubin before chemotherapy. Fever with deteriorating liver tests during treatment should trigger a search for infection or stent failure.
- Assess performance status, intake, weight, marrow reserve, renal function, hearing and hydration tolerance. Separate disease-related decline from fixed comorbidity before excluding combination treatment.
- Preserve biopsy material for HER2 IHC with confirmatory testing as appropriate, MMR assessment and broader profiling when feasible. Gallbladder disease should not inherit an intrahepatic-cholangiocarcinoma target assumption.

## Choosing first line — deliver a coherent backbone

| Situation | Preferred approach | Alternatives / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Fit for cisplatin and checkpoint blockade | Gemcitabine–cisplatin + durvalumab or pembrolizumab | Select by local indication, access and continuation plan |
| Checkpoint blockade unsuitable or inaccessible | Gemcitabine–cisplatin | Do not postpone effective chemotherapy indefinitely |
| Cisplatin unsafe | Individualised chemotherapy alternative | Substituting the platinum does not reproduce pivotal chemo-immune evidence |
| Frail but potentially treatable | Attenuated or single-agent treatment | Reassess after drainage and nutritional support |
| Persistent severe frailty or uncontrolled illness | Symptom-directed management | Revisit treatment only if physiology improves |

As of November 2026, check the applicable local indication for the selected checkpoint agent and later targeted treatment. No direct comparison establishes durvalumab or pembrolizumab as universally superior. Infusion logistics, autoimmune disease and the ability to sustain continuation treatment belong in the initial choice.

## The evidence — biliary populations, distinct questions

- **ABC-02:** Gemcitabine–cisplatin versus gemcitabine: OS 11.7 versus 8.1 months; HR 0.64. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0908721">[N Engl J Med 2010]</a>
- **TOPAZ-1:** Durvalumab added to gemcitabine–cisplatin: OS HR 0.80; OS at 24 months 24.9% versus 10.4%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>
- **TOPAZ-1 update:** Median OS 12.9 versus 11.3 months; HR 0.76. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38823398/">[Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2024]</a> Keep this analysis distinct from the original landmark estimate.
- **KEYNOTE-966:** Pembrolizumab added to gemcitabine–cisplatin: OS 12.7 versus 10.9 months; HR 0.83. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00727-4">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- **HERIZON-BTC-01:** Zanidatamab in previously treated HER2-amplified biliary cancer: confirmed response 41.3%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00242-5">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a> A later-line, nonrandomised signal, not a replacement first-line comparison.

## Pitfalls / pearls

- **Biliary physiology:** a blocked stent, abscess or cholangitis can mimic progression. Obtain cultures and urgent drainage assessment when indicated; do not reflexively change anticancer therapy on a deteriorating bilirubin alone.
- **Cisplatin safety:** watch renal function, magnesium, hearing and fluid tolerance. Continuing an injurious drug to preserve the original prescription can compromise every subsequent option.
- **Immune injury:** consider hepatitis, colitis, endocrine disturbance and pneumonitis, while evaluating ordinary infection and obstruction. New symptoms need timely assessment rather than an assumption that all abnormalities are drug-related.
- **Biomarker precision:** HER2 expression, amplification and mutation describe different findings. Confirm the assay and treatment-setting criteria before proposing a matched drug.
- **Anatomical precision:** FGFR fusions and IDH alterations are more characteristic targets in intrahepatic disease. Treat the alteration demonstrated in this tumour, not the name of its broader disease family.
- **Response interpretation:** meaningful disease stabilisation can matter even without dramatic shrinkage. A single-arm response result is not evidence that a targeted drug should replace established untreated-disease therapy.

<p class="opinion">Opinion: a named contact for stent problems is as important as the chemotherapy plan. Preventing avoidable interruptions from biliary infection often determines whether the intended regimen can actually be delivered.</p>

## After first line — retrieve the profile before decline

At progression, reassess performance status, bilirubin, renal reserve and residual neuropathy. Review HER2 and MMR results before defaulting to another cytotoxic regimen. The zanidatamab evidence cited here concerns previously treated HER2-amplified biliary disease; it is not an untreated gallbladder comparison.

Consider a fluoropyrimidine-based subsequent regimen, a biomarker-matched option or a trial according to prior exposure and access. A new biopsy can be helpful when the disease pattern or target status is uncertain. Selected focal progression may justify local treatment after multidisciplinary review, but conversion surgery is not a routine expectation in disseminated disease. Continue drainage surveillance, nutrition and pain management even when the systemic regimen changes.
