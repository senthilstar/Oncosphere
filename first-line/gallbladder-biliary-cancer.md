**Advanced gallbladder and biliary cancer — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Establish Origin and Treatment Intent

- Confirm adenocarcinoma and origin: gallbladder, intrahepatic or extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma; distinguish ampullary carcinoma and metastasis from another primary.
- Expert multidisciplinary review → resectable, potentially convertible, unresectable local or metastatic disease. Complete resection remains the curative pathway when feasible.
- **Record:**
  - ECOG, frailty, weight loss, pain, jaundice, pruritus and nutritional intake.
  - Liver/peritoneal burden, vascular involvement, previous surgery and adjuvant therapy.
  - Stent type/date, drainage territory, prior cholangitis and resistant organisms.
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; liver MRI/MRCP for anatomical clarification. Obtain adequate tissue while preserving material for profiling.
- CBC, bilirubin/LFT, albumin, creatinine/CrCl, electrolytes and magnesium; CA19-9 after drainage where possible. Obstruction can elevate CA19-9 without progression.

## 2️⃣ Biliary Drainage and Fitness — Correct Reversible Barriers

- Fever + obstruction → cultures, antibiotics and urgent endoscopic/interventional drainage assessment before cytotoxic treatment. A stent in place does not prove effective drainage.
- Drain clinically important obstruction; reassess bilirubin and functional recovery. Plan reintervention access before treatment starts.
- Cisplatin selection → renal reserve, hearing, neuropathy and hydration tolerance; checkpoint selection → autoimmune disease, transplant, immunosuppression and infection review.
- 🧬 Send HER2 IHC with ISH as appropriate, MMR/MSI and tumour profiling early. FGFR2 fusions/IDH1 mutations are enriched in intrahepatic disease; HER2 is particularly relevant to gallbladder/extrahepatic cancers.
- Broad profiling can identify BRAF V600E, NTRK/RET fusions and other rare actionable findings; require a validated alteration and treatment-specific eligibility.

## 3️⃣ Preferred First Line — Add Checkpoint Blockade to GemCis

**📊 TOPAZ-1: Durvalumab + Gemcitabine–Cisplatin**

- **Study design:** double-blind phase 3; n=685; untreated advanced biliary cancer; durvalumab versus placebo with identical GemCis.
- **Regimen:** durvalumab 1,500 mg IV day 1; gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² + cisplatin 25 mg/m² IV days 1,8, every 21 days for up to 8 cycles → durvalumab 1,500 mg every 4 weeks until progression/unacceptable toxicity.
- ⏱️ **Primary analysis:** PFS 7.2 vs 5.7 months (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.63–0.89); ORR 26.7% vs 18.7%.
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 AEs 75.7% vs 77.8%; monitor cytopenias, infection and immune injury.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015).

- 📈 **Three-year exploratory update (2025):** OS 12.9 vs 11.3 months (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.63–0.87); 36-month OS 14.6% vs 6.9%. [Updated publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40381735/).
- PD-L1 selection is not required for this trial-defined approach; gallbladder subgroup estimates do not establish a separate preferred checkpoint agent.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Control obstruction and infection before starting.
- 2️⃣ Chemotherapy stops at its protocol cap; antibody continuation is a separate phase.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Generic GemCis is a defensible access pathway if checkpoint treatment cannot be sustained.

## 4️⃣ Alternative Chemoimmunotherapy — Match the Continuation Schedule

**📊 KEYNOTE-966: Pembrolizumab + GemCis**

- **Study design:** double-blind phase 3; n=1,069; previously untreated unresectable/metastatic biliary cancer; pembrolizumab versus placebo.
- **Regimen:** pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks, up to 35 cycles; gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² and cisplatin 25 mg/m² IV days 1,8 every 21 days; cisplatin capped at 8 cycles, gemcitabine could continue.
- 📈 OS 12.7 vs 10.9 months (HR 0.83; 95% CI 0.72–0.95).
- ⚠️ Maximum grade 3–4 AEs 79% vs 75%; grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs 70% vs 69%.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00727-4).

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ No randomized comparison ranks pembrolizumab against durvalumab.
- 2️⃣ Do not copy the TOPAZ chemotherapy cap into this protocol without an explicit adaptation.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Select by local indication, infusion burden and total continuation cost.

## 5️⃣ When Immunotherapy or Cisplatin Is Unsuitable — Preserve Feasible Treatment

**📊 ABC-02: Establish the Chemotherapy Backbone**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=410; advanced biliary cancer; GemCis versus gemcitabine alone.
- GemCis days 1,8 every 21 days for 8 cycles versus gemcitabine days 1,8,15 every 28 days for 6 cycles.
- 📈 OS 11.7 vs 8.1 months (HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.52–0.80); PFS 8.0 vs 5.0 months.
- More neutropenia with the doublet; infection, renal injury and obstruction need active surveillance.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0908721).

- Cisplatin unsuitable → selected GEMOX, gemcitabine–carboplatin or fluoropyrimidine regimen; evidence differs from GemCis–checkpoint trials.
- Frailty → attenuated gemcitabine or symptom-directed care; reassess after drainage/nutrition. Avoid checkpoint monotherapy as an unselected substitute.
- Persistent renal injury, hearing loss or cumulative neuropathy → hold/reduce/stop the responsible drug; do not preserve dose intensity at the cost of organ function.

## 6️⃣ Progression — Choose FOLFOX or a Matched Target Before Decline

**📊 ABC-06: FOLFOX + Active Symptom Control**

- **Study design:** open-label phase 3; n=162; ECOG 0–1 after GemCis progression; FOLFOX plus symptom care versus symptom care alone.
- Every 14 days, up to 12 cycles: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m², folinic acid 175 mg or 350 mg, 5-FU 400 mg/m² bolus → 2,400 mg/m² over 46 hours, IV.
- 📈 OS 6.2 vs 5.3 months (adjusted HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.50–0.97); 12-month OS 25.9% vs 11.4%.
- ⚠️ Grade 3–5 AEs 69% vs 52%; three chemotherapy-related deaths.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00027-9).

- Retrieve profiling, check stent function and reassess ECOG/neuropathy before another line. Evidence predates routine first-line checkpoint therapy.
- A matched targeted option may be preferable when eligible and accessible; otherwise FOLFOX is the randomized cytotoxic reference. FOLFIRI or liposomal irinotecan–5-FU requires regional-evidence and access discussion.

## 7️⃣ HER2-Amplified Disease — Select Antibody Therapy by Assay

**📊 HERIZON-BTC-01: Zanidatamab**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2b; 87 HER2-amplified previously treated patients; primary IHC 2+/3+ cohort n=80.
- Zanidatamab 20 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks until progression/toxicity.
- Confirmed ORR 41.3% (95% CI 30.4–52.8) in the IHC 2+/3+ cohort.
- ⚠️ Grade 3 treatment-related AEs 18%; diarrhoea, infusion reactions and cardiac monitoring matter.
- The response denominator includes IHC 2+ disease; regulatory eligibility can be narrower, including IHC 3+ requirements.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00242-5).

- Biosimilar trastuzumab-based combinations may provide an access option after specialist review; they are not interchangeable with zanidatamab evidence. Reassess HER2 if prior tissue is scant or discordant.

## 8️⃣ HER2-Expressing Disease — Weigh ADC Response Against Lung Risk

**📊 HERB: Trastuzumab Deruxtecan**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; 32 treated; primary HER2-positive biliary cohort n=22 after gemcitabine-based therapy.
- T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks; ORR 36.4% (90% CI 19.6–56.1).
- ⚠️ ILD in 8/32 treated patients (25%), including two fatal events; this safety denominator includes HER2-low patients.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02010).

**📊 DESTINY-PanTumor02: Confirm the Tumour-Specific Denominator**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; 267 pretreated HER2 IHC 2+/3+ solid tumours; biliary cohort n=41.
- T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks; biliary-cohort ORR 22.0%; centrally confirmed IHC 3+ biliary subset ORR 56.3% (n=16).
- Do not apply the all-tumour response rate to biliary cancer.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.02005).

- New cough/dyspnoea → interrupt immediately, assess CT/infection and start appropriate pneumonitis management; symptomatic ILD → permanent discontinuation and corticosteroids.
- Prior HER2 therapy, assay, ILD risk and local indication determine eligibility.

## 9️⃣ FGFR2 Fusion Disease — Use Cholangiocarcinoma-Specific Evidence

**📊 FIGHT-202: Pemigatinib**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; final FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement cohort n=108; previously treated cholangiocarcinoma.
- Pemigatinib 13.5 mg orally daily, days 1–14 of each 21-day cycle.
- Final ORR 37.0% (95% CI 27.9–46.9); PFS 7.0 months; OS 17.5 months.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103488).

**📊 FOENIX-CCA2: Futibatinib**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; n=103; pretreated FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement-positive intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma.
- Futibatinib 20 mg orally daily continuously; ORR 42% (95% CI 32–52); PFS 9.0 months; OS 21.7 months.
- ⚠️ Grade 3 treatment-related hyperphosphataemia 30%; monitor phosphate, nails/skin and retinal symptoms.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206834).

- A generic FGFR mutation does not reproduce FGFR2 fusion eligibility. Baseline/serial ophthalmic assessment, phosphate management and drug-interaction review are essential.
- These later-line results do not establish a first-line gallbladder strategy.

## 🔟 IDH1 and Rare Targets — Treat the Demonstrated Alteration

**📊 ClarIDHy: Ivosidenib**

- **Study design:** double-blind phase 3; n=185 in primary PFS analysis; previously treated IDH1-mutant cholangiocarcinoma; ivosidenib versus placebo, crossover allowed.
- Ivosidenib 500 mg orally daily; PFS 2.7 vs 1.4 months (HR 0.37; 95% CI 0.25–0.54).
- Benefit mainly disease stabilisation; interpret OS with crossover. Monitor ECG/QTc, electrolytes and interacting drugs.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(20)30157-1).

- MSI-H/dMMR → consider checkpoint treatment within indication; benefit after progression on a prior checkpoint agent is uncertain.
- BRAF V600E → matched BRAF/MEK approach; NTRK/RET fusion → appropriate selective inhibitor where eligible. Molecular tumour board/trial if access or variant significance is uncertain.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Historical and Regional Approaches — Avoid Unsupported Substitution

- NUC-1031 + cisplatin failed to improve outcomes over GemCis in NuTide:121 → do not substitute a novel gemcitabine derivative on mechanistic grounds.
- Capecitabine, 5-FU, GEMOX and regional regimens can be pragmatic alternatives; state the evidence and goal rather than implying equivalence to contemporary chemoimmunotherapy.
- Liver-limited intrahepatic disease → expert discussion of ablation, radiation, arterial approaches or conversion surgery in selected patients. Disseminated gallbladder cancer rarely becomes a surgical problem.
- Neuroendocrine/small-cell or mixed histology → expert pathology review and histology-led systemic therapy; pure squamous disease requires individualisation.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Palliation and India Practice — Fund Drainage and Follow-up

- Pain control, nutrition, pruritus management and early supportive care alongside treatment; radiotherapy for focal painful/bleeding lesions when appropriate.
- Reassess symptoms/laboratories each cycle; imaging generally every 2–3 months, sooner for deterioration. A rising CA19-9 alone does not mandate a drug change.
- 🇮🇳 Gallbladder cancer contributes substantially to biliary cancer care in India, particularly northern/eastern regions → prioritise reliable histology, drainage and HER2 testing.
- Generic GemCis/FOLFOX: lower cost tier; checkpoint agents, zanidatamab, T-DXd and FGFR inhibitors: substantially higher cost tier. Include travel, stent exchanges, antibiotics and monitoring.
- Verify current CDSCO indication, actual supply and assistance/PM-JAY or state coverage; overseas approval does not establish Indian approval.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Drainage failures can mimic drug failure.
- 2️⃣ Plan second-line testing while patients remain fit.
- 3️⃣ 💰 A complete affordable chemotherapy/support plan is preferable to an interrupted high-cost course.
