**Advanced renal cell carcinoma — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Confirm Histology and Treatment Urgency

- Confirm renal origin and clear-cell versus non-clear-cell histology; document sarcomatoid/rhabdoid differentiation. Review unusual pathology before committing to a clear-cell algorithm.
- **Record:**
  - ECOG performance status, frailty, weight loss, symptoms and disease tempo.
  - Primary tumour status; lung, liver, bone, nodal and brain disease.
  - Nephrectomy, metastasectomy, adjuvant pembrolizumab, completion dates and unresolved immune toxicity.
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; brain MRI and skeletal imaging according to symptoms and suspicion. Neurological symptoms → urgent brain assessment.
- CBC, creatinine/eGFR, corrected calcium, liver tests, albumin, thyroid function, BP and urine protein; evaluate haematuria, anaemia and nutritional depletion.
- Low-volume asymptomatic disease with slow growth → consider active surveillance with agreed imaging and treatment triggers; prospective median time to systemic therapy 14.9 months. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27498080/">[Lancet Oncol 2016]</a>
- Organ-threatening disease → prompt systemic control.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Confirm subtype and urgency before committing to treatment.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 2️⃣ Fitness and Biomarkers — Assign IMDC Risk Before Selecting a Doublet

- **IMDC adverse factors:** Karnofsky performance status <80%; diagnosis-to-systemic-treatment interval <1 year; haemoglobin below normal; corrected calcium, neutrophils or platelets above normal.
- No factors → favourable; 1–2 → intermediate; 3–6 → poor risk. Correct reversible abnormalities and interpret disease-driven poor function separately from fixed frailty.
- PD-L1 testing does not select routine first-line RCC therapy. Broad NGS is not necessary for conventional clear-cell treatment; hereditary assessment is useful for young onset, bilateral/multifocal tumours or suggestive family history.
- Active serious autoimmune disease, transplant or prior life-threatening immune toxicity → individualise checkpoint use. Uncontrolled hypertension, major cardiovascular disease, bleeding, proteinuria or recent surgery → reconsider VEGFR inhibition.
- Intermediate/poor risk → nivolumab–ipilimumab or an immune–TKI doublet. Favourable risk needing treatment → usually an immune–TKI doublet; no randomized comparison identifies a universal best combination.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ IMDC and comorbidity guide selection; PD-L1 is not routinely required.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 3️⃣ Immune Doublet — Choose Nivolumab–Ipilimumab for Suitable Intermediate/Poor Risk

**Regimen 💉**

- Nivolumab 3 mg/kg IV + ipilimumab 1 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks for four doses → nivolumab 240 mg IV every 2 weeks or 480 mg every 4 weeks. Continue according to the applicable protocol/label; the original study did not impose a universal upfront two-year cap.
- Discuss early immune toxicity against the possibility of durable control without continuous oral TKI treatment.

**📊 CheckMate 214: Final Follow-up ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, open-label; n=1,096; untreated advanced clear-cell RCC; nivolumab–ipilimumab versus sunitinib. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2025.43.16_suppl.4505">[JCO 2025]</a>
- 📈 **Key outcomes — intention-to-treat:** OS 52.7 vs 37.8 months; HR 0.71. ORR 39.5% vs 33.0%; complete response 12.0% vs 3.5%. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2025.43.16_suppl.4505">[JCO 2025]</a>
- 📈 **Intermediate/poor risk:** median OS 46.7 vs 26.0 months; nine-year OS 30.2% vs 18.7%; OS HR 0.69; this remains the principal risk-defined population supporting routine use. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2025.43.16_suppl.4505">[JCO 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs 48.4% vs 64.1%. Immune colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis and endocrinopathy require rapid assessment. <a class="cite" href="https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2025.43.16_suppl.4505">[JCO 2025]</a>
- Sarcomatoid subgroup → ORR 60.8% vs 23.1%; PFS 26.5 vs 5.5 months; OS 48.6 vs 14.2 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2022-005445">[J Immunother Cancer 2022]</a>
- Sarcomatoid differentiation → favour an immune-containing regimen. CheckMate 214 subgroup findings support this choice; they do not constitute a separate head-to-head comparison of immune combinations.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Discuss durable control and early immune toxicity.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 4️⃣ Immune–TKI Therapy — Choose by Urgency and Toxicity Profile

**📊 KEYNOTE-426: Pembrolizumab + Axitinib**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=861; untreated advanced clear-cell RCC across IMDC groups; comparator sunitinib. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03867-5">[Nat Med 2025]</a>
- **Regimen:** pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks, up to 35 cycles, + axitinib 5 mg orally twice daily; titrate only with adequate tolerance. Pembrolizumab 400 mg every 6 weeks is an alternative label schedule.
- 📈 **Five-year outcomes:** OS 47.2 vs 40.8 months; HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.71–0.99). PFS 15.7 vs 11.1 months; HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.59–0.81); ORR 60.6% vs 39.6%; CR 11.6% vs 4.0%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03867-5">[Nat Med 2025]</a>

**📊 CheckMate 9ER: Nivolumab + Cabozantinib**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=651; untreated advanced clear-cell RCC; comparator sunitinib. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2025.09.006">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- **Regimen:** nivolumab 240 mg IV every 2 weeks, capped at two years in the trial, + cabozantinib 40 mg orally daily. Nivolumab 480 mg every 4 weeks is an alternative label schedule.
- ⏱️ **Extended follow-up:** PFS 16.4 vs 8.3 months; HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.49–0.70). OS 46.5 vs 35.5 months; HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.65–0.96); ORR 55.7% vs 27.4%; CR 13.9% vs 4.6%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2025.09.006">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs 67.8% vs 55.0%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2025.09.006">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>

**📊 CLEAR: Pembrolizumab + Lenvatinib**

- **Study design:** phase 3, three arms; n=1,069 overall; pembrolizumab–lenvatinib versus sunitinib comparison n=712. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01569">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- **Regimen:** lenvatinib 20 mg orally daily + pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks or 400 mg every 6 weeks, up to two years; oral treatment can continue afterwards.
- ⏱️ **Final prespecified OS analysis:** PFS 23.9 vs 9.2 months; HR 0.47 (95% CI 0.38–0.57). OS 53.7 vs 54.3 months; HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.63–0.99). ORR 71.3% vs 36.7%; CR 18.3% vs 4.8%; three-year OS 66.4% vs 60.2%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01569">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent AEs 84.9% vs 74.7%. Similar OS medians do not negate the hazard-ratio result. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01569">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- Rapid shrinkage may favour an immune–TKI approach, but response rates from different trials cannot rank these regimens.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Select by urgency, comorbidity and access; cross-trial comparisons cannot rank doublets.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 5️⃣ Unsuitable for Combinations — Select Sustainable Monotherapy or Surveillance

- Checkpoint contraindication or unaffordable combination → quality-assured pazopanib 800 mg orally daily fasting or sunitinib 50 mg orally daily, four weeks on/two weeks off; adapt for tolerance and organ function.
- Cabozantinib monotherapy 60 mg orally daily is another option, particularly in intermediate/poor-risk disease when appropriate and accessible.
- Frailty → correct reversible causes, consider dose adaptation and early supportive care; irreversible severe functional impairment may make symptom-directed care preferable.
- There is no chemotherapy-to-checkpoint maintenance pathway analogous to other cancers. Continue the chosen active component according to its protocol, response and toxicity.
- Planned immune treatment completion is different from stopping both drugs early because imaging shows a response; evidence for elective early oral-TKI withdrawal remains limited.

- 📊 COMPARZ → pazopanib vs sunitinib PFS 8.4 vs 9.5 months; noninferiority met; quality of life generally favoured pazopanib. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1303989">[NEJM 2013]</a>
- 📊 RESTORE → sunitinib two weeks on/one week off can ease cumulative toxicity; six-month failure-free survival 63% vs 44% with four weeks on/two weeks off; OS superiority unproven. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26347107/">[Eur J Cancer 2015]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 💰 Choose treatment and monitoring that remain affordable.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 6️⃣ Progression — Sequence by Prior Immune and VEGFR Exposure

- After nivolumab–ipilimumab → VEGFR-TKI, often cabozantinib if unused; alternatives depend on tolerance and access.
- After pembrolizumab–axitinib or pembrolizumab–lenvatinib → unused cabozantinib, another appropriate VEGFR inhibitor, lenvatinib–everolimus in selected patients, or a trial.
- After nivolumab–cabozantinib → consider lenvatinib–everolimus, another unused VEGFR inhibitor or belzutifan when eligible; cumulative toxicity and local availability matter.
- Lenvatinib–everolimus: 18 mg + 5 mg orally daily; monitor hypertension, proteinuria, mucositis, metabolic changes and pneumonitis.
- Check actual progression, adherence and tolerated TKI exposure before switching. Isolated progression → multidisciplinary local treatment may preserve otherwise effective therapy.
- 💊 Cabozantinib monotherapy 60 mg daily → reduce to 40 mg then 20 mg for toxicity. METEOR after VEGFR treatment: PFS 7.4 vs 3.8 months and OS 21.4 vs 16.5 months (HR 0.66) vs everolimus; older VEGF-pretreated setting differs from current sequences. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30107-3">[Lancet Oncol 2016]</a>
- IO-naive after VEGFR-TKI → nivolumab is an option: CheckMate 025 OS 25.8 vs 19.7 months (HR 0.73), five-year OS 26% vs 18% vs everolimus. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-20-2174">[Clin Cancer Res 2020]</a>
- 📊 Study 205 after VEGF therapy → lenvatinib–everolimus vs everolimus: PFS 14.6 vs 5.5 months (HR 0.40); ORR 43% vs 6%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(15)00290-9">[Lancet Oncol 2015]</a>
- 📊 LenCabo after PD-1 therapy → lenvatinib–everolimus vs cabozantinib: PFS 15.7 vs 10.2 months (HR 0.51; 95% CI 0.29–0.89); ORR 52.6% vs 38.6%; phase 2, OS immature. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41115467/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- Later-line tivozanib → TIVO-3 after prior VEGFR therapy: PFS 5.6 vs 3.9 months (HR 0.73; 95% CI 0.56–0.94); ORR 18% vs 8% vs sorafenib; no established OS advantage. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31810797/">[Lancet Oncol 2020]</a>
- CONTACT-03 and TiNivo-2 → adding checkpoint blockade after progression did not improve outcomes; routine rechallenge is discouraged. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01472-3">[Lancet 2023]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)01764-4">[Lancet 2024]</a>
- Routine checkpoint recycling after checkpoint-resistant progression is unsupported; distinguish intolerance, completed treatment and resistance.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Prior exposure and tolerance guide salvage.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 7️⃣ Prior Adjuvant Therapy — Reassess Before Reusing PD-1 Blockade

- KEYNOTE-564 established adjuvant pembrolizumab after nephrectomy in selected high-risk clear-cell RCC; it did not establish the optimal metastatic sequence after relapse. [Adjuvant survival analysis](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2312695).
- Relapse during or soon after adjuvant pembrolizumab → favour a VEGFR-directed strategy or trial rather than assuming a PD-1 combination reproduces checkpoint-naive results.
- Long treatment-free interval → individualise immune rechallenge; document the uncertainty and any persistent immune injury.
- Re-biopsy an unusual recurrence when histology or an alternative diagnosis could change treatment. Reassess oligometastatic local options before starting indefinite systemic therapy.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Adjuvant exposure changes the evidence for metastatic treatment.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 8️⃣ Later-line HIF-2α Inhibition — Use Belzutifan After Both Relevant Classes

**📊 LITESPARK-005: Belzutifan versus Everolimus**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=746; advanced clear-cell RCC previously treated with checkpoint and antiangiogenic therapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2313906">[NEJM 2024]</a>
- **Regimen:** belzutifan 120 mg orally daily versus everolimus 10 mg daily.
- **Published analyses:** median PFS 5.6 months in both arms, but PFS HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.63–0.90); ORR 21.9% vs 3.5%. Final OS 21.4 vs 18.2 months; HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.77–1.10); 24-month PFS 17.5% vs 4.1%, without established OS superiority. <a class="cite" href="https://s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/m-anage.com.storage.esmo/static/esmo2024_abstracts/LBA74.html.pdf">[Ann Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs 61.8% vs 62.5%. Anaemia and hypoxia are signature risks → check haemoglobin and oxygen saturation, investigate symptoms and use label-directed holds/reductions. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2313906">[NEJM 2024]</a>
- 💊 After PD-(L)1 therapy in advanced RCC with a clear-cell component → belzutifan 120 mg + lenvatinib 20 mg orally daily; US FDA approval September 2026. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/206947s035lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- 📊 LITESPARK-011 (n=747) vs cabozantinib → PFS 14.6 vs 10.6 months (HR 0.74) at the primary analysis; final analysis (Feb 2026 cut-off) → PFS HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.62–0.88); ORR 53.1% vs 40.2%; response duration 22.0 vs 11.9 months; OS 33.7 vs 28.6 months (HR 0.85; 95% CI 0.70–1.03), not statistically significant; grade ≥3 TEAEs 85.4% vs 83.0%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.109">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- Belzutifan is a later-line option in this evidence base; do not extrapolate it to untreated sporadic metastatic RCC.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Monitor haemoglobin, oxygenation and vascular toxicity.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 9️⃣ Escalation and Historical Regimens — Avoid Unproven Routine Triplets

**📊 COSMIC-313: Adding Cabozantinib to Nivolumab–Ipilimumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=855; untreated intermediate/poor-risk clear-cell RCC; cabozantinib 40 mg orally daily versus placebo, each with standard nivolumab–ipilimumab induction and nivolumab continuation. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2026.02.011">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- ⏱️ **Final analysis:** PFS 16.6 vs 11.2 months (HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.69–0.98); OS 41.9 vs 42.0 months (HR 1.02); no OS improvement. Grade 3–4 treatment-related toxicity approximately 75% vs 43%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2026.02.011">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- Extra toxicity without a demonstrated survival gain → do not adopt routine triplet escalation. [Final analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2026.02.011).
- Historical access-driven options → sorafenib 400 mg twice daily (TARGET PFS 5.5 vs 2.8 months vs placebo after cytokines); temsirolimus 25 mg IV weekly (poor-risk Global ARCC OS 10.9 vs 7.3 months vs interferon). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa060655">[NEJM 2007]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa066838">[NEJM 2007]</a>
- Everolimus alone → fallback when preferred therapies are inaccessible or unsuitable; monitor stomatitis, glucose, lipids, infection and pneumonitis.
- High-dose interleukin-2 is an exceptional specialist option; severe toxicity and infrastructure needs limit applicability. Conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy has little routine role in clear-cell RCC.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Historical options require their original trial context.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 🔟 Primary Tumour and Oligometastases — Select Surgery After Assessing Systemic Need

- CARMENA supported omitting automatic upfront cytoreductive nephrectomy in intermediate/poor-risk patients needing sunitinib. SURTIME supported using initial systemic treatment to help select patients for deferred surgery; its small sample limits conclusions.
- 📊 CARMENA → OS 18.4 vs 13.9 months for sunitinib alone vs upfront surgery; noninferiority met. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1803675">[NEJM 2018]</a>
- 📊 SURTIME → deferred vs immediate surgery OS 32.4 vs 15.0 months; small trial, secondary endpoint. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2018.5543">[JAMA Oncol 2019]</a>
- Both were conducted in the TKI era; neither proves that all nephrectomy should be abandoned or defines an immune-combination survival benefit from surgery. [CARMENA](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1803675), [SURTIME](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2018.5543).
- High metastatic burden, poor risk or rapidly progressive disease → systemic therapy first. Good response, limited residual disease and good fitness → discuss deferred nephrectomy.
- Symptomatic bleeding primary → embolisation, surgery or radiation according to anatomy and goals. Selected oligometastases → metastasectomy or stereotactic radiation after multidisciplinary review.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Use multidisciplinary assessment to select local therapy.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Non-clear-cell Disease — Choose a Histology-specific Pathway

**📊 KEYNOTE-B61: Pembrolizumab + Lenvatinib**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; n=158 treated; untreated advanced non-clear-cell RCC; pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks, up to two years, + lenvatinib 20 mg orally daily. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00276-0">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- 🎯 **Later follow-up:** ORR 50.6%; CR 16/158; median PFS 17.9 months. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12509603/">[Oncologist 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Initial analysis: grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs 51%. Activity supports an option, without randomized proof of superiority. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00276-0">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>

**📊 CA209-9KU: Cabozantinib + Nivolumab**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; n=47 across two histology cohorts; advanced non-clear-cell RCC, with zero or one prior systemic line. Cabozantinib 40 mg orally daily + nivolumab 240 mg IV every 2 weeks or 480 mg every 4 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.21.01944">[J Clin Oncol 2022]</a>
- **Initial cohort 1 analysis:** n=40 with papillary, unclassified or translocation-associated disease; ORR 47.5% (95% CI 31.5–63.9); PFS 12.5 months; OS 28 months. The small chromophobe cohort had no objective responses. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.21.01944">[J Clin Oncol 2022]</a>
- This is cabozantinib–nivolumab evidence, distinct from lenvatinib–pembrolizumab; histology and prior-line differences prevent direct comparison. [Phase 2 publication](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.21.01944).
- **PAPMET/SWOG-1500:** cabozantinib vs sunitinib → PFS 9.0 vs 5.6 months; ORR 23% vs 4%; crizotinib and savolitinib arms stopped for futility. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00152-5">[Lancet 2021]</a>
- 📊 SUNNIFORECAST → nivolumab–ipilimumab vs investigator-selected care in non-clear-cell RCC: 12-month OS 78% vs 68%; median OS 33.2 vs 25.2 months (HR 0.81; 95% CI 0.61–1.099); median OS difference not significant. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40180121/">[Ann Oncol 2025]</a>
- Chromophobe → trial, lenvatinib–pembrolizumab, lenvatinib–everolimus or selected VEGFR-TKI. First-line lenvatinib–everolimus phase 2: ORR 26% overall, 4/9 chromophobe patients; small single-arm study. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33867192/">[Eur Urol 2021]</a>
- 🧬 Suspected FH-deficient RCC → FH and 2SC pathology work-up, germline assessment and counselling. Bevacizumab 10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks + erlotinib 150 mg orally daily → phase 2 hereditary cohort ORR 72%, PFS 21.1 months, OS 44.6 months; grade ≥3 hypertension 34%, proteinuria 17%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2200900">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- Collecting duct → gemcitabine–cisplatin; substitute carboplatin for renal insufficiency. GETUG phase 2: ORR 26%, PFS 7.1 months, OS 10.5 months. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17437788/">[J Urol 2007]</a>
- BONSAI → cabozantinib in untreated collecting duct disease: ORR 35%, PFS 4 months, OS 7 months; separate small studies cannot rank it against platinum. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35420628/">[JAMA Oncol 2022]</a>
- Renal medullary carcinoma → assess SMARCB1 loss and sickle haemoglobinopathy; platinum-based chemotherapy, expert pathology and rare-tumour referral or trial.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Rare histologies need expert pathology and tailored treatment.
- 2️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Match the plan to sustainable access and monitoring.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Monitoring and India Practice — Fund Toxicity Care Alongside Treatment

- Reassess clinically each cycle; CT typically every 8–12 weeks initially, adapted to tempo. Check BP, urine protein, renal/liver/thyroid function and symptoms during therapy.
- Severe diarrhoea or hepatitis on immune–TKI therapy → assess both drugs, infection and dehydration. Holding the oral drug must not delay treatment of serious immune injury.
- Oral dose reduction can preserve benefit: cabozantinib combination dosing commonly 40 → 20 mg daily → 20 mg alternate days; lenvatinib RCC combination dosing 20 → 14 → 10 → 8 mg daily. Use the exact label and toxicity-specific restart rules. Checkpoint drugs are held/discontinued rather than dose-reduced.
- 🦴 Painful bone disease → analgesia, radiation and stabilisation when needed; assess dental/renal suitability for bone-directed agents. Brain metastases → neurosurgery/radiation review and systemic planning.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Match risk, tempo and toxicity; avoid cross-trial rankings.
- 2️⃣ Record perioperative PD-1 exposure before choosing metastatic treatment.
- 3️⃣ 💰 🇮🇳 Generic VEGFR-TKIs may be the sustainable option; include monitoring, travel and later-line costs in the plan. Verify exact CDSCO indications, procurement quality and assistance/PM-JAY or state-scheme coverage.
- 4️⃣ Biosimilars used elsewhere in oncology do not establish an interchangeable checkpoint product; use only appropriately authorised products. Preserve a rapid contact route for immune symptoms and early palliative care.
