## Before cycle 1 — define the biological pathway

- Confirm clear-cell carcinoma and document sarcomatoid features. Papillary, chromophobe, collecting-duct and hereditary subtypes require their own treatment discussion; a renal primary alone does not justify this algorithm.
- Establish disease distribution, growth rate and symptom burden. Review the primary tumour, prior nephrectomy, previous adjuvant treatment and any persistent toxicity.
- Assign an IMDC category using performance status, diagnosis-to-treatment interval, haemoglobin, corrected calcium, neutrophils and platelets. Use a validated calculator; risk classification complements the clinical picture.
- Obtain blood count, renal and hepatic tests, thyroid function, blood pressure and urine protein. Record weight, appetite and functional limitations so subsequent toxicity can be recognised.
- Review autoimmune illness, transplant history, cardiovascular disease, bleeding risk and current medications. A solitary kidney calls for thoughtful monitoring, not an automatic arbitrary reduction of every drug.
- PD-L1 is unnecessary for routine selection. Imaging of brain or bone should respond to symptoms and clinical suspicion rather than delaying urgently needed treatment for an indiscriminate work-up.

## Choosing first line — risk plus urgency

| Situation | Preferred approach | Alternatives / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Intermediate/poor risk; suitable for immune therapy | Nivolumab–ipilimumab or an immune–TKI doublet | Select by tempo, likely tolerance and patient priorities |
| Rapid growth or threatened organ function | Immune–TKI doublet | Pembrolizumab–axitinib, nivolumab–cabozantinib or pembrolizumab–lenvatinib |
| Favourable risk needing treatment | Immune–TKI doublet | Selected indolent, asymptomatic disease may be observed |
| Sarcomatoid differentiation | An immune-containing regimen | Review unusual pathology and clinical urgency |
| Immune therapy unsuitable or inaccessible | VEGFR-TKI monotherapy | Pazopanib or sunitinib with a sustainable monitoring plan |

There is no direct trial establishing a preferred immune doublet for every patient. Discuss the difference between continuous oral toxicity and the early immune-toxicity burden of nivolumab–ipilimumab. Surveillance requires agreed imaging and treatment triggers; it is an active management plan.

## The evidence — read the comparator

- **CheckMate 214:** Nivolumab–ipilimumab versus sunitinib: OS HR 0.72 overall and 0.69 in intermediate/poor risk. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.07.727">[Ann Oncol 2024]</a>
- **CheckMate 214 sarcomatoid analysis:** Median OS 48.6 versus 14.2 months; HR 0.46. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2022-005445">[J Immunother Cancer 2022]</a> Interpret as a subgroup analysis.
- **KEYNOTE-426:** Pembrolizumab–axitinib versus sunitinib: OS HR 0.84; PFS HR 0.69. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03867-5">[Nat Med 2025]</a>
- **CheckMate 9ER:** Nivolumab–cabozantinib versus sunitinib: PFS 16.4 versus 8.3 months; HR 0.58. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2025.09.006">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a> OS 46.5 versus 35.5 months; HR 0.79. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2025.09.006">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- **CLEAR:** Lenvatinib–pembrolizumab versus sunitinib: PFS 23.9 versus 9.2 months; HR 0.47. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01569">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a> OS HR 0.79 despite medians of 53.7 versus 54.3 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.01569">[J Clin Oncol 2024]</a>
- **COSMIC-313:** Adding cabozantinib to nivolumab–ipilimumab: PFS HR 0.82, OS HR 1.02; severe treatment-related toxicity 75% versus 43%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2026.02.011">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a> No OS improvement to justify routine escalation.

## Pitfalls / pearls

- **Blood pressure and proteinuria:** establish a home or local monitoring route before a VEGFR inhibitor. Treat emerging hypertension promptly; quantify proteinuria and assess renal change in context.
- **Diarrhoea or hepatitis on a combination:** either component may be responsible. Severe or persistent illness needs urgent assessment for immune toxicity, infection and dehydration. A brief oral-drug interruption must not delay treatment of suspected serious immune injury.
- **Dose adaptation:** oral TKI reduction can preserve useful therapy. Immune toxicity instead calls for protocol-directed interruption, immunosuppression or discontinuation, rather than empiric immune-drug dose reduction.
- **Interpretation:** similar median survival values do not negate an effect across the survival curve. Conversely, an attractive response rate from a different trial does not establish superiority.
- **Primary-tumour surgery:** discuss selectively after considering burden, symptoms and response. Automatic upfront nephrectomy can postpone systemic control in patients who need it urgently.

<p class="opinion">Opinion: choose the regimen whose toxicities can be recognised and managed where the patient lives. Reliable blood-pressure review and rapid access for immune symptoms are part of regimen selection, not administrative details.</p>

## After first line — sequence by exposure

After nivolumab–ipilimumab, a VEGFR inhibitor is a practical subsequent strategy. After immune–TKI treatment, review which kinase inhibitor was used, dose tolerance and whether the stop represented resistance or toxicity. Cabozantinib when unused, or another mechanistically appropriate regimen, may be considered according to access and eligibility.

Routine checkpoint recycling after clear progression is not a default strategy. Selected local progression can merit multidisciplinary assessment while otherwise useful systemic treatment continues. Record residual diarrhoea, neuropathy, proteinuria and immune complications at every handover; these may determine the next feasible option more than the original risk category.
