**Metastatic colorectal cancer — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Identify Curable Metastases and Urgent Symptoms

- Confirm histology and metastatic extent; CT chest, abdomen and pelvis, with liver MRI when it will clarify local treatment. Record primary location, prior adjuvant therapy and residual toxicity.
- **Record:**
  - Performance status, frailty, nutrition, neuropathy, organ function, tumour burden and treatment goals.
  - Pain, bleeding, obstruction, perforation and symptoms from liver or peritoneal disease; check blood count, renal and liver function.
- Potentially resectable or convertible liver-limited or oligometastatic disease → early multidisciplinary review with liver surgery, radiology and oncology; plan systemic therapy around local treatment.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🎯 Resectability and urgent symptoms determine the immediate pathway.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Coordinate imaging and surgical review early to avoid wasted treatment.

## 2️⃣ Fitness + Biomarkers — Order Results Before Choosing Treatment

- 🧬 Request MMR immunohistochemistry or MSI testing, extended RAS including KRAS and NRAS, BRAF V600E and HER2 assessment, particularly in RAS-wild-type disease. Broader profiling can identify KRAS G12C and rare fusions.
- Record sidedness: left includes splenic flexure through rectum. Interpret it alongside RAS, BRAF and MMR rather than in isolation.
- Consider DPYD testing before fluoropyrimidines where available and UGT1A1 testing when irinotecan toxicity risk is relevant; adjust treatment using applicable laboratory and prescribing guidance.
- Assess neuropathy before oxaliplatin, diarrhoea risk before irinotecan and bleeding, perforation, wound healing, blood pressure and proteinuria before bevacizumab.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 Order biomarkers before selecting a biologic; reconcile discordant MMR findings.
- 2️⃣ 🇮🇳 Prioritise accessible testing that changes the first treatment decision.

## 3️⃣ Preferred First Line — Select Immunotherapy or Biomarker-Directed Treatment

- 🎯 MSI-H/dMMR → checkpoint blockade; confirm unexpected results with repeat or concordant testing.
- 🧬 BRAF V600E with MSS → targeted therapy plus chemotherapy; concurrent MSI-H → generally favour immunotherapy.
- Discuss toxicity, infusion access and a sustainable sequence before treatment.

| Situation | Preferred | Alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MSI-H/dMMR | Pembrolizumab or nivolumab–ipilimumab | Chemotherapy if immunotherapy unsuitable |
| BRAF V600E MSS | Encorafenib–cetuximab plus fluorouracil-based chemotherapy | Chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab |
| RAS/BRAF-wild-type left | Doublet plus anti-EGFR | Doublet plus bevacizumab |
| Right-sided or RAS-mutant | Doublet plus bevacizumab | Chemotherapy alone; triplet plus bevacizumab if fit |
| Frail or older | Capecitabine with or without bevacizumab | Supportive care according to goals |
| Potentially resectable liver-limited | Multidisciplinary review and conversion-directed systemic therapy | Local treatment when feasible |

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 MMR and driver alterations → choose the treatment pathway.
- 2️⃣ 🎯 Sidedness, fitness and resection potential → select intensity and biologic.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Match testing and treatment to sustainable access.

## 4️⃣ Alternatives — Match Intensity to Fitness and Treatment Goals

**📊 PARADIGM: Panitumumab vs Bevacizumab + mFOLFOX6 ✨**

- **Study design:** first-line RAS-wild-type disease; as-treated n=802, including 604 left-sided primaries; panitumumab vs bevacizumab with mFOLFOX6. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.4428">[JAMA 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ Left-sided PFS 13.1 vs 11.9 months (HR 1.00; 95% CI 0.83–1.20). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.4428">[JAMA 2023]</a>
  - 🫀 Left-sided OS 37.9 vs 34.3 months (HR 0.82; 95.798% CI 0.68–0.99); overall OS 36.2 vs 31.3 months (HR 0.84; 95% CI 0.72–0.98). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.4428">[JAMA 2023]</a>
  - 🎯 Left-sided ORR 80.2% vs 68.6%; curative resection 18.3% vs 11.6%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.4428">[JAMA 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Acneiform rash (any grade) 74.8% vs 3.2% → pre-emptive skin care, monitor magnesium. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.4428">[JAMA 2023]</a>

**📊 FIRE-3: Cetuximab vs Bevacizumab + FOLFIRI ✨**

- **Study design:** RAS-wild-type disease; FOLFIRI plus cetuximab vs bevacizumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41416-020-01140-9">[Br J Cancer 2021]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS comparable. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41416-020-01140-9">[Br J Cancer 2021]</a>
  - 🫀 OS 31 vs 26 months (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.62–0.94); sidedness subgroup analyses favour left-sided disease. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41416-020-01140-9">[Br J Cancer 2021]</a>
- ⚠️ Anti-EGFR → rash, hypomagnesaemia, infusion reactions; bevacizumab → hypertension, proteinuria, bleeding, delayed wound healing.

- 💊 Anti-EGFR unsuitable or unavailable → doublet plus bevacizumab; chemotherapy alone if bevacizumab is unsafe.

**📊 TRIBE: FOLFOXIRI–Bevacizumab vs FOLFIRI–Bevacizumab ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=508; untreated disease; FOLFOXIRI plus bevacizumab vs FOLFIRI plus bevacizumab, followed by fluorouracil–bevacizumab maintenance. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1403108">[NEJM 2014]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 12.1 vs 9.7 months (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.62–0.90). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1403108">[NEJM 2014]</a>
  - 🫀 OS 29.8 vs 25.8 months (HR 0.80; 95% CI 0.65–0.98), at final analysis. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26338525/">[Lancet Oncol 2015]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR 65% vs 53%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1403108">[NEJM 2014]</a>
- ⚠️ More grade 3–4 neurotoxicity, stomatitis, diarrhoea and neutropenia with triplet. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1403108">[NEJM 2014]</a>

**📊 TRIBE2: Triplet Reintroduction vs Sequential Doublets ✨**

- **Study design:** n=679; upfront FOLFOXIRI–bevacizumab and reintroduction vs mFOLFOX6–bevacizumab followed by FOLFIRI–bevacizumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(19)30862-9">[Lancet Oncol 2020]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS to second progression 19.2 vs 16.4 months (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.63–0.88). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(19)30862-9">[Lancet Oncol 2020]</a>
- ⚠️ During first-line treatment, grade 3–4 neutropenia 50% vs 21%; diarrhoea 17% vs 5%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(19)30862-9">[Lancet Oncol 2020]</a>

- 🎯 Consider triplet for fit patients with high burden or conversion potential; weigh toxicity.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Left-sided RAS-wild-type disease → anti-EGFR therapy can improve outcomes.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ Triplet intensity requires careful fitness assessment.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Bevacizumab-based doublets may be more accessible.

## 5️⃣ Maintenance + Frailty — Preserve Benefit and Function

**📊 CAIRO3: Maintenance vs Observation ✨**

- **Study design:** n=558; after 6 cycles of CAPOX–bevacizumab, capecitabine–bevacizumab maintenance vs observation. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)62004-3">[Lancet 2015]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS to second progression 11.7 vs 8.5 months (HR 0.67; 95% CI 0.56–0.81); quality of life preserved. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)62004-3">[Lancet 2015]</a>
- ⚠️ Hand-foot skin reaction 23% during maintenance; global quality of life did not deteriorate. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)62004-3">[Lancet 2015]</a>

- 🔁 After response or stable disease → consider fluoropyrimidine–bevacizumab maintenance; stop oxaliplatin to limit neuropathy and reintroduce active treatment when appropriate.

**📊 AVEX: Capecitabine–Bevacizumab vs Capecitabine ✨**

- **Study design:** n=280; patients aged ≥70 years, median age 76, unsuitable for oxaliplatin- or irinotecan-based doublets; capecitabine 1000 mg/m² twice daily on days 1–14, with or without bevacizumab 7.5 mg/kg on day 1, every 3 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(13)70154-2">[Lancet Oncol 2013]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 9.1 vs 5.1 months (HR 0.53; 95% CI 0.41–0.69). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(13)70154-2">[Lancet Oncol 2013]</a>
- ⚠️ Treatment-related grade ≥3 adverse events 40% vs 22%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(13)70154-2">[Lancet Oncol 2013]</a>

- Frailty or comorbidity → capecitabine with or without bevacizumab, or supportive care when treatment burden outweighs benefit.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🔁 De-escalate after induction when disease control permits; reassess at progression.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Oral maintenance requires reliable supply and toxicity monitoring.

## 6️⃣ Progression — Sequence by Prior Exposure and Molecular Findings

- 🔁 Switch oxaliplatin- and irinotecan-based backbones at progression; continuing anti-VEGF is an option when appropriate.
- 🧬 MSI-H/dMMR after immunotherapy-free treatment → offer immunotherapy if eligible. Anti-EGFR rechallenge → selected RAS/BRAF-wild-type disease, ideally with circulating tumour DNA confirming RAS wild-type status.

**📊 SUNLIGHT: Trifluridine–Tipiracil + Bevacizumab vs Trifluridine–Tipiracil ✨**

- **Study design:** n=492; refractory disease; combination vs trifluridine–tipiracil alone. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2214963">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 5.6 vs 2.4 months (HR 0.44; 95% CI 0.36–0.54). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2214963">[NEJM 2023]</a>
  - 🫀 OS 10.8 vs 7.5 months (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.49–0.77). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2214963">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Neutropenia, nausea and anaemia most common; no treatment-related deaths. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2214963">[NEJM 2023]</a>

**📊 FRESCO-2: Fruquintinib vs Placebo ✨**

- **Study design:** n=691; refractory disease; fruquintinib vs placebo. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00772-9">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS 7.4 vs 4.8 months (HR 0.66; 95% CI 0.55–0.80). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00772-9">[Lancet 2023]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 adverse events 63% vs 50%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00772-9">[Lancet 2023]</a>

- Match later treatment to marrow reserve, vascular risk, access and patient priorities.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🔁 Select the next backbone and targeted drug according to prior exposure.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Check access and blood count monitoring before later-line treatment.

## 7️⃣ MSI-H / dMMR — Choose Checkpoint Blockade First

**📊 KEYNOTE-177: Pembrolizumab vs Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** n=307; untreated MSI-H/dMMR metastatic disease; pembrolizumab 200 mg every 3 weeks vs mFOLFOX6 or FOLFIRI with or without bevacizumab or cetuximab; median follow-up 44.5 months; 60% of chemotherapy patients crossed over to anti-PD-(L)1. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(22)00197-8">[Lancet Oncol 2022]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 16.5 vs 8.2 months (HR 0.59; 95% CI 0.45–0.79). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(22)00197-8">[Lancet Oncol 2022]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR 45.1% vs 33.1%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(22)00197-8">[Lancet Oncol 2022]</a>
  - 🫀 Prespecified final OS: median not reached vs 36.7 months (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.53–1.03); superiority not formally established. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(22)00197-8">[Lancet Oncol 2022]</a>
  - 🫀 Exploratory longer follow-up: OS 77.5 vs 36.7 months (HR 0.73; 95% CI 0.53–0.99); formal significance was not established in the prespecified analysis. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39631622/">[Ann Oncol 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events 22% vs 66%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(22)00197-8">[Lancet Oncol 2022]</a>

**📊 CheckMate 8HW: Nivolumab–Ipilimumab vs Chemotherapy ✨**

- **Study design:** first-line MSI-H/dMMR disease; 303 randomised, 255 centrally confirmed; nivolumab–ipilimumab vs chemotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2402141">[NEJM 2024]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS at 24 months 72% (95% CI 64–79) vs 14% (95% CI 6–25). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2402141">[NEJM 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events 23% vs 48%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2402141">[NEJM 2024]</a>

- 🫀 Final first-line OS HR 0.37 (95% CI 0.24–0.57) vs chemotherapy; across lines OS HR 0.61 (95% CI 0.46–0.82) vs nivolumab. First-line comparison with nivolumab did not meet the prespecified significance threshold. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42926106/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- ⏱️ FDA first-line analysis → median PFS not reached vs 5.8 months (HR 0.21; 95% CI 0.14–0.32). <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-nivolumab-ipilimumab-unresectable-or-metastatic-msi-h-or-dmmr-colorectal-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a>
- 💊 Nivolumab 240 mg plus ipilimumab 1 mg/kg every 3 weeks for up to 4 doses, then nivolumab 480 mg every 4 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-nivolumab-ipilimumab-unresectable-or-metastatic-msi-h-or-dmmr-colorectal-cancer">[FDA 2025]</a>

**📊 CheckMate 8HW: Nivolumab–Ipilimumab vs Nivolumab ✨**

- **Study design:** n=707; MSI-H/dMMR disease across treatment lines; combination vs nivolumab; median follow-up 47.0 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)02848-4">[Lancet 2025]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ Extended follow-up PFS median not reached vs 39.3 months (HR 0.60; 95% CI 0.47–0.77). <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42926106/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR 72% vs 58% in centrally confirmed disease. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42926106/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events 22% vs 14%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)02848-4">[Lancet 2025]</a>

- ⚠️ Early apparent progression → reassess clinical status and MSI/MMR classification; pseudoprogression is possible.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 Confirm unexpected MSI/MMR results before committing to treatment.
- 2️⃣ 💊 Combination blockade improves PFS versus nivolumab alone across lines, with more grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42926106/">[Ann Oncol 2026]</a>
- 3️⃣ 💰 Access and monitoring capacity guide checkpoint choice.

## 8️⃣ BRAF V600E — Add Targeted Treatment to Chemotherapy

**📊 BREAKWATER: Encorafenib–Cetuximab + mFOLFOX6 vs Standard Care ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=236 vs 243 in the chemotherapy-combination and standard-care arms; BRAF V600E metastatic disease; encorafenib–cetuximab plus mFOLFOX6 vs standard chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2501912">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 12.8 vs 7.1 months (HR 0.53; 95% CI 0.41–0.68). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2501912">[NEJM 2025]</a>
  - 🫀 Interim OS 30.3 vs 15.1 months (HR 0.49; 95% CI 0.38–0.63). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2501912">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Serious adverse events 46.1% vs 38.9%; safety consistent with each component → watch rash, diarrhoea, neuropathy, cytopenias. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2501912">[NEJM 2025]</a>

- 🎯 Earlier primary response analysis, first 110 per arm: ORR 60.9% vs 40.0%; median response duration 13.9 vs 11.1 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03443-3">[Nat Med 2025]</a>
- 💊 FDA analysis: n=236 vs 243; encorafenib 300 mg daily with cetuximab and fluorouracil-based chemotherapy; FOLFIRI cohort ORR 64% vs 39%. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-grants-traditional-approval-encorafenib-metastatic-colorectal-cancer-braf-v600e-mutation">[FDA 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Monitor rash, diarrhoea, neuropathy, cytopenias and interactions.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 BRAF V600E MSS → targeted combination plus chemotherapy is preferred when accessible.
- 2️⃣ 🎯 MSI-H alongside BRAF mutation → generally prioritise checkpoint blockade.
- 3️⃣ 🇮🇳 Check local approval and access before planning the regimen.

## 9️⃣ Later Molecular Options — Reserve HER2 and KRAS G12C Treatment for Progression

**📊 MOUNTAINEER: Tucatinib + Trastuzumab ✨**

- **Study design:** chemotherapy-refractory HER2-positive RAS-wild-type disease; cohorts A and B n=84; tucatinib plus trastuzumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00150-X">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🎯 Confirmed ORR 39.3%; response duration 15.2 months; PFS 8.1 months; OS 23.9 months. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41526345/">[Nat Commun 2026]</a>
- ⚠️ Most common grade ≥3 adverse event: hypertension 7%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00150-X">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>


**📊 DESTINY-CRC02: Trastuzumab Deruxtecan ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 2; previously treated HER2-positive disease; n=82 assigned trastuzumab deruxtecan 5.4 mg/kg every 3 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(24)00380-2">[Lancet Oncol 2024]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🎯 ORR 37.8% (95% CI 27.3–49.2). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(24)00380-2">[Lancet Oncol 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 drug-related adverse events 41%; adjudicated interstitial lung disease 8%, all grade 1–2. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(24)00380-2">[Lancet Oncol 2024]</a>

- ⚠️ Investigate new respiratory symptoms promptly. First-line tucatinib–trastuzumab plus mFOLFOX6 remains under study in MOUNTAINEER-03. <a class="cite" href="https://www.clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05253651">[ClinicalTrials.gov]</a>

**📊 CodeBreaK 300: Sotorasib–Panitumumab vs Standard Treatment ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=160 across three arms; previously treated KRAS G12C disease; sotorasib 960 mg plus panitumumab vs trifluridine–tipiracil or regorafenib. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2308795">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 5.6 vs 2.2 months (HR 0.49; 95% CI 0.30–0.80). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2308795">[NEJM 2023]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR 30.2% vs 1.9% (later analysis); OS HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.41–1.18), not statistically significant. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40215429/">[J Clin Oncol 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events 35.8% vs 43.1%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2308795">[NEJM 2023]</a>

**📊 KRYSTAL-1: Adagrasib + Cetuximab ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 1/2; n=94; previously treated KRAS G12C disease; single-arm adagrasib–cetuximab. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11152245/">[Cancer Discov 2024]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 6.9 months (95% CI 5.7–7.4); 🫀 OS 15.9 months (95% CI 11.8–18.8). <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11152245/">[Cancer Discov 2024]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR 34.0% (expanded cohort). <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11152245/">[Cancer Discov 2024]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events 27.7% (expanded cohort). <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11152245/">[Cancer Discov 2024]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 HER2 and KRAS G12C results can unlock later targeted therapy.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ Monitor for lung toxicity with trastuzumab deruxtecan.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Confirm access and eligibility before sequencing targeted drugs.

## 🔟 Primary and Metastatic Sites — Coordinate Systemic and Local Treatment

- Asymptomatic primary in situ with unresectable metastases → systemic treatment generally takes priority; routine primary resection is not required solely because the primary remains.
- Obstruction, perforation or significant bleeding → urgent multidisciplinary assessment for stabilisation, endoscopic stent, diversion, surgery, radiotherapy or embolisation as appropriate. Consider bevacizumab-related wound and perforation risks.
- Liver-limited or oligometastatic disease → review resectability and possible ablation before and during systemic treatment. Conversion requires repeated imaging and a coordinated plan; avoid losing the local treatment window.
- Peritoneal disease → assess obstruction, nutrition and symptom burden; selected patients may merit specialist surgical review. Extensive disease or poor fitness can make intervention burdensome.
- Rectal primary → assess pelvic symptoms and anatomy; pelvic radiotherapy or surgery may be useful for local control or palliation, coordinated with systemic treatment and prior radiation.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🎯 Treat complications urgently and revisit resectability during systemic therapy.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Coordinate local treatment with available specialist services.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Special Histology + Populations — Individualise the Pathway

- Confirm unusual histology with specialist pathology; high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma requires a distinct treatment approach. Do not assume adenocarcinoma trial results apply to every variant.
- Older age alone does not determine fitness; assess function, comorbidity, cognition, support and patient goals. Organ dysfunction and prior adjuvant exposure may change backbone choice.
- Autoimmune disease, transplant or previous severe immune toxicity → individual checkpoint-inhibitor assessment; consider alternatives when risk is unacceptable.
- Consider hereditary cancer assessment for dMMR or a suggestive family history, with appropriate counselling and germline testing pathways.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧬 Histology, inherited risk and comorbidity can redirect treatment.
- 2️⃣ 🇮🇳 Arrange genetic counselling and testing when available and clinically indicated.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Palliation, Follow-up + India Practice — Maintain Access and Reassess Goals

- Integrate symptom control, nutrition, psychosocial support and palliative care early. Treat pain, bleeding and bowel dysfunction; assess function and toxicity at every visit.
- Repeat imaging to assess response and conversion potential; investigate new symptoms earlier. Monitor blood counts, renal and liver function, neuropathy, diarrhoea and treatment-specific immune or vascular toxicity.
- 🇮🇳 Check local CDSCO indications and actual access for encorafenib, nivolumab–ipilimumab, tucatinib and fruquintinib; an FDA approval does not establish Indian availability.
