**Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Treat Only Active Disease

- Confirm typical CLL/SLL immunophenotype; investigate atypical morphology/phenotype before applying this pathway.
- Apply iwCLL criteria: progressive marrow failure, usually Hb <10 g/dL or platelets <100 ×10⁹/L attributable to CLL; a stable lower platelet count does not automatically require therapy. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540348/">[Blood 2018]</a>
- Massive/progressive/symptomatic nodes (≥10 cm) or spleen (≥6 cm below left costal margin); symptomatic extranodal disease; steroid-refractory autoimmune cytopenias. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540348/">[Blood 2018]</a>
- Lymphocytes rising ≥50% in 2 months or doubling time <6 months → exclude infection/steroid effects and interpret low starting counts carefully. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540348/">[Blood 2018]</a>
- Disease-related weight loss ≥10% in 6 months, ECOG ≥2 fatigue, fever ≥38°C for ≥2 weeks or night sweats ≥1 month → exclude infection. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540348/">[Blood 2018]</a>
- High lymphocyte count, adverse genetics or low IgG alone → observation. CLL12 did not demonstrate an OS advantage from early ibrutinib in inactive disease. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39602678/">[J Clin Oncol 2025]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Active disease → treat; adverse markers alone → observe.
- 2️⃣ 🇮🇳 Preserve access to follow-up during observation.

## 2️⃣ Pretreatment Biology and Fitness — Choose Finite or Continuous Therapy

- Before every line: del(17p) FISH **and** TP53 sequencing; negative FISH does not exclude TP53 mutation. IGHV usually needs testing once. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540348/">[Blood 2018]</a>
- **Record:**
  - Prior agents, intolerance versus progression, response duration, adherence and treatment-free interval.
  - BP/rhythm, bleeding/anticoagulants, renal function, hepatic function, infection history and CYP3A interactions. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
  - Patient preference, total cost, infusion access and capacity for real-time tumour-lysis syndrome (TLS) monitoring.
- TP53 aberration → avoid chemoimmunotherapy; often favour a continuous second-generation BTKi. Finite venetoclax remains possible with counselling about shorter remission durability. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1002/hem3.70403">[HemaSphere 2026]</a>
- Significant arrhythmia/bleeding risk → favour venetoclax-based therapy when feasible; difficult TLS logistics, substantial renal impairment or hydration intolerance → BTKi monotherapy may be easier.
- CT for venetoclax tumour burden; routine PET is unnecessary in uncomplicated CLL. Unexplained cytopenias require a mechanism-specific work-up.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Check TP53 at every treatment decision. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540348/">[Blood 2018]</a>
- 2️⃣ 💰 Match the regimen to sustainable monitoring and supply.

## 3️⃣ Continuous BTK Inhibition — Prefer Evidence-Based Second-Generation Options

**📊 ELEVATE-TN: Acalabrutinib ± Obinutuzumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=535; older/comorbid untreated patients; acalabrutinib–obinutuzumab, acalabrutinib or chlorambucil–obinutuzumab. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40198878/">[Blood 2025]</a>
- Acalabrutinib 100 mg orally twice daily until progression/toxicity; antibody for 6 cycles, beginning after acalabrutinib lead-in. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40198878/">[Blood 2025]</a>
- Six-year update: 72-month PFS 78.0% / 61.5% / 17.2%; median PFS not reached / not reached / 27.8 months. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40198878/">[Blood 2025]</a>
- 📊 PFS HR versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab: 0.14 (95% CI 0.10–0.20) with combination and 0.24 (95% CI 0.17–0.32) with monotherapy (74.5-month median follow-up publication). <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12824692/">[Blood 2025]</a>
- At median 74.5-month follow-up, acalabrutinib–obinutuzumab improved OS versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab; antibody addition improves disease control but adds infusion, neutropenia and infection burden; the comparison with acalabrutinib alone was exploratory. Grade ≥3 neutropenia was 30.9% / 11.7% / 42.0% across the three arms. <a class="cite" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12824692/">[Blood 2025]</a>


**📊 SEQUOIA: Zanubrutinib**

- **Study design:** randomized phase 3 cohort n=479 without del(17p); zanubrutinib versus 6-cycle bendamustine–rituximab (BR). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-24-02265">[J Clin Oncol 2025]</a>
- Zanubrutinib 160 mg orally twice daily (or licensed 320 mg once daily) continuously. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/213217s015lbl.pdf">[FDA 2025]</a>
- Six-year congress update: 72-month PFS 74% vs 32% (HR 0.28; 95% CI 0.20–0.38); at 60 months the earlier analysis reported 75.8% vs 40.1%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-24-02265">[J Clin Oncol 2025]</a> Benefit spans mutated and unmutated IGHV; del(17p) evidence comes from a separate nonrandomized cohort. <a class="cite" href="https://beonemedaffairs.com/us/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2025/12/ASH2025_phase-3-sequoia-6-year-long-term-data-of-zanubrutinib-vs-br-in-tn-cll-sll.pdf">[Blood 2025]</a>
- 🎯 ORR 98% vs 89%; 72-month OS 84% vs 80%. <a class="cite" href="https://beonemedaffairs.com/us/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2025/12/ASH2025_phase-3-sequoia-6-year-long-term-data-of-zanubrutinib-vs-br-in-tn-cll-sll.pdf">[Blood 2025]</a>
- Monitor BP, rhythm, bleeding, infection, CBC and liver injury with either BTKi.


**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ A finite antibody course does not make BTKi therapy finite.
- 2️⃣ Do not rank drugs using unmatched follow-up percentages.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Continuous therapy requires sustainable long-term access.

## 4️⃣ Finite Venetoclax–Obinutuzumab — Plan the Endpoint and TLS Support

**📊 CLL14: Venetoclax–Obinutuzumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=432; untreated CLL with coexisting conditions; 12 cycles versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39082668/">[Blood 2024]</a>
- Obinutuzumab IV cycle 1: 100 mg day 1, 900 mg day 2, 1,000 mg days 8,15; cycles 2–6: 1,000 mg day 1, every 28 days. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Venetoclax starts cycle 1 day 22 with weekly ramp-up → 400 mg orally daily through cycle 12. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Six-year analysis: PFS 76.2 vs 36.4 months (HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.31–0.52); 6-year PFS 53.1% vs 21.7%. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39082668/">[Blood 2024]</a>
- 📈 Six-year OS 78.7% vs 69.2% (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.48–1.01; P=.052) → no established OS superiority in this analysis. TP53 aberration, unmutated IGHV and bulk predict shorter durability. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39082668/">[Blood 2024]</a>
- 🎯 ORR 84.7% vs 71.3%; grade 3–4 neutropenia 52.8% vs 48.1% and infections 17.5% vs 15.0%. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa1815281">[NEJM 2019]</a>
- 🧬 End-of-treatment peripheral blood uMRD below 10⁻⁴ → approximately 76% with venetoclax–obinutuzumab. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa1815281">[NEJM 2019]</a>
- ⚠️ Infusion reactions and TLS require active prevention.


**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Finite treatment can yield years without medication.
- 2️⃣ Isolated MRD positivity does not justify extending the standard course.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Include obinutuzumab infusions and TLS admissions/laboratories in the total cost.

## 5️⃣ Finite BTKi–Venetoclax — Match the Studied Population and Duration

**📊 AMPLIFY: Acalabrutinib–Venetoclax ± Obinutuzumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=867; untreated patients without del(17p)/TP53 mutation; AV, AVO or FCR/BR. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2409804">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- Acalabrutinib 100 mg orally twice daily cycles 1–14; venetoclax starts cycle 3 with standard ramp-up, through cycle 14; 28-day cycles. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2409804">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- 36-month PFS 76.5% / 83.1% / 66.5%; AV versus chemotherapy HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.49–0.87). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2409804">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- 36-month OS 94.1% / 87.7% / 85.9%; do not infer AVO superiority over AV from numerical PFS. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2409804">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 neutropenia 32.3% / 46.1% / 43.2%; serious or grade ≥3 infection 14% with AV vs 25% with AVO. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2409804">[NEJM 2025]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>


- AV received US approval in February 2026; confirm Indian indication separately. AVO is not an automatic regulatory or clinical substitute. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-acalabrutinib-venetoclax-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-or-small-lymphocytic-lymphoma">[FDA 2026]</a>
- 🎯 AV vs FCR/BR: ORR 93% vs 75%; median PFS not reached vs 47.6 months (HR 0.65; 95% CI 0.49–0.87). <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>

**📊 GLOW: Fixed-Duration Ibrutinib–Venetoclax**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=211; older/comorbid untreated patients without del(17p) or known TP53 mutation at screening; ibrutinib–venetoclax versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00452-7">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- Ibrutinib 420 mg orally daily for 3 lead-in cycles → 12 combination cycles with venetoclax ramp-up to 400 mg daily. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00452-7">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- At 67-month follow-up: 66-month PFS 51.7% vs 18.1% (HR 0.27; 95% CI 0.19–0.40); OS 79.0% vs 60.8% (HR 0.46; 95% CI 0.27–0.78). Earlier 42-month PFS was 74.6% vs 24.8% (HR 0.214). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00452-7">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a> <a class="cite" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41375-026-03150-7">[Leukemia 2026]</a>
- Lead-in can reduce TLS risk; repeat CT/ALC assessment. Cardiac/bleeding risks of ibrutinib persist.


**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ AV and ibrutinib–venetoclax have different schedules and studied populations.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Confirm monitoring and drug access before starting.

## 6️⃣ Duration and MRD — Apply the Protocol Rather Than an Isolated Test

**📊 FLAIR: MRD-Guided Ibrutinib–Venetoclax**

- **Study design:** phase 3; initial IV-versus-FCR comparison n=523; MRD-directed duration, not the GLOW fixed schedule. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2310062">[NEJM 2024]</a>
- Initial 3-year PFS 97.2% vs 76.8% (HR 0.13; 95% CI 0.07–0.24); OS 98.0% vs 93.0%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2310062">[NEJM 2024]</a>
- **2025 update:** n=786 across IV, ibrutinib and FCR; 5-year PFS 93.9% / 79.0% / 58.1%; IV versus FCR HR 0.13 (95% CI 0.08–0.21); longer follow-up complements the initial analysis. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2504341">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- Duration used serial blood/marrow assessments and protocol rules; a single negative blood result is insufficient for stopping.


**📊 CLL17: Finite versus Continuous Treatment**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=909; Ven–Obi, venetoclax–ibrutinib or continuous ibrutinib; prespecified interim analysis, median follow-up 34.2 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2515458">[NEJM 2026]</a>
- Three-year PFS 81.1% / 79.4% / 81.0%; Ven–Obi versus ibrutinib HR 0.87 (98.3% CI 0.54–1.41), venetoclax–ibrutinib versus ibrutinib HR 0.84 (98.0% CI 0.53–1.32); both met prespecified noninferiority criteria. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2515458">[NEJM 2026]</a>
- Three-year OS 91.5% / 96.0% / 95.7%, respectively. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2515458">[NEJM 2026]</a>
- Comparator was ibrutinib, not acalabrutinib/zanubrutinib; early noninferiority does not establish lifelong equivalence.


- **GAIA/CLL13:** phase 3; n=926; fit, TP53-intact patients. Month-15 blood uMRD 86.5% with Ven–Obi, 92.2% with Ven–Obi–ibrutinib, 57.0% with VenR and 52.0% with chemoimmunotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2213093">[NEJM 2023]</a>
- GAIA/CLL13 updated: 5-year PFS 69.8% with Ven–Obi vs 50.7% with chemoimmunotherapy; Ven–Obi–ibrutinib 81.3%, VenR 57.4% (updated: these supersede earlier 3-year estimates). Five-year OS was 93.6% / 90.7% / 94.3% / 94.7% in the same respective order. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41911073/">[Blood 2026]</a>
- **BOVen:** investigational MRD-directed zanubrutinib–obinutuzumab–venetoclax; single-arm phase 2, n=39; median follow-up 25.8 months and treatment duration 10 cycles. Blood and marrow uMRD occurred in 33/37 evaluable patients; grade ≥3 neutropenia 18%. No established superiority over doublets; MRD negativity is not cure. <a class="cite" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352302621003070">[Lancet Haematol 2021]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ MRD-guided stopping requires the studied protocol.
- 2️⃣ 💰 More testing and longer treatment may change total cost.

## 7️⃣ Frontline Pirtobrutinib and BTKi Selection — Respect the Comparator

**📊 BRUIN CLL-313: Pirtobrutinib versus BR**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=282; untreated CLL/SLL without del(17p); continuous pirtobrutinib versus 6-cycle BR. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02380">[J Clin Oncol 2026]</a>
- Pirtobrutinib 200 mg orally daily; median PFS not estimable vs 33.5 months (HR 0.20; 95% CI 0.11–0.37), approximately 28-month PFS follow-up. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02380">[J Clin Oncol 2026]</a>
- 📈 Interim OS HR 0.257 (95% CI 0.070–0.934); early analysis. Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events 40.0% vs 67.4%; longer pirtobrutinib exposure complicates safety comparisons. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02380">[J Clin Oncol 2026]</a>
- BR comparison does not establish superiority over second-generation covalent BTKi or Ven–Obi.


- US untreated indication dated 2 October 2026 covers adults without known del(17p); confirm Indian approval/access independently. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-pirtobrutinib-previously-untreated-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-or-small-lymphocytic">[FDA 2026]</a>

**📊 ALPINE: Use Relapsed Head-to-Head Evidence Appropriately**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=652; relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL; zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib, not a frontline comparison. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024024667">[Blood 2024]</a>
- Final comparative analysis: 36-month PFS 65.4% vs 54.4% (HR 0.68; 95% CI 0.54–0.84); AF/flutter 7.1% vs 17.0%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024024667">[Blood 2024]</a>
- 🧬 del(17p)/TP53-mutated subgroup → PFS HR 0.51 (95% CI 0.33–0.78). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024024667">[Blood 2024]</a>
- ELEVATE-RR in previously treated disease: median PFS 38.4 months in both arms; AF/flutter 9.4% with acalabrutinib vs 16.0% with ibrutinib. No randomized acalabrutinib-versus-zanubrutinib comparison. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34310172/">[J Clin Oncol 2021]</a>


**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Compare drugs within the population and comparator actually studied.
- 2️⃣ 🇮🇳 Confirm local availability before selecting therapy.

## 8️⃣ Venetoclax Initiation — Prevent TLS Before Every Escalation

- Weekly oral ramp: **20 → 50 → 100 → 200 → 400 mg daily**; never replace with a rapid AML schedule. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- **Risk categories:**
  - Low: all nodes <5 cm **and** ALC <25 ×10⁹/L. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
  - Medium: node 5–<10 cm **or** ALC ≥25 ×10⁹/L without high-risk criteria. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
  - High: node ≥10 cm **or** node ≥5 cm with ALC ≥25 ×10⁹/L. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- CrCl <80 mL/min, splenomegaly and comorbidity can increase risk; reclassify after debulking. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Oral hydration 1.5–2 L daily; IV hydration if needed, including 150–200 mL/hour as tolerated for high risk. Start allopurinol 2–3 days before venetoclax; consider rasburicase for elevated uric acid. Correct electrolytes and review chemistry in real time. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- High risk → inpatient first 20/50-mg doses, predose and 4/8/12/24-hour chemistry. Low/medium → predose, 6–8 and 24 hours for first 20/50-mg doses; consider admission for medium risk with CrCl <80. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Later ramp doses → predose chemistry; high risk or other patients at risk of TLS also need 6–8- and 24-hour checks after each escalation. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Strong CYP3A inhibitors contraindicated during initiation/ramp; review azoles/macrolides and other interactions. Interruption >1 week during ramp or >2 weeks afterwards → reassess TLS/restart dose; do not automatically resume 400 mg. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/208573s032lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Reassess TLS risk after debulking or an interruption.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Arrange laboratory turnaround and admission access before prescribing.

## 9️⃣ Relapse — Separate Intolerance from Resistance

- Reapply treatment criteria; repeat TP53/del(17p), investigate discordant progression and review adherence/interactions. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024025525">[Blood 2025]</a>
- Covalent BTKi intolerance → another covalent BTKi may be reasonable; avoid rechallenge after life/organ-threatening toxicity.
- Progression on covalent BTKi → change mechanism to venetoclax-based therapy or noncovalent BTKi; switching only between covalent agents usually fails. Supervised overlap during venetoclax escalation can reduce disease-flare risk.
- After finite venetoclax remission → BTKi if naïve; selected venetoclax retreatment after durable treatment-free response. Dual-exposed does not mean dual-refractory.

**📊 MURANO: Finite Venetoclax–Rituximab at Relapse**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=389; VenR versus BR; venetoclax 24 months from rituximab cycle 1 after ramp-up, six antibody cycles. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024025525">[Blood 2025]</a>
- Final 2025 report: PFS 54.7 vs 17.0 months (HR 0.23; 95% CI 0.18–0.29); 7-year OS 69.6% vs 51.0%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024025525">[Blood 2025]</a>
- 🧬 Seven-year PFS with VenR 23.0%; approximately 5% in the TP53-aberrant subgroup. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024025525">[Blood 2025]</a>
- Retreatment substudy n=25: ORR 72%, PFS 23.3 months; supports selected retreatment after prior response, not retreatment for progression on venetoclax. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024025525">[Blood 2025]</a>
- Original population largely preceded routine BTKi exposure; repeat TLS assessment before retreatment. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024025525">[Blood 2025]</a>

**📊 BRUIN CLL-321: Pirtobrutinib after Covalent BTKi**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=238; pirtobrutinib 200 mg orally daily versus idelalisib–rituximab/BR. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-00166">[J Clin Oncol 2025]</a>
- FDA primary analysis: PFS 11.2 vs 8.7 months (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.38–0.89). <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216059s006lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Updated 2025 publication: PFS 14.0 vs 8.7 months (HR 0.54; 95% CI 0.39–0.75); OS HR 1.09 (95% CI 0.68–1.75), no demonstrated OS advantage. Keep the two analysis dates separate. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-00166">[J Clin Oncol 2025]</a>
- US relapsed indication since December 2025 requires prior covalent BTKi, without a requirement for prior BCL2 inhibition; verify local label. Monitor infections, bleeding, cytopenias, rhythm and liver injury. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216059s006lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- After targeted-class failures → early cellular-therapy/trial referral. Liso-cel US indication requires ≥2 prior lines including BTKi and BCL2 inhibitor; selected allogeneic transplant remains an option. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/145711/download">[FDA 2026]</a>
- 📊 TRANSCEND CLL 004 → regulatory efficacy population n=65; ORR 45%, complete response 20%. ⚠️ CAR-T may cause cytokine release syndrome, neurological toxicity, prolonged cytopenias, infections and hypogammaglobulinaemia. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/media/145711/download">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Pirtobrutinib → adjust for severe renal impairment and relevant CYP3A interactions according to the label. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216059s006lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Relapse needing treatment → choose the next mechanism by exposure and resistance.
- 2️⃣ 🇮🇳 Refer early when cellular therapy or trials may be needed.

## 🔟 Richter and Autoimmune Cytopenias — Confirm the Mechanism

- Rapid discordant nodal growth, LDH rise, severe new B symptoms or extranodal lesions → PET-directed core/excision biopsy of the most suspicious accessible site. PET uptake cannot diagnose transformation.
- Define DLBCL versus Hodgkin transformation; clonal relationship can inform prognosis. Unrelated DLBCL follows a de novo pathway; related transformation warrants specialist trials.

**📊 Venetoclax–DA-R-EPOCH: Selected Richter Evidence**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; n=26; venetoclax plus rituximab, etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide and doxorubicin. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2021011386">[Blood 2022]</a>
- 🎯 ORR 62%, CR 50%; PFS 10.1 months, OS 19.6 months; grade ≥3 neutropenia 65%, febrile neutropenia 38%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2021011386">[Blood 2022]</a>
- Specialist inpatient treatment with growth-factor/infection support; not a universal standard. Do not apply its accelerated venetoclax protocol to routine CLL. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2021011386">[Blood 2022]</a>
- Do not treat biopsy-proven transformation as ordinary CLL progression.
- Anaemia → reticulocytes, DAT, bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin; exclude bleeding, deficiency, kidney disease and marrow failure. DAT positivity alone is not active AIHA.
- Abrupt isolated thrombocytopenia → assess ITP, drugs/infection; marrow evaluation if unclear.
- Warm AIHA/ITP without active CLL → corticosteroids ± rituximab; cold-antibody disease generally responds poorly to steroids → use a different pathway. Refractory autoimmunity or concurrent active CLL → consider CLL-directed therapy.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Biopsy suspected transformation before changing the disease pathway.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Balance intensive treatment with local supportive-care capacity.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Historical Fallbacks and Toxicity — Reserve Less Safe Options

- FCR: exceptional fit, IGHV-mutated, TP53-intact patient when targeted therapy is inaccessible; discuss prolonged immunosuppression and secondary myeloid malignancy risk. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2504341">[NEJM 2025]</a>
- BR or obinutuzumab–chlorambucil: historical TP53-intact access fallbacks, not equivalent to contemporary targeted regimens. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-24-02265">[J Clin Oncol 2025]</a>
- 📊 CLL8 → median PFS 56.8 vs 32.9 months with FCR vs FC (HR 0.59; 95% CI 0.50–0.69). <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26486789/">[Blood 2016]</a>
- 📊 CLL10 → median PFS 41.7 vs 55.2 months with BR vs FCR (HR 1.643; 90.4% CI 1.308–2.064). <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27216274/">[Lancet Oncol 2016]</a>
- 📊 CLL11 → median PFS 26.7 vs 15.2 months with obinutuzumab–chlorambucil vs rituximab–chlorambucil. <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1313984">[NEJM 2014]</a>
- 📊 HDMP–rituximab → selected rescue/bridge; single-arm frontline study n=28, ORR 96%, CR 32%, median PFS 30.3 months; some received alemtuzumab consolidation. ⚠️ Infection and hyperglycaemia risk. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19693094/">[Leukemia 2009]</a>
- Idelalisib–rituximab and duvelisib: restricted reserves, not routine first line; colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis and opportunistic infection, with mortality concerns for duvelisib. PJP prophylaxis and CMV monitoring where appropriate.
- 📊 Study 116 → relapsed CLL: idelalisib–rituximab vs placebo–rituximab, 24-week PFS 93% vs 46% (HR 0.15; 95% CI 0.08–0.28). <a class="cite" href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1315226">[NEJM 2014]</a>
- 📊 DUO → relapsed CLL/SLL: duvelisib vs ofatumumab, median PFS 13.3 vs 9.9 months (HR 0.52). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2018-05-850461">[Blood 2018]</a>
- BTKi → monitor BP/rhythm, bleeding, CBC, infections and liver injury; planned perioperative holds commonly 3–7 days before/after, individualised to haemostasis/procedure. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216059s006lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Controlled mild AF does not always require permanent cessation; coordinate anticoagulation and interaction management. Acalabrutinib capsules → avoid proton-pump inhibitors; tablets permit their use.
- Severe neutropenia/infection → indicated drug holds, supportive care/G-CSF and protocol-specific dose reduction.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Use historical regimens selectively when targeted therapy is inaccessible.
- 2️⃣ 💰 Include toxicity support and follow-up in the treatment plan.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Follow-up and India Practice — Sustain Infection Prevention and Access

- Vaccinate early: influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal and recombinant zoster; avoid live vaccines. HBV screening before anti-CD20 and indicated antiviral prophylaxis. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216059s006lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Immunoglobulin replacement for recurrent/severe infections with antibody deficiency, not low IgG alone; PJP/antiviral prophylaxis by regimen/risk.
- Clinical examination/CBC at planned visits; image for a clinical question. Early BTKi redistribution lymphocytosis is not progression; isolated MRD recurrence does not mandate restarting therapy. Routine rituximab maintenance is not standard.
- 🇮🇳 Prioritise del(17p), focused TP53 sequencing and IGHV if broad profiling is unaffordable. Generic ibrutinib and venetoclax can reduce cost; verify product quality, supply and actual CDSCO indication. <a class="cite" href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216059s006lbl.pdf">[FDA 2026]</a>
- Rituximab biosimilars reduce selected antibody costs; rituximab cannot automatically replace obinutuzumab in a studied first-line regimen.
- Compare years of BTKi tablets with finite therapy plus TLS laboratories/admissions, antibody infusions and travel; check assistance and state/PM-JAY coverage.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Observe inactive CLL even with adverse biomarkers.
- 2️⃣ Select duration and mechanism around biology, safety and infrastructure.
- 3️⃣ 💰 An affordable tablet is useful only with a sustainable monitoring plan.
