## Before cycle 1 — confirm active disease

- Establish CLL with the appropriate blood and immunophenotypic assessment. Investigate atypical findings rather than forcing a different lymphoid disorder into this treatment pathway.
- Apply iwCLL treatment indications. Progressive marrow failure, clinically significant symptomatic burden or refractory disease-related autoimmunity can justify therapy; a molecularly high-risk result by itself does not.
- Explain observation as planned care for inactive disease. Record symptoms, examination findings and blood-count trends, with clear reasons to return sooner.
- Assess TP53 mutation and del(17p) before treatment, alongside IGHV status. These results answer different questions and should not be treated as substitutes for each other.
- Investigate cytopenias: immune destruction, deficiency, bleeding, renal disease and marrow infiltration need different management. A positive antiglobulin test alone does not establish active haemolysis.
- Review cardiac history, blood pressure, bleeding, anticoagulation, renal function, infection history and interacting medicines. Screen and plan infection prevention appropriate to the chosen regimen.
- For venetoclax, assess nodal and circulating burden, kidney function and the ability to deliver hydration, prophylaxis and real-time laboratory review. An oral regimen can still require intensive early monitoring.

## Choosing first line — duration is a clinical choice

| Situation | Preferred approach | Alternatives / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| TP53-aberrant disease requiring therapy | Usually continuous BTK inhibition | Venetoclax-based treatment if BTK inhibition is unsuitable |
| A finite treatment course is a major priority | Venetoclax–obinutuzumab | Other finite combinations within the applicable indication |
| Major arrhythmia or bleeding concern | Consider a venetoclax-based strategy | Balance tumour-lysis and infusion requirements |
| Tumour-lysis infrastructure difficult | Consider continuous BTK inhibition | Cardiovascular and interaction review remains essential |
| Suitable for continuous treatment | Acalabrutinib-based or zanubrutinib therapy | Include sustainable supply and monitoring in the decision |
| Novel agents inaccessible | Selected chemoimmunotherapy discussion | FCR has a narrow role in fit, IGHV-mutated, TP53-intact disease |

As of November 2026, pirtobrutinib has an FDA untreated CLL/SLL indication without known del(17p). <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pirtobrutinib-previously-untreated-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-or-small-lymphocytic">[FDA 2026]</a> This expands the menu; it does not establish superiority over contemporary covalent BTK inhibitors or venetoclax–obinutuzumab, and it does not establish Indian approval.

## The evidence — understand who was enrolled

- **CLL14:** Finite venetoclax–obinutuzumab versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab: median PFS 76.2 versus 36.4 months; HR 0.40. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39082668/">[Blood 2024]</a>
- **ELEVATE-TN:** Acalabrutinib–obinutuzumab versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab: PFS at 72 months 78.0% versus 17.2%; OS HR 0.62. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40198878/">[Blood 2025]</a>
- **GAIA/CLL13:** Fit patients without TP53 aberration: undetectable MRD at month 15 was 86.5% with venetoclax–obinutuzumab and 92.2% with its ibrutinib triplet, versus 52.0% with chemoimmunotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163621/">[N Engl J Med 2023]</a>
- **AMPLIFY:** Acalabrutinib–venetoclax with or without obinutuzumab: PFS at 36 months 76.5% without and 83.1% with antibody, versus 66.5% with chemoimmunotherapy. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39976417/">[N Engl J Med 2025]</a>
- **BRUIN CLL-313:** Pirtobrutinib versus bendamustine–rituximab, excluding del(17p): PFS HR 0.20; PFS at 24 months 93.4% versus 70.7%. <a class="cite" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41363773/">[J Clin Oncol 2026]</a> The comparator limits claims against modern alternatives.

## Pitfalls / pearls

- **Tumour lysis:** follow the CLL-specific venetoclax escalation and risk-management protocol. Reassess burden after debulking; do not replace supervised laboratory review with reassurance that treatment is oral.
- **BTK safety:** monitor rhythm, blood pressure, bruising, bleeding and infection. Reconcile anticoagulants, antiplatelets and interacting anti-infectives before prescribing or restarting treatment.
- **Duration:** a finite antibody course alongside a continuous BTK inhibitor does not make the whole regimen finite. Explain the planned endpoint before treatment begins.
- **Response interpretation:** early redistribution lymphocytosis on BTK inhibition is not automatically progression. MRD negativity is not cure, and isolated MRD recurrence does not by itself require restarting treatment.
- **Transformation:** disproportionate nodal growth, new severe systemic symptoms or an unexpected LDH rise warrants directed assessment and biopsy. Imaging can identify a biopsy site; it cannot establish transformation alone.
- **Triplets:** a numerically attractive result does not automatically justify more drugs. Antibody addition changes infection, cytopenia, infusion and access burdens as well as disease control.

<p class="opinion">Opinion: ask whether the patient prefers a defined treatment endpoint or fewer early monitoring demands, then test that preference against renal and cardiovascular safety. Duration preference is meaningful only when the monitoring plan is feasible.</p>

## After first line — name the reason for stopping

At relapse, first confirm that treatment indications have returned. Distinguish intolerance from biological resistance and relapse after a completed finite course from progression while taking the drug. These are different sequencing problems.

After covalent BTK resistance, consider a different mechanism or an appropriate noncovalent inhibitor rather than simply exchanging covalent agents. After finite venetoclax therapy, a BTK inhibitor or selected retreatment may be reasonable depending on prior response and interval. Plan transitions to avoid uncontrolled disease flare, and refer early for trials when multiple targeted classes have failed. Biopsy suspected transformation before treating it as ordinary CLL progression.
