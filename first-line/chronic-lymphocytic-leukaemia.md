**Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia — treat active disease and plan the next mechanism**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Treat Only Active Disease

- Confirm typical CLL/SLL immunophenotype; investigate atypical morphology/phenotype before applying this pathway.
- Apply iwCLL criteria: progressive marrow failure, usually Hb <10 g/dL or platelets <100 ×10⁹/L attributable to CLL; a stable lower platelet count does not automatically require therapy.
- Massive/progressive/symptomatic nodes (≥10 cm) or spleen (≥6 cm below left costal margin); symptomatic extranodal disease; steroid-refractory autoimmune cytopenias.
- Lymphocytes rising ≥50% in 2 months or doubling time <6 months → exclude infection/steroid effects and interpret low starting counts carefully.
- Disease-related weight loss ≥10% in 6 months, ECOG ≥2 fatigue, fever ≥38°C for ≥2 weeks or night sweats ≥1 month → exclude infection.
- High lymphocyte count, adverse genetics or low IgG alone → observation. CLL12 did not demonstrate an OS advantage from early ibrutinib in inactive disease.

## 2️⃣ Pretreatment Biology and Fitness — Choose Finite or Continuous Therapy

- Before every line: del(17p) FISH **and** TP53 sequencing; negative FISH does not exclude TP53 mutation. IGHV usually needs testing once.
- **Record:**
  - Prior agents, intolerance versus progression, response duration, adherence and treatment-free interval.
  - BP/rhythm, bleeding/anticoagulants, renal function, hepatic function, infection history and CYP3A interactions.
  - Patient preference, total cost, infusion access and capacity for real-time tumour-lysis syndrome (TLS) monitoring.
- TP53 aberration → avoid chemoimmunotherapy; often favour a continuous second-generation BTKi. Finite venetoclax remains possible with counselling about shorter remission durability.
- Significant arrhythmia/bleeding risk → favour venetoclax-based therapy when feasible; difficult TLS logistics or hydration intolerance → BTKi may be easier.
- CT for venetoclax tumour burden; routine PET is unnecessary in uncomplicated CLL. Unexplained cytopenias require a mechanism-specific work-up.

## 3️⃣ Continuous BTK Inhibition — Prefer Evidence-Based Second-Generation Options

**📊 ELEVATE-TN: Acalabrutinib ± Obinutuzumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=535; older/comorbid untreated patients; acalabrutinib–obinutuzumab, acalabrutinib or chlorambucil–obinutuzumab.
- Acalabrutinib 100 mg orally twice daily until progression/toxicity; antibody for 6 cycles, beginning after acalabrutinib lead-in.
- Six-year update: 72-month PFS 78.0% / 61.5% / 17.2%; median PFS not reached / not reached / 27.8 months.
- Antibody addition improves disease control but adds neutropenia, infusion and infection burden; combination-versus-monotherapy comparison is exploratory.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024024476).

**📊 SEQUOIA: Zanubrutinib**

- **Study design:** randomized phase 3 cohort n=479 without del(17p); zanubrutinib versus 6-cycle bendamustine–rituximab (BR).
- Zanubrutinib 160 mg orally twice daily (or licensed 320 mg once daily) continuously.
- Median 5-year follow-up: 60-month PFS 75.8% vs 40.1%; del(17p) evidence came from a separate nonrandomized cohort.
- Monitor BP, rhythm, bleeding, infection, CBC and liver injury with either BTKi.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-24-02265).

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ A finite antibody course does not make BTKi therapy finite.
- 2️⃣ Do not rank drugs using unmatched follow-up percentages.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Continuous therapy requires sustainable long-term access.

## 4️⃣ Finite Venetoclax–Obinutuzumab — Plan the Endpoint and TLS Support

**📊 CLL14: Venetoclax–Obinutuzumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=432; untreated CLL with coexisting conditions; 12 cycles versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab.
- Obinutuzumab IV cycle 1: 100 mg day 1, 900 mg day 2, 1,000 mg days 8,15; cycles 2–6: 1,000 mg day 1, every 28 days.
- Venetoclax starts cycle 1 day 22 with weekly ramp-up → 400 mg orally daily through cycle 12.
- Six-year analysis: PFS 76.2 vs 36.4 months (HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.31–0.52); 6-year PFS 53.1% vs 21.7%.
- 📈 OS comparison P=.052 → no established OS superiority in this analysis. TP53 aberration, unmutated IGHV and bulk predict shorter durability.
- ⚠️ Neutropenia, infection, infusion reactions and TLS require active prevention.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024024631).

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Finite treatment can yield years without medication.
- 2️⃣ Isolated MRD positivity does not justify extending the standard course.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Include obinutuzumab infusions and TLS admissions/laboratories in the total cost.

## 5️⃣ Finite BTKi–Venetoclax — Match the Studied Population and Duration

**📊 AMPLIFY: Acalabrutinib–Venetoclax ± Obinutuzumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=867; untreated patients without del(17p)/TP53 mutation; AV, AVO or FCR/BR.
- Acalabrutinib 100 mg orally twice daily cycles 1–14; venetoclax starts cycle 3 with standard ramp-up, through cycle 14; 28-day cycles.
- 36-month PFS 76.5% / 83.1% / 66.5%; AV versus chemotherapy HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.49–0.87).
- 36-month OS 94.1% / 87.7% / 85.9%; do not infer AVO superiority over AV from numerical PFS.
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 neutropenia 32.3% / 46.1% / 43.2%; antibody addition increases infection burden.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2409804).

- AV received US approval in February 2026; confirm current Indian indication separately. AVO is not an automatic regulatory or clinical substitute.

**📊 GLOW: Fixed-Duration Ibrutinib–Venetoclax**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=211; older/comorbid untreated patients without del(17p)/TP53 mutation; IV versus chlorambucil–obinutuzumab.
- Ibrutinib 420 mg orally daily for 3 lead-in cycles → 12 combination cycles with venetoclax ramp-up to 400 mg daily.
- Four-year report, median follow-up 46 months: **42-month** PFS 74.6% vs 24.8% (HR 0.214; 95% CI 0.138–0.334).
- Lead-in can reduce TLS risk; repeat CT/ALC assessment. Cardiac/bleeding risks of ibrutinib persist.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00452-7).

## 6️⃣ Duration and MRD — Apply the Protocol Rather Than an Isolated Test

**📊 FLAIR: MRD-Guided Ibrutinib–Venetoclax**

- **Study design:** phase 3; initial IV-versus-FCR comparison n=523; MRD-directed duration, not the GLOW fixed schedule.
- Initial 2023 analysis: 3-year PFS 97.2% vs 76.8% (HR 0.13; 95% CI 0.07–0.24); OS 98.0% vs 93.0%.
- **2025 update:** n=786 across IV, ibrutinib and FCR; 5-year PFS 93.9% / 79.0% / 58.1%; IV versus FCR HR 0.13 (95% CI 0.08–0.21). [Updated primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2504341).
- Duration used serial blood/marrow assessments and protocol rules; a single negative blood result is insufficient for stopping.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2310062).

**📊 CLL17: Finite versus Continuous Treatment**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=909; Ven–Obi, venetoclax–ibrutinib or continuous ibrutinib; prespecified interim analysis, median follow-up 34.2 months.
- Three-year PFS 81.1% / 79.4% / 81.0%; both finite regimens met prespecified noninferiority criteria.
- Comparator was ibrutinib, not acalabrutinib/zanubrutinib; early noninferiority does not establish lifelong equivalence.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2515458).

- **GAIA/CLL13:** phase 3; n=926; fit, TP53-intact patients. Month-15 blood uMRD 86.5% with Ven–Obi, 92.2% with Ven–Obi–ibrutinib, 57.0% with VenR and 52.0% with chemoimmunotherapy. [Publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2213093).
- BOVen (zanubrutinib–obinutuzumab–venetoclax) has promising phase 2 MRD-directed evidence; no proof of universal superiority over established doublets. MRD negativity is not cure.

## 7️⃣ Frontline Pirtobrutinib and BTKi Selection — Respect the Comparator

**📊 BRUIN CLL-313: Pirtobrutinib versus BR**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=282; untreated CLL/SLL without del(17p); continuous pirtobrutinib versus 6-cycle BR.
- Pirtobrutinib 200 mg orally daily; median PFS not estimable vs 33.5 months (HR 0.20; 95% CI 0.11–0.37), approximately 28-month PFS follow-up.
- 📈 OS immature; BR superiority comparison does not establish superiority over second-generation covalent BTKi or Ven–Obi.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02380).

- US untreated indication dated 2 October 2026 covers adults without known del(17p); confirm Indian approval/access independently. [Regulatory source](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-pirtobrutinib-previously-untreated-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-or-small-lymphocytic).

**📊 ALPINE: Use Relapsed Head-to-Head Evidence Appropriately**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=652; relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL; zanubrutinib versus ibrutinib, not a frontline comparison.
- Final comparative analysis: 36-month PFS 65.4% vs 54.4% (HR 0.68; 95% CI 0.54–0.84); AF/flutter 7.1% vs 17.0%.
- ELEVATE-RR supports acalabrutinib as another less arrhythmogenic alternative to ibrutinib in previously treated disease; no randomized acalabrutinib-versus-zanubrutinib comparison.

[Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024024667).

## 8️⃣ Venetoclax Initiation — Prevent TLS Before Every Escalation

- Weekly oral ramp: **20 → 50 → 100 → 200 → 400 mg daily**; never replace with a rapid AML schedule.
- **Risk categories:**
  - Low: all nodes <5 cm **and** ALC <25 ×10⁹/L.
  - Medium: node 5–<10 cm **or** ALC ≥25 ×10⁹/L without high-risk criteria.
  - High: node ≥10 cm **or** node ≥5 cm with ALC ≥25 ×10⁹/L.
- CrCl <80 mL/min, splenomegaly and comorbidity can increase risk; reclassify after debulking.
- Oral/IV hydration as appropriate, allopurinol and selected rasburicase; correct electrolytes and review chemistry in real time.
- High risk → inpatient first 20/50-mg doses, predose and 4/8/12/24-hour chemistry. Low/medium → predose, 6–8 and 24 hours for first 20/50-mg doses; consider admission for medium risk with CrCl <80.
- Strong CYP3A inhibitors contraindicated during initiation/ramp; review azoles/macrolides and other interactions. Interruption >1 week during ramp or >2 weeks afterwards → reassess TLS/restart dose; do not automatically resume 400 mg.

## 9️⃣ Relapse — Separate Intolerance from Resistance

- Reapply treatment criteria; repeat TP53/del(17p), investigate discordant progression and review adherence/interactions.
- Covalent BTKi intolerance → another covalent BTKi may be reasonable; avoid rechallenge after life/organ-threatening toxicity.
- Progression on covalent BTKi → change mechanism to venetoclax-based therapy or noncovalent BTKi; switching only between covalent agents usually fails. Supervised overlap during venetoclax escalation can reduce disease-flare risk.
- After finite venetoclax remission → BTKi if naïve; selected venetoclax retreatment after durable treatment-free response. Dual-exposed does not mean dual-refractory.
**📊 MURANO: Finite Venetoclax–Rituximab at Relapse**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=389; VenR versus BR; venetoclax 24 months from rituximab cycle 1 after ramp-up, six antibody cycles.
- Final 2025 report: PFS 54.7 vs 17.0 months (HR 0.23; 95% CI 0.18–0.29); 7-year OS 69.6% vs 51.0%.
- Retreatment substudy n=25: ORR 72%, PFS 23.3 months; supports selected retreatment after prior response, not retreatment for progression on venetoclax.
- Original population largely preceded routine BTKi exposure; repeat TLS assessment before retreatment. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2024025525).
**📊 BRUIN CLL-321: Pirtobrutinib after Covalent BTKi**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=238; pirtobrutinib 200 mg orally daily versus idelalisib–rituximab/BR.
- FDA primary analysis: PFS 11.2 vs 8.7 months (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.38–0.89). [Regulatory analysis](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216059s006lbl.pdf).
- Updated 2025 publication: PFS 14.0 vs 8.7 months (HR 0.54; 95% CI 0.39–0.75); OS HR 1.09 (95% CI 0.68–1.75), no demonstrated OS advantage. Keep the two analysis dates separate. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-00166).
- US relapsed indication since December 2025 no longer requires prior BCL2 inhibition; verify local label. Monitor infections, bleeding, cytopenias, rhythm and liver injury.
- After targeted-class failures → early cellular-therapy/trial referral. Liso-cel US indication includes ≥2 prior lines including BTKi and BCL2 inhibitor; selected allogeneic transplant remains an option.

## 🔟 Richter and Autoimmune Cytopenias — Confirm the Mechanism

- Rapid discordant nodal growth, LDH rise, severe new B symptoms or extranodal lesions → PET-directed core/excision biopsy of the most suspicious accessible site. PET uptake cannot diagnose transformation.
- Define DLBCL versus Hodgkin transformation; clonal relationship can inform prognosis. Unrelated DLBCL follows a de novo pathway; related transformation warrants specialist trials.
**📊 Venetoclax–DA-R-EPOCH: Selected Richter Evidence**

- **Study design:** single-arm phase 2; n=26; venetoclax plus rituximab, etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide and doxorubicin.
- 🎯 ORR 62%, CR 50%; PFS 10.1 months, OS 19.6 months; grade ≥3 neutropenia 65%, febrile neutropenia 38%.
- Specialist inpatient treatment with growth-factor/infection support; not a universal standard. Do not apply its accelerated venetoclax protocol to routine CLL. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood.2021011386).
- Do not treat biopsy-proven transformation as ordinary CLL progression.
- Anaemia → reticulocytes, DAT, bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin; exclude bleeding, deficiency, kidney disease and marrow failure. DAT positivity alone is not active AIHA.
- Abrupt isolated thrombocytopenia → assess ITP, drugs/infection; marrow evaluation if unclear.
- Warm AIHA/ITP without active CLL → corticosteroids ± rituximab; cold-antibody disease needs a different pathway. Refractory autoimmunity or concurrent active CLL → consider CLL-directed therapy.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Historical Fallbacks and Toxicity — Reserve Less Safe Options

- FCR: exceptional fit, IGHV-mutated, TP53-intact patient when targeted therapy is inaccessible; discuss prolonged immunosuppression and secondary myeloid malignancy risk.
- BR or obinutuzumab–chlorambucil: historical TP53-intact access fallbacks, not equivalent to contemporary targeted regimens. HDMP–rituximab: selected rescue/bridge with substantial infection/hyperglycaemia risk.
- Idelalisib–rituximab and duvelisib: restricted reserves, not routine first line; colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis and opportunistic infection, with mortality concerns for duvelisib. PJP prophylaxis and CMV monitoring where appropriate.
- BTKi → monitor BP/rhythm, bleeding, CBC, infections and liver injury; planned perioperative holds commonly 3–7 days before/after, individualised to haemostasis/procedure.
- Controlled mild AF does not always require permanent cessation; coordinate anticoagulation and interaction management. Acalabrutinib capsules require acid-suppression precautions; tablets differ.
- Severe neutropenia/infection → indicated drug holds, supportive care/G-CSF and protocol-specific dose reduction.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Follow-up and India Practice — Sustain Infection Prevention and Access

- Vaccinate early: influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal and recombinant zoster; avoid live vaccines. HBV screening before anti-CD20 and indicated antiviral prophylaxis.
- Immunoglobulin replacement for recurrent/severe infections with antibody deficiency, not low IgG alone; PJP/antiviral prophylaxis by regimen/risk.
- Clinical examination/CBC at planned visits; image for a clinical question. Early BTKi redistribution lymphocytosis is not progression; isolated MRD recurrence does not mandate restarting therapy. Routine rituximab maintenance is not standard.
- 🇮🇳 Prioritise del(17p), focused TP53 sequencing and IGHV if broad profiling is unaffordable. Generic ibrutinib and venetoclax can reduce cost; verify product quality, supply and actual CDSCO indication.
- Rituximab biosimilars reduce selected antibody costs; rituximab cannot automatically replace obinutuzumab in a studied first-line regimen.
- Compare years of BTKi tablets with finite therapy plus TLS laboratories/admissions, antibody infusions and travel; check assistance and state/PM-JAY coverage.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Observe inactive CLL even with adverse biomarkers.
- 2️⃣ Select duration and mechanism around biology, safety and infrastructure.
- 3️⃣ 💰 An affordable tablet is useful only with a sustainable monitoring plan.
