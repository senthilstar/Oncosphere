**Advanced (unresectable or metastatic) hepatocellular carcinoma — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Is This Really Systemic-Therapy Territory?

- **Confirm:** HCC by biopsy, or by contrast CT/MRI criteria in a cirrhotic liver; biopsy when imaging is atypical, the liver is non-cirrhotic, or mixed HCC–cholangiocarcinoma is possible.
- **Stage with BCLC:** advanced (BCLC C: portal invasion, extrahepatic spread, or ECOG 1–2) or intermediate disease no longer suited to locoregional therapy → systemic therapy. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.018">[J Hepatol 2022]</a>
- 🔁 **Before labelling "advanced":** multidisciplinary review for resection, transplant, ablation, TACE/TARE or radiotherapy → treatment stage migration works in both directions.
- **Treat first what kills first:**
  - 🩸 Variceal or tumour bleeding → endoscopic control first.
  - 🟡 Rising bilirubin, ascites, encephalopathy → manage decompensation; it may rule out systemic therapy.
  - 🦠 Active hepatitis B → start antiviral therapy now (entecavir or tenofovir); continue throughout.
- → Goal: prolong life without spending the liver reserve faster than the tumour does.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🎯 Two diseases, one patient: cancer and cirrhosis are treated together.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ Always ask whether locoregional or curative therapy is still possible before cycle 1.

## 2️⃣ Liver Reserve + Eligibility — Four Checks Before Choosing a Regimen

- 🫀 **Child-Pugh class:** every first-line phase 3 trial below enrolled Child-Pugh A; ECOG 0–1. Child-Pugh B → see part 9️⃣. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)00403-9">[Lancet 2025]</a>
- 🩸 **Upper endoscopy:** IMbrave150 required endoscopy within 6 months before enrolment, with varices treated → the same standard applies before bevacizumab. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1915745">[NEJM 2020]</a>
- 🛡️ **Immunotherapy eligibility:** prior liver transplant, active autoimmune disease, or need for immunosuppression → checkpoint inhibitors unsafe; go to TKI.
- 🦠 **Viral status:** HBsAg, anti-HBc, HBV DNA, anti-HCV; aetiology also informs prognosis discussions.
- 🧪 **Baseline:** AFP (prognosis, REACH-2 eligibility later), thyroid, cortisol, glucose, urine protein, blood pressure.
- 🧬 No routine predictive biomarker selects first-line therapy; PD-L1 and tumour mutation burden are not used for selection.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧭 Child-Pugh A + controlled varices + immunotherapy-eligible → all options open.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ Untreated large varices → treat first or choose an anti-VEGF-free regimen.
- 3️⃣ 💊 HBV antivirals are not optional during immunotherapy or TKI therapy.

## 3️⃣ Preferred First Line — Choose the Immunotherapy Combination by Bleeding Risk and Fitness

| Situation | Preferred | Alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Child-Pugh A, varices controlled, no bleeding risk | Atezolizumab + bevacizumab | STRIDE; nivolumab + ipilimumab |
| High bleeding risk, recent bleed, cardiovascular/thrombotic risk, proteinuria | STRIDE (tremelimumab + durvalumab) | Nivolumab + ipilimumab |
| Fit, need for deep response (e.g. downstaging intent) | Nivolumab + ipilimumab or atezolizumab + bevacizumab | Camrelizumab + rivoceranib where approved |
| Unfit for dual immunotherapy and anti-VEGF | Durvalumab or tislelizumab alone | Lenvatinib |
| Immunotherapy contraindicated | Lenvatinib or sorafenib | — |

- → No head-to-head trials among the immunotherapy combinations; all beat sorafenib or a TKI control. Choose on safety, not cross-trial medians.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🩸 Bevacizumab needs a safe endoscopy; STRIDE and nivolumab–ipilimumab do not.
- 2️⃣ ⚖️ Pick the regimen whose toxicity the patient and the liver can best absorb.

## 4️⃣ Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab — The Benchmark When Varices Are Controlled

**📊 IMbrave150: Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab vs Sorafenib ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, open-label, 2:1; n=501 (336 vs 165); Child-Pugh A; atezolizumab 1,200 mg + bevacizumab 15 mg/kg every 3 weeks vs sorafenib 400 mg twice daily. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.030">[J Hepatol 2022]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS, primary analysis: HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.42–0.79); 12-month OS 67.2% vs 54.6%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1915745">[NEJM 2020]</a>
  - 🫀 OS, updated: 19.2 vs 13.4 months (HR 0.66; 95% CI 0.52–0.85). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.030">[J Hepatol 2022]</a>
  - ⏱️ PFS, updated: 6.9 vs 4.3 months (HR 0.65; 95% CI 0.53–0.81). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.030">[J Hepatol 2022]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR (RECIST 1.1): 30% vs 11%; complete response 8%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.030">[J Hepatol 2022]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 AEs 56.5% vs 55.1%; grade 3–4 hypertension 15.2%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1915745">[NEJM 2020]</a> Treatment-related grade 3–4 AEs 43% vs 46%; treatment-related deaths 2% vs <1%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.030">[J Hepatol 2022]</a>
- 🩸 Check blood pressure and urine protein before each dose; hold bevacizumab for bleeding, uncontrolled hypertension or nephrotic-range proteinuria → atezolizumab can continue alone.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ ✅ First regimen to beat sorafenib on both OS and PFS.
- 2️⃣ 🩸 Endoscopy and variceal treatment are part of the regimen.

## 5️⃣ STRIDE — Anti-VEGF-Free, With the Longest Survival Follow-Up

**📊 HIMALAYA: Tremelimumab + Durvalumab (STRIDE) vs Durvalumab vs Sorafenib ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, open-label; n=1,171 (393 STRIDE, 389 durvalumab, 389 sorafenib); tremelimumab 300 mg once + durvalumab 1,500 mg, then durvalumab 1,500 mg every 4 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2100070">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS: 16.43 vs 13.77 months (HR 0.78; 96.02% CI 0.65–0.93); 36-month OS 30.7% vs 20.2%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2100070">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>
  - 🫀 4-year OS 25.2% vs 15.1% (HR 0.78; 95% CI 0.67–0.92). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.02.005">[Ann Oncol 2024]</a>
  - 🫀 5-year OS 19.6% vs 9.4% (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.65–0.89); 28.7% vs 12.7% with disease control. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2025.03.033">[J Hepatol 2025]</a>
  - ⏱️ PFS not improved: 3.8 vs 4.1 months (HR 0.90; 95% CI 0.77–1.05). <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-trials-snapshots/drug-trials-snapshots-imjudo">[FDA 2022]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR 20.1% vs 5.1%; median duration of response 22.3 vs 18.4 months. <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-trials-snapshots/drug-trials-snapshots-imjudo">[FDA 2022]</a>
- 💊 Durvalumab alone: OS 16.56 months, non-inferior to sorafenib (HR 0.86; 95.67% CI 0.73–1.03). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2100070">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 treatment-emergent AEs 50.5% (STRIDE) vs 37.1% (durvalumab) vs 52.4% (sorafenib). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/EVIDoa2100070">[NEJM Evid 2022]</a> Serious treatment-related AEs 17.5% with STRIDE; no late safety signal. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.02.005">[Ann Oncol 2024]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🩸 Choice for varices, bleeding risk or anti-VEGF contraindication.
- 2️⃣ 📈 Benefit is a survival tail, not PFS → do not abandon at the first scan without clinical progression.
- 3️⃣ 💊 Single priming dose of tremelimumab → one CTLA-4 exposure only.

## 6️⃣ Nivolumab + Ipilimumab — Highest Response, Front-Loaded Toxicity

**📊 CheckMate 9DW: Nivolumab + Ipilimumab vs Lenvatinib or Sorafenib ✨**

- **Study design:** phase 3, open-label; n=668 (335 vs 333); Child-Pugh A, ECOG 0–1; nivolumab 1 mg/kg + ipilimumab 3 mg/kg every 3 weeks × up to 4 doses, then nivolumab 480 mg every 4 weeks. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)00403-9">[Lancet 2025]</a>
- **Key outcomes:**
  - 🫀 OS: 23.7 vs 20.6 months (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.65–0.96); 24-month 49% vs 39%; 36-month 38% vs 24%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)00403-9">[Lancet 2025]</a>
  - ⚠️ Early crossing: more deaths in the first 6 months (HR 1.65; 95% CI 1.12–2.43), then sustained benefit (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.48–0.77). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)00403-9">[Lancet 2025]</a>
  - 🎯 ORR 36.1% vs 13.2% (blinded central review). <a class="cite" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-nivolumab-ipilimumab-unresectable-or-metastatic-hepatocellular-carcinoma">[FDA 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Treatment-related grade 3–4 AEs 41% vs 42%; treatment-related deaths 12 vs 3 patients. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)00403-9">[Lancet 2025]</a>
- 🧪 Weekly liver tests during the 4 combination doses; immune hepatitis vs tumour progression vs viral flare → check HBV DNA before steroids.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🎯 Best for fit patients needing shrinkage, with close access to monitoring.
- 2️⃣ ⚠️ Avoid in borderline liver reserve or poor follow-up access → early deaths matter.

## 7️⃣ Other Immunotherapy Options — Region-Specific Combinations and What Failed

**📊 CARES-310: Camrelizumab + Rivoceranib vs Sorafenib ✨**

- n=543 (272 vs 271; 83% Asian); final OS 23.8 vs 15.2 months (HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.52–0.79); PFS 5.6 vs 3.7 months (HR 0.54; 95% CI 0.44–0.67). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(25)00543-1">[Lancet Oncol 2025]</a>
- ⚠️ Grade 3–4 hypertension 38% vs 15%, AST rise 17% vs 5%; treatment-related serious AEs 25% vs 7%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(25)00543-1">[Lancet Oncol 2025]</a>
- 📌 Not FDA-approved as of 25 Nov 2026: third complete response letter (July 2026, manufacturing); resubmission under review, target date 9 Apr 2027. <a class="cite" href="https://elevartx.com/2026/11/04/elevar-fda-pdufa-new-drug-application/">[Elevar 2026]</a>

**📊 ORIENT-32: Sintilimab + Bevacizumab Biosimilar vs Sorafenib (China) ✨**

- n=571 (380 vs 191); OS not reached vs 10.4 months (HR 0.57; 95% CI 0.43–0.75); PFS 4.6 vs 2.8 months (HR 0.56; 95% CI 0.46–0.70). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00252-7">[Lancet Oncol 2021]</a>
- 💰 Proof that a bevacizumab biosimilar can carry the PD-1–VEGF strategy.

**📊 Single-Agent PD-1 ✨**

- 💊 Tislelizumab (RATIONALE-301): n=674; OS 15.9 vs 14.1 months, non-inferior (HR 0.85; 95.003% CI 0.71–1.02); ORR 14.3% vs 5.4%; grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs 22.2% vs 53.4%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2023.4003">[JAMA Oncol 2023]</a>
- 💊 Nivolumab (CheckMate 459): OS 16.4 vs 14.7 months (HR 0.85; 95% CI 0.72–1.02) → superiority missed. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(21)00604-5">[Lancet Oncol 2022]</a>

**❌ Negative First-Line Combinations**

- Lenvatinib + pembrolizumab (LEAP-002): OS 21.2 vs 19.0 months (HR 0.84; 95% CI 0.71–1.00) → prespecified significance not met. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00469-2">[Lancet Oncol 2023]</a>
- Cabozantinib + atezolizumab (COSMIC-312): PFS 6.8 vs 4.2 months, but OS 15.4 vs 15.5 months (HR 0.90). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(22)00326-6">[Lancet Oncol 2022]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ ✅ PD-1 + anti-VEGF works across agents, including biosimilar bevacizumab.
- 2️⃣ ❌ TKI + PD-1 doublets (LEAP-002, COSMIC-312) did not improve OS → not first-line standards.

## 8️⃣ Immunotherapy Not Possible — Lenvatinib or Sorafenib

**📊 REFLECT: Lenvatinib vs Sorafenib ✨**

- n=954; lenvatinib 12 mg daily (≥60 kg) or 8 mg (<60 kg); OS 13.6 vs 12.3 months (HR 0.92; 95% CI 0.79–1.06) → non-inferior. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30207-1">[Lancet 2018]</a>
- ⚠️ Hypertension 42%, diarrhoea 39%, decreased appetite 34% with lenvatinib; hand–foot reaction 52% with sorafenib. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30207-1">[Lancet 2018]</a>

**📊 Sorafenib — The Original Standard ✨**

- SHARP: n=602; OS 10.7 vs 7.9 months vs placebo (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.55–0.87); partial response 2%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0708857">[NEJM 2008]</a>
- Asia-Pacific (largely HBV): n=226; OS 6.5 vs 4.2 months (HR 0.68; 95% CI 0.50–0.93). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(08)70285-7">[Lancet Oncol 2009]</a>

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 💊 Transplant recipients and active autoimmune disease → lenvatinib or sorafenib.
- 2️⃣ 🎯 Lenvatinib dose by weight; start low in borderline liver reserve.

## 9️⃣ Child-Pugh B, ECOG 2 and Special Situations — Treat Carefully or Not at All

- 🟡 **Child-Pugh B7 (selected):** single-agent PD-1 or TKI; avoid dual immunotherapy and bevacizumab outside trials.
  - CheckMate 040 Child-Pugh B cohort: n=49; nivolumab ORR 12%, disease control 55%, median duration of response 9.9 months; discontinuation for toxicity 4%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.04.047">[J Hepatol 2021]</a>
- 🔴 **Child-Pugh C or ECOG 3–4:** best supportive care; refer for transplant only if within criteria. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.018">[J Hepatol 2022]</a>
- 🫀 **Main portal vein tumour thrombus:** higher bleeding risk → endoscopy and variceal prophylaxis before bevacizumab, or an anti-VEGF-free regimen.
- 🦠 **HBV/HCV:** antiviral therapy throughout; HCV treatment can follow once oncological plan is set.
- 🧓 **Older or frail:** single-agent durvalumab or tislelizumab, given their lower grade ≥3 toxicity in trials.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ ⚠️ Systemic therapy does not rescue a failing liver.
- 2️⃣ 🧭 Child-Pugh B8–B9 benefit is unproven → discuss supportive care honestly.

## 🔟 Toxicity Decisions — Liver Tests, Bleeding and Blood Pressure

- 🧪 **Raised transaminases on immunotherapy:** separate immune hepatitis from tumour progression, HBV flare, alcohol and drug injury before steroids; HBV DNA first.
- 💉 **Immune hepatitis:** grade-based holding and steroids per immune-toxicity guidance; ipilimumab-containing regimens need the most vigilance.
- 🩸 **Bleeding:** stop bevacizumab or TKI; endoscopic control; restart only after the source is treated.
- 🫀 **Hypertension and proteinuria:** common with bevacizumab, lenvatinib and rivoceranib → treat early with standard antihypertensives; hold for uncontrolled values.
- 🖐️ **Hand–foot reaction (sorafenib), diarrhoea and appetite loss (lenvatinib):** dose reduction preserves benefit better than stopping.
- 🧠 **Encephalopathy, ascites:** may reflect decompensation from tumour, therapy or infection → reassess Child-Pugh before the next dose.
- 🦋 Thyroid, adrenal and pituitary checks on immunotherapy.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ ⚠️ Not every liver-test rise is immune hepatitis.
- 2️⃣ 💊 Dose-reduce TKIs rather than stop.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Progression — Sequence by Prior Regimen, Liver Function and AFP

- 🔁 **After an immunotherapy combination:** no randomised post-immunotherapy trial; TKIs are used based on sorafenib-era evidence → lenvatinib or sorafenib if not used, then regorafenib, cabozantinib or ramucirumab.
- 🔁 **After lenvatinib/sorafenib (immunotherapy-naive):** an immunotherapy regimen if still eligible.

**📊 Second-Line Trials After Sorafenib ✨**

- 💊 Regorafenib (RESORCE, sorafenib-tolerant): n=573; OS 10.6 vs 7.8 months (HR 0.63; 95% CI 0.50–0.79). <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)32453-9">[Lancet 2017]</a>
- 💊 Cabozantinib (CELESTIAL): OS 10.2 vs 8.0 months (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.63–0.92); PFS 5.2 vs 1.9 months; grade 3–4 AEs 68% vs 36%. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1717002">[NEJM 2018]</a>
- 💊 Ramucirumab, AFP ≥400 ng/mL (REACH-2): n=292; OS 8.5 vs 7.3 months (HR 0.71; 95% CI 0.53–0.95); PFS 2.8 vs 1.6 months. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(18)30937-9">[Lancet Oncol 2019]</a>
- 🧪 Clinical trial wherever available, especially after first-line immunotherapy.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ 🧪 Recheck Child-Pugh and ECOG at progression → many patients no longer qualify.
- 2️⃣ 🎯 AFP ≥400 ng/mL opens ramucirumab.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Response Assessment, India Access and the First-Line Rulebook

- 📷 Multiphase CT/MRI every 8–12 weeks with AFP; with STRIDE and nivolumab–ipilimumab, disease control matters → avoid stopping for small early changes without clinical decline. <a class="cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2025.03.033">[J Hepatol 2025]</a>
- 🤝 Hepatology alongside oncology: varices, ascites, nutrition, antivirals, alcohol cessation.
- 🕊️ Early palliative care for pain, itch, ascites and goals of care.
- 🇮🇳 Check current CDSCO indications and real-world supply for atezolizumab, durvalumab, tremelimumab, nivolumab–ipilimumab and tislelizumab; an FDA approval does not establish Indian availability.

**🔑 Key insights — first-line rulebook**

- 1️⃣ 🫀 Liver first: Child-Pugh A and ECOG 0–1 are the evidence base.
- 2️⃣ 🩸 Endoscopy decides bevacizumab; high bleeding risk → STRIDE or nivolumab–ipilimumab.
- 3️⃣ ✅ Immunotherapy combinations beat TKIs; lenvatinib or sorafenib when immunotherapy is unsafe.
- 4️⃣ 🦠 Antivirals for HBV throughout.
- 5️⃣ 🔁 Plan the TKI sequence at the start; recheck liver reserve at each progression.
