**Recurrent / metastatic cervical cancer — first-line management**

## 1️⃣ Initial Assessment — Separate Curable Relapse from Systemic Disease

- Confirm recurrence histologically when feasible; record squamous, adenocarcinoma, adenosquamous or neuroendocrine histology.
- Pelvic examination plus CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; pelvic MRI for local anatomy. PET-CT when defining a potentially curative local approach; brain imaging for neurological symptoms.
- Isolated central pelvic recurrence → assess salvage surgery or specialist reirradiation before assigning palliative systemic treatment. Limited distant relapse → multidisciplinary assessment for local treatment.
- **Record:**
  - ECOG PS, nutrition, pain, bleeding, discharge, infection and obstruction.
  - Prior pelvic radiation fields/dose, brachytherapy, platinum exposure and dates.
  - Prior pembrolizumab or other checkpoint inhibitor, response and residual toxicity.
- CBC, renal/liver function, electrolytes, albumin; thyroid function before immunotherapy. Hydronephrosis with AKI → drainage when appropriate → reassess platinum fitness.

## 2️⃣ Fitness + Biomarkers — Test CPS and Screen Bevacizumab Risk

- 🧬 Request PD-L1 CPS using a validated assay, commonly 22C3. CPS counts stained tumour and relevant immune cells relative to viable tumour cells; it is different from tumour proportion score.
- CPS ≥1 supports pembrolizumab–chemotherapy selection under applicable labels. All-comer trial benefit does not establish benefit in the small CPS <1 subgroup.
- Assess renal reserve, hearing, neuropathy and ability to tolerate hydration → cisplatin versus carboplatin. Prior radiosensitising cisplatin does not automatically exclude either platinum.
- **Bevacizumab screen:**
  - Active fistula, perforation, serious bleeding, nonhealing wound or recent major surgery → avoid or defer according to the prescribing guidance.
  - Prior pelvic RT, tumour invading bladder/rectum and instrumentation → heightened fistula risk; review imaging and symptoms together.
  - Check BP, urine protein, thrombosis history and vascular comorbidity.
- Transplantation, active autoimmune disease or prior severe immune toxicity → individual checkpoint-inhibitor assessment. Broad profiling, MSI/MMR and HER2 testing may unlock later options when affordable.

## 3️⃣ Preferred First Line — Add Pembrolizumab for CPS ≥1 Disease

**💊 Regimen**

- Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks; trial cap 35 cycles. A locally authorised 400 mg every 6 weeks schedule may simplify maintenance visits.
- Paclitaxel 175 mg/m² IV day 1 + cisplatin 50 mg/m² IV day 1 or carboplatin AUC 5 IV day 1, every 21 days; generally 6 chemotherapy cycles, with extension only after individual review.
- Add bevacizumab 15 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks when safe; continue pembrolizumab ± bevacizumab after cytotoxic induction until progression/toxicity or the pembrolizumab cap.

**📊 KEYNOTE-826: Pembrolizumab + Chemotherapy ± Bevacizumab ✨💉**

- **Study design:** phase 3, double-blind; n=617; persistent, recurrent or metastatic disease unsuitable for curative treatment; no prior systemic chemotherapy for advanced disease. Placebo + the same chemotherapy ± bevacizumab was the comparator; planned bevacizumab use was stratified, and 63% received it.
- **Final outcomes — CPS ≥1 (n=548):**
  - 📈 OS 28.6 vs 16.5 months (HR 0.60; 95% CI 0.49–0.74).
  - ⏱️ PFS 10.5 vs 8.2 months (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.47–0.71).
- 📈 **Final outcomes — all-comers:** OS 26.4 vs 16.8 months (HR 0.63; 95% CI 0.52–0.77); PFS 10.4 vs 8.2 months (HR 0.61).
- **CPS ≥10:** final OS 29.6 vs 17.4 months (HR 0.58).
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs 82.4% vs 75.4%; anticipate marrow toxicity, neuropathy and immune events. [Final analysis](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.00914).

## 4️⃣ Bevacizumab Decision — Retain Pembrolizumab When Bevacizumab Is Unsafe

**📊 KEYNOTE-826: Exploratory Bevacizumab Subgroups**

- These compare pembrolizumab versus placebo within bevacizumab-use groups; bevacizumab itself was not randomly assigned. Differences between groups cannot quantify the causal benefit of adding bevacizumab.
- **CPS ≥1, with bevacizumab:**
  - ⏱️ PFS 15.3 vs 10.3 months (HR 0.56; 95% CI 0.43–0.73).
  - 📈 OS 43.9 vs 23.0 months (HR 0.60; 95% CI 0.45–0.79); 24-month OS 62.3% vs 48.0%.
- ⏱️ **CPS ≥1, without bevacizumab:** PFS 7.0 vs 6.0 months (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.44–0.85); OS 17.5 vs 11.9 months (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.44–0.85).
- ⏱️ **All-comers, with bevacizumab:** PFS 15.2 vs 10.2 months (HR 0.57; 95% CI 0.45–0.73); OS 37.6 vs 22.5 months (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.47–0.80).
- ⏱️ **All-comers, without bevacizumab:** PFS 6.3 vs 6.2 months (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.50–0.94); OS 17.1 vs 12.6 months (HR 0.67; 95% CI 0.49–0.91).
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs with bevacizumab: 74.0% vs 66.8%. [Subgroup publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.10.002).
- CPS ≥1 and bevacizumab contraindicated → pembrolizumab + platinum–paclitaxel remains an evidence-based route.

## 5️⃣ PD-L1-Unselected Treatment — Consider the BEATcc Quadruplet

**📊 BEATcc: Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab + Platinum–Paclitaxel**

- **Study design:** phase 3, open-label; n=410; first-line metastatic, persistent or recurrent disease; no PD-L1 selection. Atezolizumab 1,200 mg IV every 3 weeks added to bevacizumab 15 mg/kg, paclitaxel 175 mg/m² and cisplatin 50 mg/m² or carboplatin AUC 5, all IV every 21 days, versus the same backbone alone.
- ⏱️ PFS 13.7 vs 10.4 months (HR 0.62; 95% CI 0.49–0.78).
- 📈 OS 32.1 vs 22.8 months (HR 0.68; 95% CI 0.52–0.88); ORR 84% vs 72%.
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs 79% vs 75%. Continue/adapt treatment using the studied protocol and locally applicable prescribing guidance. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)02405-4).
- Benefit in the PD-L1-unselected population supports a quadruplet strategy across PD-L1 categories; subgroup estimates are less precise than the overall result. Confirm local approval and access.
- Bevacizumab was mandatory → do not infer equivalent evidence for atezolizumab + doublet without bevacizumab. No direct trial establishes superiority over the pembrolizumab quadruplet.

## 6️⃣ When Immunotherapy Is Unavailable — Use a Deliverable Platinum Backbone

**📊 GOG-240: Chemotherapy ± Bevacizumab**

- **Study design:** phase 3, n=452, factorial comparison of cisplatin–paclitaxel or topotecan–paclitaxel, with or without bevacizumab 15 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks.
- 📈 **Interim analysis:** OS 17.0 vs 13.3 months (HR 0.71; **98% CI** 0.54–0.95); PFS 8.2 vs 5.9 months (HR 0.67); ORR 48% vs 36%.
- 📈 **Final analysis:** OS 16.8 vs 13.3 months (HR 0.77; 95% CI 0.62–0.95).
- ⚠️ Fistula of any grade 15% vs 1%; all affected patients had previous pelvic irradiation. Grade 3 fistula 6% vs <1%. [Final publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)31607-0).
- Topotecan–paclitaxel did not outperform cisplatin–paclitaxel; it is a niche alternative rather than an automatic escalation.
- 💰 Cisplatin–paclitaxel ± a quality-assured bevacizumab biosimilar is a practical India pathway. Carboplatin can reduce hydration burden when renal reserve or logistics limit cisplatin.
- If bevacizumab and immunotherapy are unsuitable → platinum–paclitaxel alone; severe fixed frailty → symptom-directed care or carefully selected single-agent treatment.

## 7️⃣ Maintenance + Toxicity — Stop the Culprit and Preserve Benefit

- Review CBC, creatinine/electrolytes and neuropathy before chemotherapy; use protocol-directed delays/reductions for cytopenias or neuropathy. Paclitaxel requires hypersensitivity premedication.
- Progressive neuropathy → reduce/stop paclitaxel; avoid extending chemotherapy merely because maintenance access is uncertain.
- Bevacizumab → monitor BP/proteinuria, bleeding, abdominal pain and vaginal urinary/faecal leakage. Suspected fistula/perforation → stop treatment and investigate urgently; confirmed fistula generally requires permanent discontinuation.
- Checkpoint blockade → assess thyroid function and immune symptoms. New diarrhoea, hepatitis, dyspnoea or adrenal symptoms → grade, interrupt and treat according to immune-toxicity guidance; severe toxicity may require permanent discontinuation.
- Do not apply the pembrolizumab 35-cycle cap automatically to atezolizumab or bevacizumab. Discuss duration and monitoring costs before induction.

## 8️⃣ Progression After Platinum — Use Cemiplimab Mainly When ICI-Naïve

**📊 EMPOWER-Cervical 1: Cemiplimab versus Chemotherapy 🔁**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=608; progression after platinum; cemiplimab 350 mg IV every 3 weeks versus investigator-selected single-agent chemotherapy, regardless of PD-L1 status.
- 📈 **Primary analysis:** OS 12.0 vs 8.5 months (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.56–0.84); PFS 2.8 vs 2.9 months (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.63–0.89); ORR 16.4% vs 6.3%.
- **Final OS analysis:** 11.7 vs 8.5 months (HR 0.67; 95% CI 0.56–0.80).
- ⚠️ Primary-analysis grade ≥3 AEs 45.0% vs 53.4%. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2112187); [final OS](https://www.ejcancer.com/article/S0959-8049(24)01753-2/abstract).
- Similar median PFS does not negate the OS result; assess the full survival distribution.
- Evidence largely concerns ICI-naïve disease. Progression on first-line pembrolizumab/atezolizumab → do not assume benefit from switching to another PD-1 antibody; favour an ADC, chemotherapy or trial. Confirm regional availability/indication.

## 9️⃣ Progression After Modern First Line — Consider Tisotumab Vedotin

**📊 innovaTV 301: Tisotumab Vedotin versus Chemotherapy**

- **Study design:** phase 3; n=502; second/third-line recurrent or metastatic disease, including patients with prior anti-PD-(L)1 exposure. Tisotumab vedotin 2.0 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks versus investigator-choice chemotherapy; continue until progression/toxicity.
- 📈 OS 11.5 vs 9.5 months (HR 0.70; 95% CI 0.54–0.89).
- ⏱️ PFS 4.2 vs 2.9 months (HR 0.67; 95% CI 0.54–0.82); ORR 17.8% vs 5.2%.
- ⚠️ Grade ≥3 AEs 52.0% vs 62.3%. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2313811).
- Eye-care protocol is essential: ophthalmic assessment, prescribed steroid/vasoconstrictor drops, cold packs during infusion and lubricating drops; avoid contact lenses and follow local product guidance.
- New eye pain, redness or visual change → interrupt and urgent ophthalmology review. Monitor bleeding and peripheral neuropathy; hold/reduce/discontinue according to severity.
- If inaccessible → selected single-agent chemotherapy, biomarker-matched treatment or clinical trial; benefit is often modest, so reassess goals early.

## 🔟 Prior Chemoradiation or ICI — Reconstruct Exposure Before Retreatment

- Prior radiosensitising cisplatin → consider renal recovery, hearing, neuropathy and treatment-free interval when choosing metastatic platinum.
- KEYNOTE-A18 concerns pembrolizumab with definitive chemoradiation for high-risk locally advanced disease; it is context for prior exposure, not metastatic first-line evidence.
- Relapse during/soon after checkpoint blockade → favour a different active class or trial. A long interval after completed therapy may support individual discussion, but prospective rechallenge evidence is limited.
- Pelvic recurrence after RT → review cumulative radiation dose, tissue necrosis, bowel/bladder involvement and procedures before bevacizumab or reirradiation.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Special Histology + Populations — Change the Treatment Pathway

- High-grade neuroendocrine cervical carcinoma → specialist review; platinum–etoposide is a conventional systemic backbone. Do not assume the common-histology first-line trial results transfer directly.
- Rare adenocarcinoma subtypes → expert pathology and molecular profiling where actionable; distinguish a cervical primary from endometrial or gastrointestinal disease.
- HIV → coordinate antiretroviral treatment, infection prevention and interaction checks; controlled infection alone need not preclude active cancer therapy.
- Pregnancy, organ transplantation, severe autoimmune disease or organ failure → multidisciplinary individualisation. Trial populations do not fully represent these groups.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Palliation, Follow-up + India Access — Fund a Sustainable Sequence

- Bleeding → urgent stabilisation, transfusion when indicated, haemostatic radiation or embolisation; account for previous RT. Obstruction → ureteric stent/nephrostomy when benefit aligns with goals.
- Treat pain, malodorous discharge, infection, fistula-related skin injury and nutritional decline; integrate palliative care and sexual/psychosocial support early.
- Review symptoms/PS each visit; response imaging commonly around 8–12 weeks, sooner with deterioration. At progression, document prior drugs, residual neuropathy and eye/renal reserve.
- 🇮🇳 Check the current CDSCO indication, actual procurement and insurance coverage for each combination; US/EU approvals do not establish Indian approval. Budget for CPS testing, infusions, eye care, travel and maintenance.

**🔑 Key insights**

- 1️⃣ Quadruplet treatment improves outcomes in eligible patients: BEATcc supplies PD-L1-unselected evidence, while pembrolizumab selection should respect CPS and the local label.
- 2️⃣ CPS ≥1 with bevacizumab unsuitable → pembrolizumab + platinum–paclitaxel retains trial support.
- 3️⃣ 💰 Bevacizumab biosimilar + doublet offers an affordable relative alternative when ICI cannot be funded; it still requires careful fistula/bleeding assessment.
- 4️⃣ 🇮🇳 Generic cisplatin–paclitaxel ± bevacizumab biosimilar is a practical fallback; choose a course the patient can complete and monitor safely.
- 5️⃣ After ICI progression, consider tisotumab vedotin if accessible; do not routinely substitute another PD-1 antibody.
